Cooper's Hawk

Astur cooperii
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it does here

At a glance

  • A medium-sized hawk built for speed in tight spaces — long tail, short rounded wings, and the agility to chase songbirds through dense trees.
  • If the birds at a feeder suddenly vanish, a Cooper's hawk is often the reason.
  • Adults have a blue-gray back and rusty-barred chest; immatures are brown and streaky — a different look entirely.
  • It has adapted well to suburban life, and backyard bird feeders are effectively hawk feeders from its perspective.

How to know it

Shape

Long tail with a rounded tip, short rounded wings — built for agility.

Adult

Blue-gray above, rusty-barred chest, dark cap.

Immature

Brown above, white below with brown streaks.

Flight

Fast, agile, with quick wingbeats and glides through trees.

Voice

A sharp, rapid 'kak-kak-kak' — most vocal near the nest in spring.

Size

About fifteen to twenty inches long, with a wingspan around two and a half feet. Females are noticeably larger than males.

What to look for

A sleek hawk with a long tail gliding through trees or perched quietly near a bird feeder. Adults are blue-gray and rusty; immatures are brown and streaky.

Easily confused with

Red-shouldered Hawk — Red-shouldered Hawks are broader-winged, soar in the open and call loudly overhead. A Cooper's Hawk has short rounded wings and a long tail, and usually hunts from cover rather than soaring.

Where to find it here

Scan canopy edges and quiet perches near where small birds gather. The sudden silence of a noisy garden is often the first clue, before you see the hawk at all.

Year-round, with more birds present in winter.

When

Northern migrants supplement residents in winter.

How it lives

An ambush hunter that uses cover to get close, then comes fast. It flies through dense trees at speed, which most hawks its size will not attempt.

Mostly other birds — doves, robins, jays, starlings, smaller songbirds — plus squirrels and chipmunks. If one is perched quietly near your feeder, that is why.

What it does here

A predator of birds and small mammals in wooded and suburban areas. Its hunting shapes the behavior of smaller birds, which fall silent and freeze when one is nearby.

Trees are what make this hawk possible. Short wings and a long tail are built for flying fast through branches rather than over open ground, so wooded edges and dense canopy are hunting habitat, not obstacles. Cooper's Hawks also build their stick nests high in trees, usually against the trunk.

Native to North America. Present year-round in Florida, with additional birds arriving in winter from the north.

Flying at speed through branches is dangerous work. Studies of Cooper's Hawk skeletons have found a high rate of healed fractures in the chest, evidence of collisions survived during pursuit.
