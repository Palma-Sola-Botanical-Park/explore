Great Blue Heron

Ardea herodias
On this page

At a glance

  • Great Blue Herons stand close to four feet tall and can span more than six feet from wingtip to wingtip, making them the largest herons in North America.
  • A hunting bird may stand motionless for minutes before driving its head and neck forward in a sudden strike.
  • Fish dominate the diet, but Great Blue Herons also hunt frogs, snakes, insects, turtles and small mammals, including prey caught on lawns and open ground.
  • In extreme southern Florida, Great Blue Herons also occur in an all-white form known as the Great White Heron.

How to know it

Size

Very large, standing around four feet tall with long legs and a heavy bill.

Color

Blue-gray body, pale head and neck, and a broad black stripe extending behind the eye.

In flight

Flies with the neck folded back and the long legs trailing behind.

White morph

The Great White Heron of southern Florida is entirely white but retains the Great Blue's massive size, heavy bill and pale legs.

Size

About four feet tall, with a wingspan around six feet.

What to look for

Look for a very large blue-gray heron standing or walking slowly along the water's edge. In flight, the folded neck separates a heron from Wood Storks and cranes, which fly with their necks extended.

Easily confused with

Great Egret — Great Egrets are entirely white, slightly smaller and slimmer, with black legs. Ordinary Great Blue Herons are blue-gray and substantially heavier-looking. In southern Florida a white-form Great Blue can resemble a Great Egret but is bulkier, with a much heavier bill and paler legs.

Where to find it here

Look along pond banks and shallow water, but also scan lawns and open grassy areas. Great Blue Herons hunt both aquatic and terrestrial prey.

A year-round Florida resident and an occasional visitor at Palma Sola.

How it lives

It hunts by either standing still or stalking slowly through shallow water and open ground. When prey comes within reach, the long neck extends in a rapid strike.

Fish are the main food, but the diet is unusually broad for a heron and includes amphibians, reptiles, insects and small mammals.

What it does here

A large predator of fish and other wetland animals that also carries its hunting onto dry ground for reptiles and small mammals.

Great Blue Herons nest colonially, usually high in trees but sometimes in mangroves, shrubs or other structures. Their large stick nests may be reused and enlarged. The nest cup can be lined with pine needles, reeds, grass, moss, mangrove leaves or smaller twigs.

Native across much of North and Central America. In Florida it occurs year-round around fresh, brackish and coastal water and also feeds in nearby fields and lawns.
