Cloudless Sulphur

Phoebis sennae
On this page

At a glance

  • A large, clear lemon-yellow butterfly with a fast, restless flight across sunny, open ground.
  • Males gather on damp ground to sip water and the minerals in it, salts that nectar does not give them.
  • Its caterpillars eat Senna plants. The butterfly's Latin name, sennae, comes from them.
  • A long tongue lets it drink from deep, tubular flowers, including red ones.

How to know it

Lemon yellow

Large and clear yellow. Males are plain yellow; females range from yellow to whitish, with a dark border on the wing and a small dark spot. The wingspan is about two to nearly three inches.

Restless

The flight is fast and restless, and it rarely settles for long. Look for a flickering patch of yellow crossing a lawn or a flower bed.

Where to find it here

Sunny open ground

Watch sunny, open areas and nectar flowers, especially around Senna and its relatives. The park's Candelabra Bush is a Senna, the kind of plant these caterpillars eat.

All year

It flies all year in Florida, and more are on the wing in autumn.

How it lives

Puddling

Males often gather at damp ground, sipping water and dissolved minerals, a source of salts nectar does not supply.

Deep flowers

Its long tongue reaches into deep, tubular flowers, including red ones. In autumn some adults join a southward movement down the Florida peninsula.

What it does here

Named for its food

The caterpillars eat Senna and Cassia, pea-family plants such as partridge pea. That is where the species name, sennae, comes from.

Colored by its meal

A caterpillar eating mostly leaves is green. One feeding among yellow Senna flowers can turn yellow with dark bands and nearly vanish into the blossoms.
