Common Raccoon

Procyon lotor
On this page

At a glance

  • A raccoon's forepaws are unusually sensitive and dexterous. They use touch to search through mud, shallow water, leaf litter and cavities, often finding food where vision is little help.
  • Raccoons often manipulate food and other objects in water, which gave them the scientific name lotor, Latin for 'washer.' They are not simply cleaning their food: their highly sensitive forepaws are central to how they investigate objects and search for food.
  • Raccoons are capable problem-solvers and can learn how to open or manipulate unfamiliar objects. Experiments have also found that they can remember solutions to some tasks long after learning them.
  • Florida raccoons remain active year-round. Wetlands are especially good habitat, and raccoons forage along pond edges, marshes and coastal shorelines as well as in forests and developed areas.

How to know it

Size and build

A stocky mammal usually around the size of a small dog, with short front legs, longer hind legs and a bushy tail.

Facial mask

A black mask crosses the eyes, bordered by pale fur. Small rounded ears have pale edges.

Coat and tail

The coat is grizzled gray-brown, and the bushy tail has alternating dark and pale rings.

Paws

Each foot has five long toes. Front tracks look like small hands, while the longer hind footprints can resemble tiny human feet.

Voice

Raccoons make churring, chittering, growling and hissing sounds, but most animals encountered at a distance are quiet. Rustling vegetation or movement near water may reveal one first.

Size

Usually about two to three feet from nose to tail and roughly eight to twenty pounds in Florida. Males tend to be larger.

What to look for

Look for the black facial mask and ringed tail. Even when the animal itself is hidden, five-toed tracks in soft mud near water can reveal where a raccoon has passed. Front tracks resemble small hands; hind tracks are longer.

Where to find it here

Look near ponds and other water, especially where vegetation provides cover and soft ground preserves tracks. Raccoons also use wooded edges and large trees with cavities. They are mostly active after dark, so tracks, scat or disturbed ground are often easier to find than the animal itself.

Raccoons live here year-round and are most active from dusk through the night. Daytime activity can also occur and does not by itself mean an animal is sick.

When

Active year-round in southwest Florida. Females may be accompanied by young during part of the year, but raccoons can be encountered in any season.

How it lives

Raccoons are mainly nocturnal but may also be active around dawn, dusk or during the day. Adults usually forage alone; mothers travel with their young for months after the kits leave the den.

Raccoons eat whatever local conditions provide. Around water they take crabs, shellfish, frogs and other small animals. On land they eat insects, eggs, carrion, fruits and seeds. Around people they readily exploit pet food and unsecured garbage.

They climb well and swim readily. Tree cavities, hollow logs, root tangles and sheltered spaces in buildings can all serve as dens.

What it does here

Raccoons are both predators and scavengers. They eat insects, crabs, frogs, eggs and other animal foods, and they also consume fruits and disperse some seeds in their droppings. Larger predators may take young raccoons.

Raccoons eat the fruits of many Florida plants, including sea grape, beautyberry, cabbage palm, saw palmetto and persimmon. Seeds that survive digestion can be carried away from the parent plant in raccoon droppings. Large trees also provide cavities and sheltered places for daytime rest and dens.

Native across much of North and Central America. Raccoons occur throughout Florida, from the Panhandle to the Keys, in wetlands, forests, coastal habitats and developed areas.

Take care

The risk

Keep your distance and never handle a raccoon. Raccoons can carry rabies, although being active during the day does not by itself indicate disease. Avoid any animal that is staggering, unusually aggressive, disoriented or approaching people without normal caution, and report it to park staff. Raccoon feces can also contain roundworm eggs that are hazardous if accidentally swallowed; do not touch raccoon scat or areas used repeatedly as latrines.

What to do

Watch from a distance and never feed or approach a raccoon. Feeding teaches raccoons to associate people with food and increases the chance of conflicts. Keep food and trash secured. If a raccoon approaches closely or appears disoriented, move away and tell park staff.
