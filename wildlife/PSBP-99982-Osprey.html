Osprey

Pandion haliaetus

At a glance

  • A large fish-eating hawk, white below and on the head, with a bold dark stripe through the eye.
  • It hunts by diving feet-first, sometimes going completely under the surface.
  • In flight the long, narrow wings bend sharply partway along, like a gull's.
  • The pesticide DDT thinned osprey eggshells and numbers fell. After it was banned in 1972 they climbed back, helped by other changes too.

How to know it

Head and body

White below and on the head, with a bold dark stripe through the eye and dark brown above. A Bald Eagle is bulkier, with broader wings.

In flight

The wings are long and narrow and bend sharply at the wrist, like a gull's. Listen for high, sharp whistles, most often around a nest.

Where to find it here

Look up

Scan the sky above the ponds and waterways, then check tall, open trees, snags and poles for a perched bird. Ospreys are in Florida all year, and migrants pass through. Sightings here are occasional, so most visits will not turn one up.

How it lives

Fishing

An Osprey eats live fish and almost nothing else. It patrols over water, sometimes hovers briefly, then drops with its feet out in front. It can hit the water hard enough to go completely under.

Built for the job

The outer toe can swing backward, so a fish is held with two toes in front and two behind. Rough pads under the toes grip the slippery body, and the fish is usually turned head-first for the flight home.

Nests

Ospreys nest on an open support high above fish-bearing water. A tree, a snag, a pole or a platform will do, and the pile of sticks grows year after year.

A comeback

Osprey numbers fell in the DDT years and climbed back after the pesticide was banned in 1972. That ban was a major part of the recovery, and other changes helped too.

What it does here

The Kapok perch

One Osprey here used a high branch of the Kapok beside Cypress Pond. It carried tilapia over from Big Pond, ate on the branch, and dropped the bones onto the mulch below, often enough that a walker underneath might have one land close by.

History, not a promise

A high, open branch with a view of the water is what an Osprey looks for, and Big Pond supplied the fish. That branch is gone now, so this is a story to know rather than a spot to wait.
