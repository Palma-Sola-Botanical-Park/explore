Osprey

Pandion haliaetus
On this page

At a glance

  • The osprey is a large fish-hunting hawk found near water almost worldwide, easy to know by its white underside and dark eye-stripe.
  • It lives on live fish almost exclusively — and dives feet-first into the water to grab them, sometimes going completely under.
  • Its feet are specially built for the job: a reversible outer toe and rough, spiny pads let it lock onto slippery fish like a pair of fish-grippers.
  • Like the bald eagle, the osprey was hit hard by the pesticide DDT and has rebounded strongly since the chemical was banned.

How to know it

Easily confused with

Bald Eagle — The bald eagle is far larger with a dark body and, when adult, a white head and tail; the osprey is smaller, white below with a dark eye-stripe, and crooks its wings into an M in flight.

Underside

Mostly white, with a white head crossed by a bold dark stripe through the eye.

Upperparts

Dark brown.

In flight

Long wings held with a distinctive bend, or 'crook,' like a shallow M.

Behavior cue

Hovers over water, then plunges feet-first.

Voice

A series of clear, high, whistled chirps, often given around the nest.

Size

21 to 23 inches; 5 to 6 feet

What to look for

A large white-bellied hawk over water, hovering and then diving feet-first, or carrying a fish head-forward. The dark eye-stripe and crooked wings separate it from the bald eagle, which is bigger, darker-bodied, and white-headed.

Where to find it here

Overhead or perched on tall snags and poles near water, sometimes hovering before a dive. More regular than the bald eagle but still a treat to catch.

Florida's own ospreys are largely year-round residents, joined each winter by migrants from farther north. Often seen flying over or perched near water.

When

Local residents joined by northern migrants in winter.

How it lives

Active by day. Hunts on the wing over open water, hovering and plunging. Builds large stick nests on tall, open supports, often reusing them year after year.

Almost entirely live fish, caught by diving feet-first. It very rarely takes anything else.

What it does here

A specialized fish predator near the top of the aquatic food chain, and — because it sits at the top and is sensitive to pollution — a useful indicator of healthy water and fish stocks.

Nests in tall trees and on snags near water (and readily on poles and platforms).

Found on every continent except Antarctica, near both fresh and salt water. In Florida it is a common, year-round resident, with extra birds passing through and wintering.

The osprey is a fishing specialist unlike any other North American raptor. It hunts by hovering over the water, then folding its wings and plunging feet-first to snatch a fish from at or just below the surface.

Everything about its feet is tuned for gripping slippery prey: the outer toe swivels backward so it can hold a fish with two toes front and two back, and the soles are covered in short spines. An osprey will even rotate a caught fish to carry it head-first, cutting wind resistance in flight.

Ospreys readily build their bulky stick nests on poles, channel markers, and platforms, which has helped them thrive around people — a recovery, like the eagle's, made possible by the banning of DDT.

Take care

What to do

Enjoy from a distance and do not disturb nests. Active nests are protected, and approaching one can cause the birds to abandon eggs or young.
