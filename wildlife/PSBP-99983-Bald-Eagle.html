Bald Eagle

Haliaeetus leucocephalus

At a glance

  • Bald Eagles nearly disappeared from much of the lower 48 during the twentieth century. After DDT was banned and legal protections increased, the population recovered enough for the species to leave the federal endangered-species list in 2007.
  • Florida has the densest Bald Eagle population of any state except Alaska. Nearly 1,500 nests were recorded in 55 Florida counties during the 2023–24 breeding season.
  • A Bald Eagle nest near St. Petersburg holds the record for the largest bird nest measured: about nine and a half feet wide, twenty feet deep and estimated at more than two tons.
  • The name 'Bald Eagle' refers to the adult's white head, not a lack of feathers. Young birds remain mostly brown and mottled for several years before developing the familiar white head and tail.

How to know it

Adults

Adults have a dark brown body, white head and tail, heavy yellow hooked bill and yellow feet.

Young birds

Young Bald Eagles lack the white head and tail. They are mostly dark brown with varying amounts of white mottling and gradually acquire adult plumage over several years.

In flight

Very large, with broad wings usually held flat while soaring and a large head projecting well ahead of the wings. Turkey Vultures hold their wings in a shallow V and often rock from side to side.

Voice

The Bald Eagle's real call is a surprisingly high series of chirps and whistles. The powerful 'eagle scream' used in movies is often the call of a Red-tailed Hawk.

Size

Wingspan about six to seven feet. Females are larger than males.

What to look for

Look high in large trees near water or scan the sky for a very large raptor with broad, flat wings. Adults show the familiar white head and tail; immature birds are mostly brown and mottled. Turkey Vultures soar with their wings held in a shallow V.

Easily confused with

Osprey — Ospreys are smaller and slimmer, white below with a dark eye-stripe, and hold their wings with a distinct kink at the wrist. A Bald Eagle is bulkier, dark-bodied, and holds its broad wings flat.

Turkey Vulture — Turkey Vultures have much smaller heads, longer narrower wings held in a shallow V, and often rock while soaring. Bald Eagles look heavier and usually hold their broad wings flatter.

Where to find it here

Look overhead or high in large trees near ponds, bays and other open water. At Palma Sola this is an occasional sighting, not a bird visitors should expect on every visit.

Bald Eagles occur in Florida year-round, but numbers and movements change with the breeding cycle. Florida's nesting activity is concentrated from fall through spring. Palma Sola has only a single recorded sighting, so the park does not yet have enough observations to define a local peak.

When

Bald Eagles occur in Florida year-round and nest primarily from fall through spring. Palma Sola currently has only one documented sighting, so no local seasonal peak can be established.

How it lives

Fish are an important food, but Bald Eagles are opportunistic. They also take birds and mammals and readily eat carrion.

Taking a fish from an Osprey

Bald Eagles sometimes chase Ospreys carrying fish until the Osprey drops its catch, then take the falling fish. This kind of food theft is called kleptoparasitism.

Nesting on a Florida calendar

Florida eagles begin nesting activities in fall, and young are raised through winter and spring. This early schedule takes advantage of Florida's mild winter.

What it does here

Large trees are essential nesting and perching structures. In Florida, most Bald Eagle nests are built in living native pines, although cypress, oaks and mangroves are also used. A nest may be reused and enlarged for years, so the tree must support an enormous load while giving the birds open access to nearby water.

Florida holds one of the densest Bald Eagle populations anywhere outside Alaska. The park has only a single record, but the birds are genuinely around.

Take care

What to do

Never approach or disturb an active nest. Watch from a distance with binoculars or a long lens and leave if the birds change their behavior in response to you. Bald Eagles and their nests remain protected by federal law even though the species is no longer listed as endangered.
