Gray Catbird

Dumetella carolinensis
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it does here

At a glance

  • A sleek, slate-gray bird with a black cap and a rusty patch hidden under the tail — listen for the cat-like mewing call that gives it its name.
  • Like its cousin the mockingbird, it is a mimic, stringing together borrowed phrases — but it rarely repeats them, giving its song a rambling, unrepeated quality.
  • It skulks in dense shrubs and tangles, more often heard than seen.
  • A winter visitor and passage migrant in our area, most likely from fall through spring.

How to know it

Color

Uniform slate gray with a black cap and a rusty-red undertail patch.

Shape

Sleek, medium-sized, long-tailed.

Sound

A distinctive cat-like mewing; also a rambling, non-repeating song.

Behavior

Skulks in dense vegetation; flicks tail to reveal rusty patch.

Voice

A nasal, cat-like 'mew' and a long, rambling song of unrepeated phrases.

Size

8 to 9 inches; about 11 inches

What to look for

A plain gray bird skulking in dense shrubs, mewing like a cat. Look for the black cap and, if it flicks its tail, the hidden rusty patch underneath.

Where to find it here

Listen for the mewing call in dense shrubbery along garden edges and near the ponds.

Mainly a winter visitor and migrant in this area, most likely October through April.

When

Breeds farther north; present here mainly fall through spring.

How it lives

A skulker. It works low through dense cover and sings from somewhere inside it, so you will hear a catbird long before you see one. The tail flicks constantly.

Insects and fruit, with the balance tipping toward berries as fall comes on.

What it does here

Berry disperser and insect consumer in shrubby habitats.

Eats many native berries; forages in dense plantings and hedgerows.

Native to North America. Breeds across the eastern and central U.S. and southern Canada; winters in the southeastern U.S., Caribbean, and Central America. Present in the Bradenton area mainly as a winter visitor.

The gray catbird is a member of the Mimidae — the same mimic family as the mockingbird and brown thrasher. Its song is a long, wandering string of notes and phrases, many borrowed from other birds, but unlike the mockingbird it rarely repeats a phrase, so the song sounds more improvised.

The cat-like mewing call is distinctive and often the first clue that a catbird is hiding in the bushes nearby.

Despite its drab color, the rusty undertail coverts are a reliable field mark if you catch the bird flicking its tail.
