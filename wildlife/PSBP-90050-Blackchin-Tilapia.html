At a glance

  • Fresh, brackish or salt, it copes. This fish lives in fresh water, brackish water and seawater, and it can adjust as the water changes, as long as the change is not sudden.
  • His mouth is the nursery, and it has a size limit. In fish-farm studies, bigger fathers carried more eggs and young, and a small father could run out of room.
  • The usual roles are flipped. In laboratory trials the females did more of the fighting, and the males were the choosy ones, preferring larger partners.
  • It is not from here. Its home is the coastal lagoons of West Africa. Florida's probably came from fish farms around Tampa, though aquarium releases are another possible source.

How to know it

Start with the chin

Adults have irregular dark blotches on the lower head, which is where the name comes from. The markings vary from fish to fish, so treat the dark chin as a first clue, not proof.

Other clues

The mouth is small, and the upper jaw can push forward to pick up food. Young fish have a dark spot at the base of the soft back fin, but other tilapias have that spot too. Telling tilapias apart from the bank takes a clear look at the head.

Where to find it here

Look in the Big Pond, near the bank in the field behind the Galleria. From time to time we see what we think are tilapia circles there, round patches near the bank. Which tilapia makes them, we can't say for sure.

How it lives

A nursery that swims

Once the eggs are fertilized, the male takes them into his mouth. They hatch there, and the young stay inside, living on their yolk, until he lets them go as free-swimming fry. Brooding gets in the way of eating: in West African fish-farm trials, males grew more slowly once they started carrying a brood.

Night shift

It travels in schools and is mostly active at night. It picks up food in small bites: algae, bits of decaying plant matter, small animals. In West African lagoons these fish even swallow bottom mud for the microscopic food in it.

What it does

People have said for years that Ospreys hunt tilapia in the Big Pond and eat them up in the tree branches. We have never checked which tilapia they catch. If you see an Osprey come up with a fish here, we would love a photo.
