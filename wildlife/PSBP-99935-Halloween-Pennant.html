Halloween Pennant

Celithemis eponina
On this page

At a glance

  • A dragonfly with orange-and-brown banded wings, like a small Halloween decoration on a stem beside the pond.
  • It perches at the very tip of a flexible stem and rides the breeze like a flag. Most dragonflies choose something rigid.
  • The wing pattern makes it one of the easiest dragonflies here to name at a glance.

How to know it

Wings and body

Orange-amber wings crossed by dark brown bands, over a yellowish to orange body.

Size

A medium dragonfly, wingspan about three inches.

Perch

Sits at the tip of a flexible stem and sways with it.

Look for the stalk that is moving differently from the ones around it. That is usually the dragonfly rather than the breeze.

Easily confused with

Eastern Amberwing — Eastern Amberwings are much smaller with plain amber wings. The Halloween Pennant is larger and its wings carry bold dark bands rather than an even wash of color.

Where to find it here

Check stem tips around the ponds and wet margins, especially where grasses and sedges stand above open water. Males patrol the open water, but they spend long stretches resting between patrols.

When

A warm-season dragonfly, most common from spring through fall.

How it lives

It perches on the flexible tip of a stem and lets the wind move it, wings held out. That is the easiest way to find one: look for the stalk that is swaying differently from everything around it.

Males patrol stretches of open water and return to a favored perch between flights. The hunting happens in the air — small flying insects taken on the wing.

Before the wings

The young live underwater as nymphs, hunting small aquatic animals among submerged plants. When ready they climb a stem into the air and emerge as adults, leaving the split skin behind on the stalk.

What it does here

It takes small flying insects in the air, and it needs standing plants more than most dragonflies do — emergent grasses and sedges rooted in shallow water, and preferably a flexible stalk it can ride. Where wetland plants are cleared back to open water, the perches go with them.

Most of the animal's life happens underwater and out of sight. The nymph hunts small aquatic animals for months, then climbs out on a stem, splits its skin and emerges as the winged adult, which lives only a few weeks.
