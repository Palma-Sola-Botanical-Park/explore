Halloween Pennant

Celithemis eponina

At a glance

  • A dragonfly with orange-and-brown banded wings — like a tiny Halloween decoration perched on a plant stem near the pond.
  • It perches at the very tip of a stem and flutters in the breeze like a flag, which is unusual for a dragonfly — most prefer rigid, still perches.
  • Males patrol territories over open water, but they spend long stretches resting on stem tips between patrols.
  • The wing pattern makes it one of the easiest dragonflies to identify at a glance.

How to know it

Wings

Orange-amber with dark brown bands — the Halloween pattern.

Perch

Sits at the very tip of flexible stems, swaying in the breeze.

Body

Yellowish to orange body.

Size

Medium dragonfly, wingspan about 3 inches.

Size

about 3 inches

What to look for

A dragonfly with boldly banded orange-and-brown wings perched at the tip of a stem near water.

Easily confused with

Eastern Amberwing — Eastern Amberwings are much smaller with plain amber wings. The Halloween Pennant is larger and its wings carry bold dark bands rather than an even wash of color.

Where to find it here

Check stem tips around the ponds and wet margins, especially where grasses and sedges stand above open water. Look for the stalk that is swaying differently from the ones around it — that is usually the dragonfly, not the breeze.

A warm-season dragonfly, most common spring through fall.

How it lives

It perches on the flexible tip of a stem and lets the wind move it, wings held out. That is the easiest way to find one: look for the stalk that is swaying differently from everything around it.

Males patrol stretches of open water and return to a favored perch between flights. The hunting happens in the air — small flying insects taken on the wing.

Before the wings

The young live underwater as nymphs, hunting small aquatic animals among submerged plants. When ready they climb a stem into the air and emerge as adults, leaving the split skin behind on the stalk.

What it does here

An aerial predator of small flying insects, and prey in turn for birds, frogs and larger dragonflies. The aquatic young hunt small animals underwater before emerging as adults.

This dragonfly needs standing plants more than most. It hunts from the tips of emergent stems — grasses, sedges and other plants rooted in shallow water — and prefers a flexible stalk it can ride rather than a rigid twig. Where wetland plants are cleared back to open water, the perches go with them.

Native to the eastern United States. Common in Florida near ponds and wetlands.

Dragonflies spend most of their lives underwater. The nymph hunts small aquatic animals for months before climbing out on a stem, splitting its skin and emerging as the winged adult that lives only a few weeks.
