Little Blue Heron

Egretta caerulea
On this page

At a glance

  • Adult Little Blue Herons are dark slate-blue with a purplish head and neck, and they usually hunt with slow, deliberate movements in shallow water.
  • Young Little Blue Herons are white. As they acquire adult plumage, blue feathers replace the white in patches, creating a distinctive blue-and-white 'calico' stage.
  • White juveniles are more readily tolerated among feeding Snowy Egrets than dark adults are, and young Little Blues can feed more successfully while accompanying those flocks.
  • Little Blue Herons are State-designated Threatened in Florida. Loss and degradation of wetland feeding and nesting habitat remain concerns.

How to know it

Adult

Dark slate-blue body with a purple-maroon head and neck. The bill is pale blue-gray at the base and dark at the tip.

Juvenile

White overall, with greenish legs and a two-toned gray-and-dark bill.

Molting immature

Blue and white in irregular patches. These transitional birds are often called calico Little Blue Herons.

Size

About two feet tall, with a wingspan a little over three feet.

What to look for

Adults are small dark-blue herons with a purple-toned head and a two-colored bill. White juveniles can resemble Snowy Egrets, but Little Blues have greenish legs and a thicker bill that is pale at the base and dark at the tip. Their feeding movements are also usually slower and more deliberate.

Easily confused with

Snowy Egret — A juvenile Little Blue Heron is white like a Snowy Egret, but has greenish legs and a thicker two-toned bill. Snowy Egrets have black legs with bright yellow feet and usually forage more actively.

Tricolored Heron — Adult Tricolored Herons are also blue-gray but have a bright white belly and a white stripe down the front of the neck. Adult Little Blues are dark below as well as above.

Where to find it here

Look in shallow water along pond edges and wetland margins. Little Blue Herons usually move slowly, watching the water with the bill angled downward before making a short strike.

A year-round Florida resident. Palma Sola has only a few records, so it should be considered an occasional park visitor rather than something to expect on every visit.

When

Occurs year-round in Florida. Palma Sola has too few observations to define a local seasonal pattern.

How it lives

Little Blue Herons usually forage slowly and deliberately, wading through shallow water or pausing to watch for prey. They take small fish, amphibians, crustaceans and insects.

What it does here

A predator of small fish, amphibians, crustaceans and insects in shallow wetlands.

Little Blue Herons nest in colonies in low trees and shrubs, often on islands or over flooded ground where water helps limit access by land predators. The birds gather sticks and greener plant material for the nest. Around feeding areas, emergent vegetation provides cover for the small fish and invertebrates they hunt.

Native to the Americas. In Florida, Little Blue Herons occur year-round in freshwater, brackish and saltwater wetlands.

Take care

Conservation

State-designated Threatened in Florida and globally classified as Least Concern. FWC identifies habitat degradation, disturbance, reduced prey availability and other wetland pressures among potential threats.
