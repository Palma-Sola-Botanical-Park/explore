Red-shouldered Hawk

Buteo lineatus
On this page

At a glance

  • A repeated, descending kee-aah is the Red-shouldered Hawk's familiar call and is often the first clue that one is nearby.
  • It is strongly associated with wooded habitats near water, often hunting from a perch beneath or within the canopy.
  • Adults show reddish barring across the chest and translucent crescent-shaped panels near the wingtips in flight.
  • Blue Jays frequently imitate the Red-shouldered Hawk's call, so a convincing kee-aah does not always belong to a hawk.

How to know it

Shape

Medium-sized, broad-winged hawk with a fairly long, banded tail.

Chest

Adults have reddish-orange barring across the breast.

In flight

Translucent crescent-shaped panels near the wingtips and a strongly banded tail.

Voice

A loud, descending kee-aah, often repeated several times. Blue Jays can imitate it closely.

What to look for

Look for a medium-sized hawk perched inside mature trees, especially near ponds or other wet areas. Adults have reddish barring below; in flight, look for the pale crescents near the wingtips and strongly banded tail. The repeated kee-aah often reveals the bird first.

Voice

The loud, carrying call is one of the most familiar raptor sounds in Florida — but Blue Jays imitate it well and often, so the call on its own is not proof.

Easily confused with

Cooper's Hawk — Longer-tailed and more slender, built for chasing birds through trees. Red-shouldered Hawks are broader-winged and show reddish barring plus pale wing crescents.

Red-tailed Hawk — Usually more associated with open areas, with broader, paler underwings and no translucent wing crescents. Adults often show a reddish tail.

Where to find it here

Look in mature trees around ponds, wooded edges and other damp areas. Red-shouldered Hawks often watch the ground from a mid-level perch rather than sitting conspicuously in completely open country.

A year-round Florida resident. Palma Sola has several records, but all of them come from one observer, so the dataset is not strong enough to call the species reliably present.

How it lives

From a perch inside the canopy, a Red-shouldered Hawk watches the ground and shallow water for frogs, lizards, snakes, crayfish, rodents and other prey, then drops down to capture it.

Pairs are highly vocal and may call back and forth across a territory.

It hunts from low perches, watching the ground from a branch and dropping onto lizards, frogs, snakes, crayfish and small mammals.

What it does here

Trees and water together

Large trees provide both nest sites and hunting perches. Red-shouldered Hawks are strongly associated with mature wooded habitat near water, where branches give them elevated views of prey moving through the ground layer and wetland edge.

Take care

What to do

Keep back from an active nest. If the adults repeatedly call or circle because you are there, move away.
