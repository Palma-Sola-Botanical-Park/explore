Red-shouldered Hawk

Buteo lineatus
On this page

At a glance

  • A loud, repeated 'kee-ahh' ringing through the trees is the signature call of the red-shouldered hawk — Florida's most vocal and commonly seen hawk.
  • It is a woodland hawk, preferring to hunt from a perch under the canopy rather than soaring over open fields.
  • The reddish-orange bars across the chest and the translucent crescent near the wingtips in flight are the key field marks.
  • Blue jays imitate its call convincingly — so well that even experienced birders do a double-take.

How to know it

Chest

Reddish-orange barring across the breast.

Flight

Translucent crescent-shaped panel near the wingtips; banded tail.

Sound

Loud, repeated 'kee-ahh' — carrying and distinctive.

Habitat

Perches in wooded areas near water, not typically soaring high over open fields.

Voice

A loud, descending 'kee-ahh, kee-ahh' — one of the most recognizable hawk calls in the eastern U.S.

Size

17 to 24 inches; about 3.3 feet (40 inches)

What to look for

A medium hawk perched in a tree near water, with a barred reddish chest. The loud 'kee-ahh' call is often the first clue.

Where to find it here

Listen for the 'kee-ahh' call in the canopy near the ponds.

Year-round resident, vocal and visible in every season.

When

Nests in spring.

How it lives

It hunts from a perch, sitting partway up in the canopy, watching the ground and dropping on whatever crosses it — lizards, frogs, snakes, crayfish, a small mammal, large insects.

You will usually hear it first. Pairs call back and forth across a territory, a loud repeated scream that carries a long way.

What it does here

Top predator of small vertebrates in wooded habitats; helps control lizard and rodent populations.

Nests in large trees; hunts from canopy perches.

Native to eastern North America. A common year-round resident in Florida.

The red-shouldered hawk is the hawk of Florida's neighborhoods and wooded parks. It is more tied to trees and water than the open-country red-tailed hawk, so it turns up regularly in suburban landscapes with mature canopy.

It hunts from low perches, dropping onto lizards, frogs, snakes, crayfish, and small mammals — prey it spots by watching the ground from a branch.

Its loud, carrying call is one of the most commonly heard raptor sounds in Florida, and blue jays mimic it often enough that the call alone is not a guarantee.
