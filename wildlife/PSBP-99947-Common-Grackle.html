Common Grackle

Quiscalus quiscula
On this page

At a glance

  • A lanky blackbird with pale yellow eyes and a long tail that it folds into a keel or V shape in flight.
  • The black is structural, not pigment. In direct light the head and body show bronze, purple or green.
  • It walks rather than hops, striding across open lawns in loose flocks.
  • The call is a short, harsh creak, often compared to a rusty hinge.

How to know it

Color

All black with an iridescent bronze, purple or green sheen in sunlight. The eyes are pale yellow.

Tail

Long and keel-shaped, folded into a V in flight.

Size

About a foot long, with a wingspan around seventeen inches.

Voice

A loud, squeaky creak, like a rusty hinge.

What to look for

On a lawn, look for a long-tailed blackbird walking rather than hopping, with a pale eye that stands out against the dark head.

Iridescence

In good light the sheen is the whole bird — a bronze or greenish body with a purplish head. In flat light it simply looks black.

Easily confused with

Brown-headed Cowbird — Cowbirds are stocky and short-tailed with a dark eye; the male has a chocolate-brown head. Grackles are larger and longer-tailed with a pale yellow eye.

Where to find it here

Open lawns, pond edges and parking areas, usually in loose flocks rather than singly.

A year-round Florida resident.

How it lives

Walks on the ground and forages in flocks, taking insects, seeds, grain, fruit and whatever else is available. It will also take eggs and nestlings from other birds' nests.

Outside the breeding season grackles roost communally, sometimes in very large numbers.

What it does here

An omnivore of open ground — insects, seeds and fruit — and a nest predator on smaller birds.

Lawn and seed

Grackles use the boundary between mown grass and seeding plants: short turf to walk and hunt insects on, and grasses, weeds and fruiting shrubs nearby for seed and fruit. They are among the birds most at home in a landscape that is partly cultivated.
