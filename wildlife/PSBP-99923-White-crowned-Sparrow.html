White-crowned Sparrow

Zonotrichia leucophrys
On this page

At a glance

  • Adults have bold black-and-white crown stripes, a plain gray breast and a pinkish or yellowish bill.
  • White-crowned Sparrows breed far to the north and west and occur in Florida only during the nonbreeding season.
  • On the ground, they often use a two-footed backward hop to kick aside leaves and expose seeds and other food.
  • Their songs are learned rather than entirely innate. Regional populations develop recognizable song dialects, and birds living where two dialects meet may learn both.

How to know it

The crown

Adults have bold black-and-white crown stripes. Immature birds have the same striped pattern in brown and tan.

Build

Fairly large for a sparrow, with an upright posture and plain gray breast.

The double-scratch

On the ground it often hops backward with both feet to move leaves aside, then moves forward to pick up exposed food.

Voice

The song begins with clear whistles and ends with buzzes or trills. White-crowned Sparrows have regional song dialects. Calls include a sharp chink.

Size

About seven inches long — a fairly large sparrow.

What to look for

Look low along brushy borders, weedy ground and thickets. Adults have bold black-and-white crown stripes; immature birds have brown-and-tan stripes. Watch for birds scratching backward through leaf litter.

Easily confused with

White-throated Sparrow — Also has bold head stripes, but shows a bright white throat patch and yellow between the eye and bill. White-crowned Sparrows have a plain gray throat and no yellow facial spot.

Where to find it here

Brushy edges, low thickets, weedy ground and leaf litter near open grassy areas.

A winter visitor to Florida. Palma Sola sightings are occasional, so do not expect to find one on every visit.

How it lives

In winter, White-crowned Sparrows spend much of the day on or near the ground, moving between open feeding patches and nearby shrubs where they can quickly take cover. Outside the breeding season they often occur in loose flocks.

Seeds make up much of the winter diet, including seeds of grasses and other low plants. Insects become more important during the breeding season.

It forages on the ground with one signature move — the double-scratch, a quick two-footed hop backward that kicks the leaf litter aside and uncovers seeds.

What it does here

A ground-feeding seed and insect consumer that becomes part of Florida's winter songbird community.

Low cover, low food

Low vegetation provides both food and cover. White-crowned Sparrows feed on seeds from grasses and other weedy plants, scratch through leaf litter beneath shrubs, and retreat into dense thickets when disturbed.

Some White-crowned Sparrows fly more than 2,500 miles from the Alaskan tundra to reach their wintering grounds. That is a long haul for a bird weighing about as much as a few quarters.
