Roseate Skimmer

Orthemis ferruginea

At a glance

  • A dragonfly with a vivid rose-pink to purple body cruising the pond edge is a mature male Roseate Skimmer.
  • The color belongs to mature males only. Females and young males are brown and tan, so the same species can look like two different insects.
  • Males hold territories along the shoreline, patrolling low over the water and returning to a favored perch.
  • Before flight, the young live underwater as nymphs, hunting mosquito larvae and other small aquatic animals.

How to know it

Color

Mature males are rose-pink to red-violet; females and young males are brown and tan.

Wings

Mostly clear, sometimes with a faint amber tint at the base.

Size

A medium dragonfly, around two inches long.

What to look for

Watch the pond edge for a slim dragonfly perched on a low stem, then out over the water and back to the same stem. If it is pink, it is a mature male.

A mature male Roseate Skimmer is pink-purple rather than the Scarlet Skimmer's red. Females and immature males of both species are much less distinctive and take more care.

Where to find it here

Around the ponds and any ditches or basins. Find an exposed stem near sunny water and watch it for a minute — a male may leave on a short patrol and come back to the same perch.

A warm-season flier, common from spring through autumn.

How it lives

Active by day in warm weather, patrolling low over still water and perching on low vegetation between sorties. Males hold territories along the shoreline.

Adults take small flying insects on the wing. The aquatic young hunt mosquito larvae and other small water creatures before climbing out to emerge.

What it does here

Why the edge matters

Breeding needs still water with a vegetated margin. The low stems along a pond edge are perches for hunting males and cover for the young below; a bank mown clean to open water offers neither.

When development underwater is complete, the nymph climbs a stem above the surface, splits its skin and emerges as the winged adult.
