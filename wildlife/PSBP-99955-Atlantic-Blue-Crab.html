Atlantic Blue Crab

Callinectes sapidus
On this page

At a glance

  • A swimming crab with sapphire-blue claws turning up in a park pond is the Atlantic Blue Crab, and its presence indicates the water carries some salt.
  • Its scientific name means roughly 'savory beautiful swimmer' — a rare case of a name describing both the animal and the appetite for it.
  • The rear pair of legs are flattened into paddles, so unlike most crabs it genuinely swims rather than only walking.
  • Mature females can be told by red-tipped claws; males have blue ones.

How to know it

Size

The shell can reach about nine inches across, counting the sharp spines at each side.

Color

Olive to grayish body with bright blue legs and claws. Mature females have red-tipped claws.

Back legs

The rear pair are flattened into paddles for swimming.

What to look for

Scan the shallows and edges for a broad, spined crab with blue on the claws — or for one swimming, which few other crabs here do.

Where to find it here

In and around the ponds where the water carries any brackish influence. Scan the shallows and margins.

Possible whenever conditions suit, and most active in warm water.

How it lives

Swims with its paddle legs and forages along the bottom, taking clams, snails, small fish, plant material and whatever has died and settled.

It burrows into mud to molt and to wait out cold periods, and defends itself readily with strong pincers when cornered.

What it does here

A wide-ranging omnivore and scavenger that recycles material in brackish water, and an important link in coastal food webs.

What it says about the pond

This is an estuarine animal. Finding one inland means the pond has a tidal or saltwater connection, so the crab is itself a piece of evidence about how the water behaves.

Take care

The pincers are strong and a cornered or handled crab can deliver a hard pinch. Do not try to pick one up, and keep pets away from one at the water's edge.

Look, do not touch. Give it room along the shoreline.

Conservation

Not a conservation concern in its native range, though it is a heavily fished species whose stocks are managed.

Also known as
