Eastern Screech-Owl

Megascops asio
On this page

At a glance

  • Eastern Screech-Owls have been found roosting in Palma Sola's fruit trees, sitting motionless against bark where their patterned feathers make them easy to overlook.
  • Despite the name, its familiar songs are not screeches. Listen for a descending whinny or a steady, even trill after dark.
  • It is only about eight or nine inches tall — roughly the height of a soda can — with feather tufts on the head that look like ears but are not.
  • Eastern Screech-Owls occur in gray and reddish-brown forms. Both are patterned with streaks and bars that blend closely with tree bark.

How to know it

Size

Small — about eight to nine inches tall.

Ear tufts

Two feather tufts can be raised or flattened. They are not the owl's ears.

Color

Either gray or reddish-brown, with heavy streaking and barring that breaks up the bird's outline against bark. The eyes are yellow.

Voice

The two familiar songs are a descending whinny and an even-pitched trill. Eastern Screech-Owls also give other calls, including alarm barks and occasional screeches.

What to look for

By day, look closely at tree cavities and shaded trunks for a small, stocky owl with ear tufts. At night, the descending whinny or steady trill is usually the easier way to find one.

Easily confused with

Great Horned Owl — Much larger, with a wingspan measured in feet rather than inches. Both have ear tufts and yellow eyes, but an Eastern Screech-Owl is only about soda-can height.

Where to find it here

Look in mature trees, especially around natural cavities and shaded trunks. At dusk and after dark, listen in wooded parts of the park for whinnies and trills.

A year-round Florida resident, active mainly at night. A daytime sighting usually involves a bird resting in or near a tree cavity. Palma Sola has only a few documented records, so local frequency is not yet well established.

How it lives

Hunts mainly at night from low or middle-height perches, dropping or flying out after prey. Large insects, spiders, earthworms, small rodents, lizards, frogs and small birds are all taken.

By day it roosts in tree cavities or against bark. Pairs nest in natural cavities and readily use nest boxes.

What it does here

A nighttime predator of insects and small vertebrates, and a species strongly dependent on tree cavities for roosting and nesting.

Why old trees matter

Trees are essential habitat. Eastern Screech-Owls roost and nest in natural cavities, often in mature hardwoods, and use the surrounding branches as hunting perches. Old trees, snags and trees with cavities therefore provide habitat that younger, uniformly pruned plantings cannot replace.

Take care

No meaningful danger when watched normally. Do not handle or approach a nesting or injured owl.

If you find a roosting owl, watch quietly from a distance and let it rest. Never approach a nest cavity or repeatedly draw other visitors to a roosting bird.

Conservation

Widespread and currently of low conservation concern. The species depends on natural cavities and can benefit where mature cavity-bearing trees are retained.
