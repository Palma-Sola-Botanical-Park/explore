Black Witch Moth

Ascalapha odorata
On this page

At a glance

  • A Black Witch can span five to seven inches across the wings. By day it often rests flat in a dark sheltered place; in flight at dusk, its size can make it look briefly batlike.
  • The Black Witch carries very different traditions across its range. Some Mexican and Central American folklore associates it with death, while traditions elsewhere associate the same moth with money or good fortune.
  • Despite its size, the adult cannot sting and poses no danger to people. It feeds on juices from ripe or fermenting fruit and tree sap.
  • Black Witches are strong long-distance wanderers. Individuals regularly move north from tropical breeding areas and have been recorded far beyond the species' usual range.

How to know it

Size

Very large, with a wingspan around five to seven inches.

Color

Dark brown with many fine wavy lines. Females show much stronger pale bands across the wings than males.

Marking

A small curved pale mark occurs on each forewing.

Posture

Often rests flat with the wings spread in a shaded, sheltered place during the day.

What to look for

Look in deep shade under eaves, bridges, dense foliage and other sheltered places for an exceptionally large, dark moth resting with its wings spread.

Where to find it here

By day, check shaded walls, eaves, dense foliage and other sheltered places. At night, adults may visit fermenting fruit or lights.

Most likely during warm weather, when wandering adults move north from tropical breeding areas. Palma Sola records are too limited to define a local peak.

How it lives

Adults feed at damaged or fermenting fruit and at tree sap, using the proboscis to take in liquid. They are active mainly after dark and usually hidden in shade by day.

The caterpillars feed on several kinds of woody plants in the legume family. Adults can travel far from the places where they developed, which is why the species sometimes appears well north of its usual tropical range.

What it does here

Caterpillars are herbivores on legume trees and shrubs, while adults use fruit and sap as food. Black Witches in turn become prey for birds, bats and other predators.

Two different plant lives

The caterpillars feed on several woody plants in the legume family, including relatives of acacias, cassias and mimosas. Adults have a different plant relationship entirely: they visit damaged fruit and flowing tree sap rather than depending on flowers.

Take care

Does not sting and poses no danger to people.

Leave a resting moth in place and observe without handling. Daytime resting sites allow nocturnal moths to remain sheltered until evening.

Conservation

Widespread in tropical America and not currently considered threatened.
