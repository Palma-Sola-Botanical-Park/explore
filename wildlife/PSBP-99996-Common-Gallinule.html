Common Gallinule

Gallinula galeata
On this page

At a glance

  • A Common Gallinule is a dark marsh bird with a bright red forehead shield, a red-and-yellow bill and a white stripe along each side.
  • It swims like a duck but has no webbing on its feet. The Common Gallinule belongs to the rail family.
  • Very long toes spread its weight across soft mud and floating vegetation, letting the bird walk where a web-footed duck would push through.
  • Gallinules are often heard before they are seen, giving loud clucks, squawks and whinnying calls from dense pond vegetation.

How to know it

Adults

Dark slate-gray with a brownish back, bright red forehead shield, red bill tipped with yellow and a bold white stripe along the side.

Legs and feet

Yellow-green legs with extremely long, unwebbed toes.

Young

Brown and paler below, with a dull greenish bill and forehead shield. The white side stripe remains useful.

Voice

Listen for loud clucks, squawks and whinnying calls from dense wetland vegetation.

Size

A medium-sized marsh bird, a little over a foot long.

What to look for

Look along vegetated pond edges for a dark bird swimming with a head-bobbing motion or walking across floating plants. The red-and-yellow bill and white side stripe identify an adult.

Easily confused with

American Coot — Coots have a white bill and forehead shield and lobed toes. Common Gallinules have a bright red-and-yellow bill, a white stripe along the side and long toes without lobes.

Where to find it here

Look where open water meets thick aquatic vegetation. Gallinules swim along the edges and walk across floating leaves and plant mats, often disappearing quickly into cover.

A year-round Florida resident and regularly recorded at Palma Sola. Listen as well as look; birds hidden in pond-edge vegetation often call.

When

Recorded repeatedly at Palma Sola over several years.

How it lives

Long toes let the bird walk across floating leaves, plant mats and soft mud. It also swims well despite having no webbed feet.

Gallinules eat both plants and small animals, picking seeds and vegetation from the water and taking insects, snails and other invertebrates.

What it does here

An omnivore in marsh food webs, eating aquatic plants, seeds, insects and snails and becoming prey for larger wetland predators.

Aquatic plants are feeding surface, food, cover and nest material. Gallinules walk across floating leaves, eat seeds and other plant parts, hide in emergent vegetation and build broad nests from grasses and sedges anchored among wetland plants.

Native across much of the Americas. In Florida it is a year-round resident of freshwater and brackish ponds, marshes and other quiet water with abundant aquatic vegetation.

Until 2011, the New World Common Gallinule and the Old World Common Moorhen were treated as one species. They are now classified separately.
