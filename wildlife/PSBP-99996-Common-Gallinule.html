Common Gallinule

Gallinula galeata
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it doesPhotographs

At a glance

  • A dark marsh bird with a bright red forehead shield, a red bill tipped in yellow, and a white stripe along its side.
  • Its feet are not webbed. Very long toes spread its weight, so it can walk across floating leaves.
  • It flips floating leaves with its feet to find the small animals underneath.
  • Older chicks from an earlier hatch sometimes stay and help feed the new ones.

How to know it

Adults

Dark slate-gray with a brownish back, a bright red forehead shield, a red bill tipped with yellow, and a bold white stripe along the side. The legs are yellow-green. It is a little over a foot long.

Young birds

Brown and paler below, with a dull greenish bill and shield. The white side stripe still helps.

Voice

Loud clucks, squawks and whinnying calls from dense vegetation are often the first sign one is there. A coot is the look-alike, but it has a white bill and shield.

Where to find it here

Where water meets plants

Look where open water meets thick plants. Gallinules swim along the edge, walk across floating leaves, and slip quickly into cover. Listen as well as look.

How it lives

Walking on the water

It swims well, but it has no webbed feet. Its toes are long and widely spread, which spreads its weight across floating leaves, plant mats and soft mud.

Leaf flipper

It eats seeds and plants and also insects, snails and other small animals. It sometimes flips floating leaves with its feet to expose animals hiding underneath.

Family help

Young birds from an earlier brood may stay with their parents and help brood or feed the next chicks.

What it does

Pond plants

To a gallinule, pond plants are the floor, the pantry, the hiding place and the nursery. It walks across them, eats their seeds and leaves, hides in the upright stems, and builds a broad nest of grasses and sedges anchored among them.
