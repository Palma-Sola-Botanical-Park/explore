Yellow-crowned Night Heron

Nyctanassa violacea
On this page

At a glance

  • Adults are gray with a black head, broad white cheek patch and pale yellow crown.
  • Crabs and crayfish dominate the diet, which helps explain the species' unusually stout, heavy bill.
  • Despite the name, Yellow-crowned Night Herons feed in daylight as well as after dark. In coastal habitats, feeding activity may follow the tides more closely than the clock.
  • Young birds are brown and spotted and can look surprisingly similar to young Black-crowned Night Herons.

How to know it

Adults

Gray body, black head, broad white cheek patch and pale yellow crown. Breeding birds grow long pale head plumes.

Bill

Thick, heavy and black.

Young

Brown with pale spots and streaks. Young birds lack the adult's bold face pattern and are much harder to separate from young Black-crowned Night Herons.

Size

About two feet tall, with a wingspan around three and a half feet.

What to look for

Adults are compact gray herons with a bold black-and-white face and thick black bill. They often walk slowly with the body angled forward while searching shallow water, mud or wet ground for crustaceans.

Easily confused with

Black-crowned Night Heron — Adults are straightforward: Black-crowned has a black back over pale gray wings, while Yellow-crowned is gray-bodied with a strongly patterned black-and-white face. Juveniles of the two species are much harder to separate and may require a close view of bill shape, leg proportions and spotting.

Where to find it here

Look along pond margins, wet ground and other shallow areas where crayfish or crabs are available. During rest periods, check waterside trees and shrubs.

A year-round Florida resident. Palma Sola has only a few records, so local frequency and peak timing are not yet established.

When

Occurs year-round in Florida. Palma Sola has too few records to establish a local seasonal pattern.

How it lives

It walks slowly through shallow water and wet ground, searching mainly for crabs and crayfish. Feeding may occur in daylight or after dark.

It roosts and nests in trees and shrubs, either alone or in colonies. Some nesting sites are reused for many years.

What it does here

A predator specialized heavily on crabs and crayfish in coastal and inland wetlands.

Yellow-crowned Night Herons roost and nest in trees and shrubs near water. Documented nest sites include oaks, pines, mangroves, myrtles and other woody plants. The stick platform may be lined with leaves, vines or Spanish moss.

Native to the Americas. In Florida it occurs year-round in coastal wetlands, mangroves, swamps and inland areas where crabs or crayfish are abundant.
