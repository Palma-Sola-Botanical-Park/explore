Yellow-crowned Night Heron

Nyctanassa violacea
On this page

At a glance

  • Adults are gray with a black head, a broad white cheek patch and a pale yellow crown.
  • Crabs and crayfish dominate the diet, which is why the bill is so unusually heavy.
  • Young birds are brown and spotted, and can be very hard to tell from young Black-crowned Night Herons.

How to know it

Adults

Gray body, black head, broad white cheek patch and pale yellow crown. Breeding birds grow long pale plumes off the back of the head.

Bill

Thick, heavy and black.

Young birds

Brown with pale spots and streaks, without the adult's bold face pattern.

A feeding bird walks slowly with its body tilted forward, watching shallow water and wet ground ahead of it.

Easily confused with

Black-crowned Night Heron — Adults are straightforward: Black-crowned has a black back over pale gray wings, while Yellow-crowned is gray-bodied with a strongly patterned black-and-white face. Juveniles of the two species are much harder to separate and may require a close view of bill shape, leg proportions and spotting.

Where to find it here

Pond margins and wet ground where crayfish are available. When the bird is resting rather than feeding, check the waterside trees and shrubs instead.

In Florida year-round. The park has only a few records — too few to say anything useful about the time of year.

How it lives

It walks slowly through shallow water and wet ground, searching mainly for crabs and crayfish. Feeding may occur in daylight or after dark.

It roosts and nests in trees and shrubs, either alone or in colonies. Some nesting sites are reused for many years.

What it does here

The heavy bill explains the diet. This is a crab and crayfish specialist, and the bill is built to crack them rather than to spear fish.

The name is only half right. They feed in daylight as readily as after dark, and on the coast the timing follows the tide more closely than the clock.

They roost and nest in waterside trees and shrubs — oaks, pines, mangroves and myrtles have all been used — building a stick platform lined with leaves, vines or Spanish moss.
