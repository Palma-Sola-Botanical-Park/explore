Yellow-crowned Night Heron

Nyctanassa violacea
On this page

At a glance

  • The yellow-crowned night heron is a stocky gray wading bird with a bold black-and-white face and a creamy crown.
  • It is a crustacean specialist — more than most herons, it lives on crabs and crayfish, and its stout, heavy bill is built for cracking their shells.
  • Despite the 'night' in its name, it feeds by day as well as after dark, whenever its prey is active.
  • You will usually find it alone, hunched and leaning forward at the water's edge, watching the mud and shallows for movement.

How to know it

Easily confused with

Black-crowned Night Heron — The black-crowned has a black crown AND back over pale gray wings; the yellow-crowned is evenly gray with a black-and-white face and creamy crown.

Adults

Smooth gray body, a black head with a broad white cheek patch and a pale creamy crown, and long white head plumes in breeding season.

Bill

Stout, heavy, and black — noticeably thicker than other herons'.

Young

Brown with fine white spots and streaks.

Posture

Hunched and forward-leaning.

Voice

Generally quiet; gives a sharp, barking squawk, especially in flight or around the colony.

Size

22 to 28 inches; about 3.5 feet (42 inches)

What to look for

A compact gray heron with a striking black-and-white face, often hunched at the water's edge while hunting — though it stands more upright and longer-legged when walking. The heavy bill and bold face pattern set it apart from the lankier, plainer herons; young birds are brown and spotted.

Where to find it here

Alone at the pond edges and shallows, hunched and watching for crayfish, or roosting quietly in waterside trees. Active by day as well as toward dusk.

Year-round resident, though often quiet and easy to overlook. Active in daylight as well as at night.

When

Quiet and easy to overlook.

How it lives

Forages day and night, usually alone, with a slow, hunched stalk. Roosts in trees and shrubs and nests in them too, sometimes in small colonies. Most active where crabs and crayfish are plentiful.

Mostly crabs and crayfish, cracked open with its heavy bill, plus some fish, insects, and other small animals.

What it does here

A specialized predator of crabs and crayfish, helping regulate those populations and linking the park's waters to the wider coastal food web.

Nests and roosts in waterside trees and shrubs, including mangroves — so the trees ringing a pond matter to it, even though its food comes from the water.

Native to the Americas and found only in this hemisphere, unlike its black-crowned cousin, which occurs worldwide. In Florida it is a year-round resident, most common around coastal marshes, mangroves, and other wetlands.

Among the herons, the yellow-crowned is the great crab specialist. Diet studies routinely find crustaceans making up the large majority of what it eats — crabs along the coast, crayfish inland — and its unusually thick, strong bill is the tool that lets it crush those hard shells.

It is also less tied to the night than its name suggests. It forages whenever crabs and crayfish are active, which can be midday as easily as midnight, usually hunting alone with a slow, deliberate, forward-leaning stalk.

Like other herons it nests in trees and shrubs near water, sometimes alone and sometimes in small colonies, building a stick platform that a pair may enlarge and reuse year after year.

Take care

What to do

Watch from a distance and do not feed it. A hunting heron needs quiet to catch its prey.
