Brown Pelican

Pelecanus occidentalis
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it does here

At a glance

  • Brown Pelicans hunt by plunging headfirst from the air after fish. American White Pelicans feed mainly from the surface instead.
  • DDT once caused severe breeding failures in Brown Pelicans. Populations recovered strongly after the pesticide was banned.
  • The throat pouch acts as a scoop. After a dive, the pelican drains the water before swallowing the fish.
  • Brown Pelicans often fly just above the water in lines, alternating wingbeats with long glides.

How to know it

Color

Gray-brown body with a pale head and long gray bill. Breeding adults develop richer yellow and chestnut colors on the head and neck.

Flight

Often flies low over the water in lines, alternating flaps and glides.

Feeding

Plunge-dives headfirst from the air.

Size

Wingspan about six to seven feet.

What to look for

Look for a large brown-gray pelican flying low over nearby water or plunging headfirst after fish. American White Pelicans are much whiter and usually feed from the water's surface rather than diving.

Easily confused with

American White Pelican — Much larger and almost entirely white, with black flight feathers. White Pelicans usually scoop fish from the surface; Brown Pelicans characteristically plunge-dive.

Where to find it here

Look overhead for birds moving between nearby coastal waters. Brown Pelicans are primarily birds of bays, estuaries and the coast, so Palma Sola sightings are more likely to be flyovers than birds using the park ponds.

Present year-round around the Bradenton coast. Palma Sola has only one recorded sighting, so park appearances should be considered rare.

When

Year-round coastal resident; Palma Sola currently has only one documented sighting.

How it lives

A hunting bird spots fish from above, folds its wings and plunges into the water. The throat pouch scoops fish and water; the water is drained before the catch is swallowed.

Between feeding bouts, Brown Pelicans rest on pilings, docks, sandbars and other open sites or travel low over coastal water.

What it does here

A predator of schooling fish in coastal and estuarine waters.

Brown Pelicans nest colonially on coastal islands, using mangroves, shrubs or low trees where available, as well as the ground on suitable islands. Coastal vegetation provides nest structure and helps isolate colonies from disturbance and land predators.

Native to the coasts of the Americas. In Florida it is a year-round resident of bays, estuaries, beaches and nearshore waters.
