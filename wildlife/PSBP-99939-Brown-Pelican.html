Brown Pelican

Pelecanus occidentalis

At a glance

  • Brown Pelicans hunt by plunging headfirst from the air after fish. American White Pelicans feed mainly from the surface instead.
  • DDT once caused severe breeding failures in Brown Pelicans. Populations recovered strongly after the pesticide was banned.
  • The throat pouch acts as a scoop. After a dive, the pelican drains the water before swallowing the fish.
  • Brown Pelicans often fly just above the water in lines, alternating wingbeats with long glides.

How to know it

Size

A heavy coastal bird with a wingspan of six to seven feet.

Color

Gray-brown body with a pale head and a long gray bill. Breeding adults develop richer yellow and chestnut tones on the head and neck.

In flight

Travels low over the water in loose lines, alternating a few wingbeats with a long glide.

Feeding

Plunge-dives headfirst from the air. No other large waterbird here feeds that way.

Look overhead rather than along the shoreline — a park sighting is almost always a bird crossing between coastal waters.

Easily confused with

American White Pelican — Much larger and almost entirely white, with black flight feathers. White Pelicans usually scoop fish from the surface; Brown Pelicans characteristically plunge-dive.

Where to find it here

Brown Pelicans belong to the bay and the beach, not to park ponds. The realistic sighting at Palma Sola is a bird passing overhead, so this is one to catch by looking up.

They are present along the Bradenton coast all year, but the park has a single documented record. Seeing one here is luck rather than timing.

How it lives

A hunting bird spots fish from above, folds its wings and plunges into the water. The throat pouch scoops fish and water; the water is drained before the catch is swallowed.

Between feeding bouts, Brown Pelicans rest on pilings, docks, sandbars and other open sites or travel low over coastal water.

What it does here

Brown Pelicans hunt schooling fish in bays and nearshore water. The throat pouch works as a scoop: after a dive the bird drains the water out over the edge of the bill before swallowing.

They nest in colonies on coastal islands, building in mangroves, shrubs and low trees where those exist and on open ground where they do not. Coastal vegetation gives the nests structure and keeps the colony separated from disturbance and from land predators.

The species is also one of the clearest conservation recoveries in the country. DDT thinned pelican eggshells badly enough to cause widespread breeding failure; after the pesticide was banned in 1972 numbers climbed back, and Florida's population was removed from the federal endangered list in 1985.

Take care

What to do

Never offer fish by hand, and keep discarded fishing line and hooks out of the water and off the shoreline. Line and tackle are among the most serious hazards pelicans face.
