Yellow-throated Vireo

Vireo flavifrons
On this page

At a glance

  • A Yellow-throated Vireo combines a bright yellow throat and yellow eye markings with an olive back, gray wings and two white wing bars.
  • This is a migrant through Florida rather than a breeding bird at Palma Sola. It passes through the state on its journeys between northern breeding grounds and tropical wintering areas.
  • Behavior helps separate it from similarly colored warblers. Yellow-throated Vireos move methodically through branches, often pausing to inspect a perch before continuing.
  • The song is a slow series of burry, two- or three-part phrases separated by noticeable pauses.

How to know it

Summary

A sturdy songbird with a thick, slightly hooked bill, bright yellow throat and breast, yellow markings around the eyes, olive-green upperparts and two white wing bars. The belly is white.

Confusion species

Pine Warblers can show similar yellow-and-white colors, but their bills are thinner and their movements are usually quicker. Yellow-throated Vireos have a heavier, slightly hooked bill and bold yellow around the eyes.

Voice

The song is a slow series of short, burry phrases with clear pauses between them. The call is harsher and less musical.

Size

About five to six inches long, with a wingspan around nine inches.

What to look for

Search the middle and upper parts of mature trees for a yellow-throated bird moving slowly through branches. Look for the yellow eye markings, two white wing bars and thick, slightly hooked bill. Its deliberate pace is often as useful as its color.

Easily confused with

Pine Warbler — Pine Warblers can show similar yellow-and-white plumage, but Yellow-throated Vireos have a heavier, slightly hooked bill and bold yellow markings around the eyes.

Where to find it here

Look in mature trees along woodland edges, gardens and other open wooded areas. Yellow-throated Vireos usually forage in the middle and upper canopy, where their slow movements can make them surprisingly easy to overlook.

A spring and fall migrant through Florida. Palma Sola has only a few records, all from one spring date, so the park does not yet have enough observations to define a reliable local migration peak.

When

Spring and fall migrant through Florida. Palma Sola records are currently too limited to define local peak dates.

How it lives

It searches branches and foliage slowly, pausing often to inspect leaves, twigs and bark for prey.

Most of the diet is insects, especially caterpillars and other small invertebrates. Fruit becomes more important during migration and on the wintering grounds.

Like many small migratory songbirds, it travels mostly at night.

What it does here

A canopy predator of insects and other small invertebrates. Fruit eaten during migration may also move seeds away from the parent plant.

Trees provide nearly everything this bird needs during a migration stop: branches and leaves hold the insects it hunts, and fruiting shrubs and trees can provide additional food. During the breeding season farther north, pairs suspend a cup-shaped nest from a fork in a small branch using plant fibers and spider silk.

Native to the Americas. Yellow-throated Vireos breed across much of the eastern United States and southern Canada and winter in Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean and northern South America. Florida lies along the migration route.
