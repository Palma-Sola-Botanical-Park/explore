Cedar Waxwing

Bombycilla cedrorum
On this page

At a glance

  • Berries, swallowed whole. A waxwing plucks fruit and gulps it down in one piece, and fruit stays a big part of its diet all year round.
  • A winter visitor. In Florida, waxwings arrive from about October and are gone by May. That is a broad season, not a timetable.
  • A tail that keeps a record. The yellow band at the tip of the tail can come out orange, depending on what the bird was eating while that feather grew.
  • Fruit can go to their heads. Berries that have fermented on the branch can make waxwings drunk, and a drunk waxwing flies badly.

How to know it

The mask and the band

Look for a smooth, tan-brown bird with a pointed crest, a black mask with a neat pale border, a pale yellow belly and a bright yellow band across the end of the tail. The crest can lie flat against the head, so the outline changes with posture.

Where the name comes from

Some birds carry small red tips on a few wing feathers, like drops of red wax. They are the flattened ends of the feather shafts. They can be hard to see, and some birds have none, so a bird without them can still be a waxwing. Young birds are streaked underneath and have a weaker mask.

Not a starling

A flock in a treetop can look like starlings at first. Up close, a waxwing shows the black mask and the yellow tail band. A starling has neither, and in winter it is covered in pale speckles.

How it lives

A gift passed back

Courting birds hop toward each other and may touch bills. A berry, an insect or a flower petal can pass from one bird to the other and back again before the female eats it.

Hovering for fruit, zigzagging for insects

At a fruiting tree a flock gathers to feed. Each bird plucks berries and swallows them whole, sometimes hovering for a moment beneath a cluster to reach one. Waxwings hunt flying insects too, flying out from a perch or zigzagging low over water.

Too much of a good thing

Berries left on the branch can ferment. A waxwing that eats enough of them can become drunk, and a drunk bird flies badly. It is one strange cost of living on fruit.

Nests far from here

Waxwings raise their young well north of Florida. The female does most of the building, weaving twigs, grasses and other fibers into a bulky cup, and waxwings sometimes take material from other birds' nests. Both parents feed the chicks: mostly insects at first, then more and more berries.

What it does

Food you can see in a feather

Some waxwings have orange tail tips instead of yellow. In a feeding experiment, tail feathers that grew while the birds were eating Morrow's honeysuckle berries came out orange at the tip, and feathers replaced after the diet changed grew yellow again. The color comes from pigment in the food, laid down while the feather forms. So a waxwing's tail carries a small record of what it was eating while that feather grew.
