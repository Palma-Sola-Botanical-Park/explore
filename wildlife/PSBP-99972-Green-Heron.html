Green Heron

Butorides virescens
On this page

At a glance

  • The Green Heron is a small, stocky heron with a dark green-gray back, chestnut neck and dark cap.
  • It sometimes uses bait, dropping insects, feathers or other small objects onto the water to bring fish within striking range.
  • It often hunts from a bank or low branch, crouched almost horizontally over the water and partly hidden by vegetation.
  • At rest the neck looks very short. During a strike it extends suddenly, revealing how much neck was folded into that compact body.

How to know it

Size

Small and stocky, only about a foot and a half tall, with a wingspan a little over two feet.

Color

Dark green-gray back, chestnut neck and breast, dark cap and yellowish legs.

Posture

Often crouched low and horizontal at the water's edge. The neck is usually drawn in, making the bird look almost neckless until it strikes.

Voice

A sharp, explosive skeow is often given as the bird flushes from cover — frequently the first sign it was there at all.

What to look for

Look at shaded, vegetated pond edges for a small dark heron crouched low on a bank or branch. The chestnut neck, dark cap and compact shape separate it from the taller wading birds.

Easily confused with

Yellow-crowned Night Heron — Larger, gray overall and much heavier-billed, with a bold black-and-white face. Green Herons are smaller, chestnut-necked and usually crouch much lower.

Little Blue Heron — Larger and longer-legged, with a uniform slate-blue adult body. Green Herons are stockier with a chestnut neck.

Where to find it here

Search quiet, vegetated pond margins rather than open water. Green Herons often perch on low branches, roots or banks partly hidden by foliage while watching the water below.

Occurs in Florida year-round, but Palma Sola has only a few older records, so local frequency is not established.

How it lives

It usually hunts alone from a bank, root or low branch at the water's edge, waiting for small fish, insects, frogs and other prey to move within reach.

Fishing with bait

Green Herons sometimes place an insect, feather or other small object on the water. A fish approaching the object may move within striking range.

Pairs build stick nests in trees or shrubs near water, usually as isolated pairs or small groups rather than in the large mixed colonies many other herons favor.

What it does here

Cover is the hunting perch

Green Herons use plants as hunting cover as well as nesting structure. Low branches and dense shrubs let them wait directly above the water while remaining concealed, and pairs build stick nests in trees or shrubs near wetlands.
