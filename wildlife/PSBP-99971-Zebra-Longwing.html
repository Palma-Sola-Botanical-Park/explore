Zebra Longwing

Heliconius charithonia
On this page

At a glance

  • Florida's state butterfly: long, narrow black wings with pale yellow stripes.
  • It flies slowly and lazily, often through shade.
  • The caterpillars eat passionflower vines.
  • At night, they often sleep clustered together in small groups.

How to know it

Wings

Long and narrow, black with pale yellow stripes. The wingspan is about three to four inches.

Flight

Slow and relaxed compared with most butterflies, often through shaded gardens and woodland edges.

Where to find it here

Shade and flowers

Look in shaded gardens and woodland edges, around flowering plants and passionflower vines. It flies all year through much of central and southern Florida.

Dusk

Toward evening, check sheltered vegetation, where adults may be settling in for the night.

How it lives

Pollen

At a flower, a Zebra Longwing takes pollen as well as nectar, and digests nutrients from it. That is rare among butterflies, and it helps explain how an adult can live for several months. Most butterflies live two to four weeks.

Roosts

Adults often gather in small groups to spend the night together on a sheltered perch, then scatter to feed by day.

What it does here

Passionflower

Females lay their eggs on passionflower vines, and the caterpillars eat the leaves and new growth. Adults are less picky and visit many flowers for nectar and pollen. The Gulf Fritillary uses passionflower too.
