Zebra Longwing

Heliconius charithonia
On this page

At a glance

  • Florida's state butterfly is easy to recognize: long, narrow black wings crossed by pale yellow stripes.
  • Zebra Longwings feed on pollen as well as nectar, an unusual ability among butterflies. Nutrients from the pollen help adults live for several months rather than only a few weeks.
  • The caterpillars feed on passionflower vines and acquire chemical defenses associated with those plants. Both caterpillars and adults are distasteful to many predators, and the bold wing pattern serves as a warning.
  • At dusk, Zebra Longwings gather at communal roosts and may return to the same sheltered spot night after night.

How to know it

Wings

Long and narrow, black with pale yellow stripes.

Flight

Slow and floating compared with many other butterflies, often through shaded vegetation.

Size

Wingspan about three to four inches.

What to look for

Look for a black butterfly with narrow pale-yellow stripes and unusually long wings, often moving slowly through shaded gardens and woodland edges. Several may gather at a sheltered roost near dusk.

Easily confused with

Gulf Fritillary — Bright orange above with large silver-white spots underneath. Zebra Longwings are black with narrow pale-yellow stripes and much longer, narrower wings.

Where to find it here

Look in shaded gardens, woodland edges and hammock-like plantings, especially around passionflower vines and flowering plants. Near dusk, check sheltered vegetation for communal roosts.

A year-round resident through much of central and southern Florida.

How it lives

Eating pollen

At flowers, a Zebra Longwing gathers both nectar and pollen. It collects pollen on the proboscis and extracts nutrients from it, giving the adult access to protein and other nutrients that most butterflies do not obtain this way.

Females lay eggs on passionflower vines. The caterpillars feed on the new growth, linking the butterfly's entire reproductive cycle to Passiflora.

Toward evening, individuals gather at sheltered communal roosts.

What it does here

Adults visit many flowers for nectar and pollen and can carry pollen between blooms. Caterpillars are specialist herbivores of passionflower vines and become prey for predators able to tolerate their chemical defenses.

Passionflower is the whole cycle

Passionflower vines are essential host plants. Zebra Longwings lay their eggs on Passiflora, and the caterpillars eat the leaves and young growth. Adults are much less specialized and visit many flowers for nectar and pollen. A planting that combines passionflower with a succession of nectar- and pollen-producing blooms supports both stages of the butterfly's life.

Take care

Harmless to people. The caterpillars have spines but do not sting.

Watch without handling. Native passionflower vines and flowering plants provide more useful support than handling or feeding butterflies.

Conservation

Widespread in suitable habitat and not currently considered threatened.

Also known as

Zebra Heliconian · Florida Zebra Longwing
