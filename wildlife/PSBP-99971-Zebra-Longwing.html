Zebra Longwing

Heliconius charithonia
On this page

At a glance

  • The zebra longwing is Florida's official state butterfly, named in 1996 — long, narrow wings boldly striped in black and pale yellow.
  • It is one of the very few butterflies that eats pollen as well as nectar, and that extra protein lets it live for months rather than the usual few weeks.
  • Its caterpillars feed only on passionflower vines, taking in the plant's natural toxins so that the adult is distasteful to predators — the bold stripes are a warning.
  • At dusk, zebra longwings gather to sleep together, returning to the same communal roost night after night, sometimes dozens of butterflies in one spot.

How to know it

Wings

Long and narrow, black with pale-yellow stripes.

Flight

Slow, graceful, and floating, usually in shade.

Size

Medium-large, with that distinctive elongated wing shape.

Voice

Silent. (Curiously, the chrysalis can make a faint rasping sound if disturbed.)

Size

2.8 to 4 inches

What to look for

A black butterfly with thin yellow stripes drifting slowly through a shady garden or wood edge. The long, narrow wings and unhurried, floating flight are unmistakable. At dusk, look for several roosting together.

Where to find it here

Drifting slowly through shaded plantings and wood edges, especially near passionflower vines. At dusk, check sheltered spots for small groups roosting together.

Year-round resident through much of central and southern Florida, active in every month in warm areas.

How it lives

A slow, graceful flier that favors shade, and one of the few butterflies that gathers to sleep — the same communal roosts, used night after night.

It eats pollen, not just nectar

Adults take pollen as well as nectar, which is unusual for a butterfly and is why zebra longwings live for months rather than weeks.

Caterpillars eat only passionflower. Several generations a year.

What it does here

A pollinator with a twist — by feeding on pollen it moves it efficiently between flowers — and a passionflower specialist whose presence reflects the health of native vine habitat.

Caterpillars feed only on passionflower vines (Passiflora spp.) such as corkystem and purple passionflower — the same vines that support the gulf fritillary. Adults also gather pollen from many flowers.

Native to the Americas, reaching north into Florida and southern Texas. It is common throughout peninsular Florida, favoring shaded hammocks, woodland edges, and gardens — and it has been the state butterfly since 1996.

The zebra longwing is unusual on several counts. Most butterflies live only two to four weeks, but the zebra longwing can live several months. The secret is its diet: along with nectar, it gathers and digests pollen, a rare ability among butterflies that supplies extra protein and greatly extends its life.

Its whole life cycle runs through passionflower vines. The caterpillars eat only Passiflora, and in doing so absorb compounds that make the adult butterfly distasteful, even toxic, to predators — which is what the bold black-and-yellow stripes advertise.

It is also social in a way few butterflies are. At dusk, zebra longwings return to shared roosting spots, clustering together overnight for safety, sometimes in groups of dozens, then dispersing again to feed by day with their slow, drifting flight.
