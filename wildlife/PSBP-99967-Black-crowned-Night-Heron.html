Black-crowned Night Heron

Nycticorax nycticorax
On this page

At a glance

  • A stocky, hunch-shouldered heron with a black cap and back, pale gray wings and red eyes is a Black-crowned Night Heron.
  • It feeds mostly from late afternoon through the night, when most other herons have stopped.
  • It hunts by ambush, standing motionless at the water's edge and waiting rather than walking prey down.
  • Young birds are brown and heavily spotted, and are difficult to separate from young Yellow-crowned Night Herons.

How to know it

Adult

Black crown and back over pale gray wings, white underparts, red eyes and short yellow legs.

Build

Stocky and short-necked, with a hunched look that is obvious even at a distance.

Juvenile

Brown, heavily streaked and spotted with white, with orange-yellow eyes. Separating young Black-crowned and Yellow-crowned Night Herons can be genuinely difficult; build, bill shape and head pattern become more useful with a good view.

Voice

A harsh barking quok, usually given in flight and often heard at dusk as birds leave the roost to feed. It is frequently the first sign the bird is there at all.

Size

About two feet tall, with a wingspan around three and a half feet.

What to look for

Look low and still rather than out in the open. A compact hunched heron tucked against waterside shrubs near dawn or dusk is usually this bird.

Easily confused with

Yellow-crowned Night Heron — Adults are straightforward: Black-crowned has a black back over pale gray wings, while Yellow-crowned is gray-bodied with a strongly patterned black-and-white face. Juveniles of the two are much harder to separate and may need a close view.

Green Heron — Green Herons are much smaller with a chestnut neck and dark cap, and they crouch far lower over the water.

Where to find it here

Check pond margins and the shrubs overhanging them, especially in the low light of early morning and evening. By day the bird is usually roosting quietly in cover.

A year-round Florida resident, most likely to be seen in low light at either end of the day. Palma Sola has only a single record, so it is an occasional visitor here.

When

Occurs year-round in Florida. Palma Sola has too few records to establish a local pattern.

How it lives

It works the shift the other herons do not. Through the day it sits still in a tree or a shrub, easy to walk straight past; from late afternoon into the night it hunts.

And it hunts by ambush rather than stalking — standing motionless at the water's edge and waiting, rather than walking prey down. Fish, frogs, crayfish, aquatic insects, and the occasional egg or nestling.

What it does here

Waterside trees and shrubs are what make the night shift possible. The heron spends the day roosting in dense cover close to the water it will hunt after dark, and nests colonially in the same kind of vegetation. A pond with bare banks offers it nowhere to wait out the daylight.

Also known as

Night Heron · Black-capped Night Heron
