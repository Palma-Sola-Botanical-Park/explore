Black-crowned Night Heron

Nycticorax nycticorax

At a glance

  • A stocky, hunch-shouldered heron with a black cap and back, pale gray wings, and startling red eyes is a black-crowned night heron.
  • It works the late shift. While other herons fish by day, this one feeds mostly at dusk and after dark, sidestepping the competition.
  • The squat, motionless posture is a hunting strategy — it stands frozen at the water's edge and waits, then strikes fast at whatever swims past.
  • Young birds look like a different species entirely: streaky brown and white, with none of the adult's clean black-and-gray.

How to know it

Easily confused with

Yellow-crowned Night Heron — The yellow-crowned has a black-and-white face and creamy crown over an even gray body; the black-crowned has a black crown and back with pale gray wings.

Adult

Black crown and back, pale gray wings, white underparts, red eyes, and short yellow legs.

Build

Stocky and short-necked, with a hunched look.

Juvenile

Brown, heavily streaked and spotted with white, with orange-yellow eyes.

Plumes

A couple of long, thin white head plumes in breeding adults.

Voice

Usually quiet. Best known for a harsh, barking "quok" given in flight, often heard at dusk as birds head out to feed.

Size

23 to 26 inches; about 3.7 feet (45 inches)

What to look for

A short, thickset heron sitting hunched and still at the water's edge, especially near dusk. The black cap and back over gray wings mark an adult; a streaky brown version is a young bird, not a separate species.

Where to find it here

Around the pond margins, often tucked into waterside shrubs or standing hunched at the edge near dawn and dusk. Look low and still rather than out in the open.

Year-round resident, but easiest to see in the low light of early morning and evening when it comes out to feed.

When

Easiest at dawn and dusk.

How it lives

Most active from late afternoon through the night, roosting quietly in trees and shrubs by day. Hunts by standing still and ambushing prey rather than stalking. Often nests in colonies with other wading birds.

Fish, frogs, crayfish, aquatic insects, and small animals, plus the occasional egg or nestling — caught mostly at dusk and through the night.

What it does here

A nocturnal predator that helps balance fish, amphibian, and crayfish numbers, filling the night shift in the pond's web of wading birds.

Roosts and nests in waterside trees and shrubs by day — so the trees ringing a pond give it the cover it needs between feeding bouts.

Native and found on every continent except Antarctica — one of the most widespread herons in the world. In Florida it is a fairly common year-round resident of marshes, pond edges, and wooded swamps.

The black-crowned night heron makes its living by avoiding a crowd. Most herons and egrets fish through the day, so this bird shifts its main feeding to dusk, night, and early morning, when it has the shallows largely to itself. Its large red eyes are part of the package, gathering what little light there is.

Its hunting style is patience over speed. It hunches at the water's edge, neck drawn in, and stays still for long stretches before snapping its bill forward at a fish, frog, crayfish, or insect. It is not above raiding other birds' nests for eggs and chicks when the chance comes.

The brown, heavily streaked youngsters look so unlike their parents that they are often mistaken for a different bird. It takes a couple of years for them to grow into the crisp black cap, gray wings, and red eyes of an adult.

Take care

What to do

Watch from a distance and please do not feed it or any park wildlife. A roosting bird is resting before its night shift — give it space.
