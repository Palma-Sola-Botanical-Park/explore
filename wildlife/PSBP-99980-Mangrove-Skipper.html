Mangrove Skipper

Phocides batabano
On this page

At a glance

  • A large, dark skipper marked with bright blue or blue-purple streaks across the wings.
  • Its caterpillars feed on red mangrove, tying the butterfly closely to warm coastal mangrove habitat.
  • The adult is a powerful flier, but the caterpillar's world is one mangrove leaf: it folds the leaf with silk and feeds from inside the shelter.
  • Red mangrove provides both larval food and shelter; adult skippers leave the mangroves to visit nearby flowers for nectar.

How to know it

Wings

Large for a skipper, dark brown to blackish with bright blue or blue-purple streaks.

Head and body

Dark-bodied with pale markings on the face and blue markings on the body.

Size

Wingspan about two to three inches.

Flight

Fast and strong, often settling beneath leaves.

What to look for

Look near mangroves and nearby flowers for a large, fast dark skipper flashing bright blue streaks across the wings.

Easily confused with

Long-tailed Skipper — The long-tailed skipper is smaller with two long tails on the hindwings; the mangrove skipper is larger, tail-less, with bolder neon-blue streaks.

Where to find it here

Look around red mangroves and nearby flowering plants. Adults may leave the mangrove foliage to nectar, but eggs and caterpillars should be searched for on red mangrove leaves.

Southern Florida supports adults through much of the year. Palma Sola currently has too few records to define a reliable local season.

How it lives

Red mangrove is the larval host. A caterpillar folds part of a leaf into a silk-bound shelter and feeds on the surrounding leaf tissue.

Adults are less specialized, leaving the host plant to take nectar from flowers near the mangrove edge.

What it does here

One butterfly, one mangrove

Red mangrove is the larval host plant. Females lay eggs on the foliage, and the caterpillar eats the leaves while using them to build a silk-bound shelter. Adults feed at flowers nearby. The butterfly's distribution in Florida is therefore closely tied to mangrove habitat.

Take care

What to do

Leave folded or silk-bound mangrove leaves intact. One may hold a caterpillar.
