Mourning Dove

Zenaida macroura
On this page

At a glance

  • A slim, soft gray-brown dove with a long pointed tail, giving a low mournful coo, is a mourning dove — one of the most abundant birds in North America.
  • The sad-sounding call fools people into thinking they are hearing an owl. It is just the dove, and it is how the bird got its name.
  • Listen when it takes off: the wings make a sharp whistling twitter. The sound is mechanical, made by the feathers, not the voice.
  • It feeds almost entirely on seeds, picked off the ground, and can store a big load in its crop to digest later in a safe spot.

How to know it

Color

Soft gray-brown, with a few black spots on the wings and a pinkish wash on the breast.

Shape

Slim and streamlined, with a small head and a long, pointed tail edged in white.

Flight

Fast and direct, wings whistling on takeoff.

Behavior cue

Often seen feeding on the ground or perched on wires.

Voice

The signature low, mournful "coo-OO-oo-oo-oo," most often heard at dawn and dusk, plus the whistling twitter of the wings in flight.

Size

9 to 13 inches; about 18 inches

What to look for

A trim, plain dove with a long pointed tail, either walking and pecking on open ground or perched quietly. The mournful coo and the whistling wing-twitter on takeoff confirm it.

Easily confused with

Eurasian Collared-Dove — The collared-dove is larger and paler with a square tail and a black half-collar on the neck; the mourning dove is smaller, browner, and long-tailed with no collar.

Where to find it here

On open lawns and paths, on wires and bare branches, and around any seeding plants. Listen for the mournful coo to locate one.

Year-round resident, present and calling in every season.

How it lives

It feeds on the ground and rests on wires, branches and rooftops, usually in pairs or loose flocks. Startle one and it goes up fast with a sharp whistling of wings — that sound is the feathers, not the bird — then settles again nearby.

Seeds almost exclusively. It fills its crop on the ground and digests later, somewhere safer.

What it does here

An abundant seed-eater that helps move and process plant seeds, and a key prey species for hawks, falcons, and other predators.

Feeds on the seeds of many grasses and weedy plants, gleaned from open and disturbed ground.

Native and abundant across North America. In Florida it is a common year-round resident of open country, farmland, suburbs, and parks.

Few birds are as widespread or as numerous as the mourning dove; it is found coast to coast and is among the most plentiful birds on the continent. Much of that success comes from a simple diet of seeds and a long breeding season that lets pairs raise several broods a year.

The call does most of the work of naming it. The slow, drawn-out "coo-OO-oo-oo-oo" carries a melancholy tone that people regularly mistake for an owl, especially at dawn and dusk.

The whistling wings are a separate trick. The narrow outer wing feathers vibrate in flight, producing a sharp twitter on takeoff and landing that may also serve as an alarm, alerting the flock when one bird flushes in a hurry.

Take care

What to do

Watch and enjoy, and please do not feed park wildlife. Doves find plenty of seed on their own.
