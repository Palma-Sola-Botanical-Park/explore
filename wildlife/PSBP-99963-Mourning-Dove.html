Mourning Dove

Zenaida macroura
On this page

At a glance

  • A Mourning Dove is a slim gray-brown dove with black wing spots, a small head and a long pointed tail edged with white.
  • Its low coo is often mistaken for an owl. The mournful quality of that call gave the bird its English name.
  • The sharp whistle heard when a Mourning Dove takes off comes from the wings, not the voice.
  • Both parents feed newly hatched young with crop milk, a fat- and protein-rich secretion produced in the crop.

How to know it

Color

Gray-brown to buff, with black spots on the wings and a pale peach wash on the breast.

Shape

Small head, plump body and long pointed tail with white outer tips.

Flight

Fast and direct, often with a sharp wing whistle at takeoff.

Voice

A low, drawn-out series of coos. The sharp whistling sound heard during takeoff and landing is made by the wings rather than the voice.

Size

About a foot long, with a wingspan around a foot and a half.

What to look for

Look on lawns, paths and other open ground for a slim brown dove picking up seeds. On a perch, the small head and long pointed tail are distinctive.

Wing whistle

The whistling comes from the wings rather than the voice. Narrow outer flight feathers vibrate on takeoff and landing, and the sharp twitter may double as an alarm when one bird flushes in a hurry.

Easily confused with

Eurasian Collared-Dove — Larger and paler, with a squared tail and a narrow black collar on the back of the neck. Mourning Doves have a long pointed tail and black spots on the wings.

White-winged Dove — Larger and heavier-bodied, with a bold white wing patch visible even when perched. Mourning Doves are slimmer and lack the broad white wing stripe.

Where to find it here

Look on lawns, paths and other open ground with scattered trees or shrubs nearby. Mourning Doves also perch conspicuously on bare branches, wires and other exposed sites.

A year-round Florida resident. Palma Sola's formal observation record is currently too small to measure how regularly it is seen in the park.

How it lives

Seeds make up nearly the entire diet. A feeding bird swallows them whole and stores them temporarily in its crop, then can leave the exposed feeding area and digest the meal from a safer perch.

Birds often occur in pairs or loose groups and fly away rapidly when startled.

A very simple nest

Mourning Doves build very simple nests from twigs, pine needles and grass stems, usually on a branch or other supported surface. Both parents care for the young.

What it does here

A major consumer of grass and weed seeds and an important prey species for hawks, falcons and other predators.

Seeds and nest material

Plants supply both food and nest material. Mourning Doves gather seeds from grasses, weeds and other low plants on open ground, then place simple nests in trees, shrubs or vines, building the platform from twigs, pine needles and grass stems.

Take care

No meaningful danger to people.

Watch without feeding. Give ground-feeding birds room to move away naturally rather than flushing them repeatedly.

Conservation

Widespread and currently of low conservation concern.
