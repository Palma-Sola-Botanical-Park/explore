Black-and-white Warbler

Mniotilta varia
On this page

At a glance

  • Unlike most warblers, Black-and-white Warblers forage directly on tree trunks and large branches, moving up, down and sideways while searching bark for insects.
  • Its feet are well suited to gripping bark, including a long hind toe and claw that help it cling while moving over trunks and branches.
  • The song is a thin repeated wee-see, wee-see, wee-see, often compared to a squeaky wheel.
  • Despite spending so much time on trunks and large limbs, Black-and-white Warblers build their nests on the ground beside roots, logs or rocks.

How to know it

Summary

A small black-and-white striped warbler with a slightly downcurved bill. Its habit of creeping directly over tree trunks and large limbs is often the easiest way to recognize it.

Male and female

Adult males have a black throat and darker cheek. Females and immature birds are paler, with a whitish or buff throat.

Possible confusion

Other black-and-white warblers work among leaves and twigs. Behavior separates them more reliably than plumage does.

Voice

The song is a very high series of repeated wee-see phrases, often compared to a squeaky wheel. Calls are short, sharp notes.

Size

About five inches long, with a wingspan around eight inches.

What to look for

Scan trunks and heavy branches rather than just the leaves. Look for a small striped bird creeping directly over the bark, sometimes moving downward or sideways as well as upward.

Easily confused with

Blackpoll Warbler — Blackpoll Warblers can also be strongly black and white in breeding plumage, but they forage through foliage rather than creeping directly over trunks. Black-and-white Warblers are usually recognized first by behavior.

Where to find it here

Check trunks and large branches of mature trees. The bird often forages much lower than other warblers, so scan at eye level as well as overhead.

A migrant and winter visitor in Florida. Palma Sola has only a few records, so local peak timing is not yet established.

When

Occurs in Florida during migration and winter. Palma Sola records are too limited to define a local peak.

How it lives

Bark holds prey that leaf-gleaning birds never reach — insect eggs and larvae wedged in crevices, spiders under loose flakes. It works a trunk methodically, probing as it goes and often circling to the far side out of view.

It eats insects, spiders and other small arthropods taken from bark and crevices.

A nest on the ground

During the breeding season farther north, the nest is placed on the ground, often beside a tree, stump, rock or fallen log.

What it does here

Mature trees are the feeding surface. Black-and-white Warblers search rough bark, trunk crevices and large limbs for insects and spiders. Farther north on the breeding grounds, fallen leaves, roots, logs and other material on the forest floor help conceal the ground nest.
