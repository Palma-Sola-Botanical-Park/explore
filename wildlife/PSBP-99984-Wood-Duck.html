Wood Duck

Aix sponsa
On this page

At a glance

  • Most waterfowl nest on the ground. Wood Ducks nest high inside tree cavities, often old woodpecker holes.
  • They have strong claws and perch readily in trees, which almost no other duck here does.
  • The male is one of the most strongly patterned ducks in North America; the female is gray-brown with a white teardrop around the eye.
  • Acorns are a favourite food in autumn and winter, which ties this duck to oaks as much as to water.

How to know it

Male

Boldly patterned — a glossy green and chestnut head with white stripes, a swept-back crest and red eyes.

Female

Soft gray-brown with a distinctive white teardrop around the eye.

Size

About twenty inches long, with a wingspan around two and a half feet.

Voice

The female gives a loud rising oo-eek squeal when flushed; the male a softer, thin whistle.

What to look for

Check shaded, sheltered water rather than open ponds, and look up — a duck perched on a branch is almost certainly this one.

Where to find it here

On quieter, shadier stretches of the ponds and wooded edges, often in pairs near overhanging cover rather than out in open water.

A year-round resident, with more birds present in winter.

How it lives

Active by day, usually in pairs or small groups and often close to cover at the edge of shaded water.

Nesting off the ground

It nests in tree cavities, frequently old woodpecker holes, and will readily use a nest box. Ducklings leave by jumping down within a day of hatching, before they can fly.

Seeds, acorns, fruits and aquatic plants make up most of the diet, with insects and other small invertebrates added.

What it does here

A forager on wetland and bottomland plants and prey for larger predators.

It needs trees as much as water

Acorns are a major autumn and winter food, and the nest is a cavity in a living or dead tree beside the water. A pond with no trees around it supports no Wood Ducks, however good the water is — which is why nest boxes worked so well where the old trees had gone.

Take care

No meaningful danger to people.

Watch from a distance and do not feed it. Wood Ducks are shy and stick to cover, and bread harms waterfowl.

Conservation

Not currently threatened, and a notable recovery from near-collapse in the early 1900s through hunting limits, habitat protection and nest-box programs.
