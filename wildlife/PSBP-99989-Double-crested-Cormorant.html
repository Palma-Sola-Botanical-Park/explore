Double-crested Cormorant

Nannopterum auritum
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it doesPhotographs

At a glance

  • A dark, heavy-bodied waterbird that dives under and chases fish.
  • Big webbed feet do most of the pushing underwater, and the hooked bill grips the catch.
  • Orange-yellow skin shows at the base of the bill.
  • It often perches with its wings held open, probably to dry them.

How to know it

Dark, with a hook

Dark brown to blackish, with orange-yellow bare skin around the base of a long, narrow bill that has a hook at the tip. It swims low in the water, with a short tail.

Anhinga or cormorant?

An Anhinga has a straight, pointed bill and a longer tail. The hooked bill and short tail say cormorant.

Young birds

Young cormorants are paler on the breast.

Where to find it here

Vanishing on the pond

Scan open pond water for a dark bird that keeps disappearing, then check snags, banks and other exposed perches for one resting with its wings held open. It is in Florida all year, and sightings here are occasional.

How it lives

A pursuit hunter

A cormorant chases fish underwater rather than waiting for them. Its large webbed feet provide most of the push, and the hooked bill grips the catch.

Wings open

Afterward it often perches with its wings spread. Cormorant feathers take up some water, and the open wings are a likely way to dry them.

What it does

The sequence to watch

A dark bird swims low, goes under, and comes up somewhere else. Later it may be on a snag with its wings held open.

The crests

The two small tufts of the name appear on adults only in the breeding season. Most of the year there is no crest to see.
