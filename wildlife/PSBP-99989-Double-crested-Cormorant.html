Double-crested Cormorant

Nannopterum auritum
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it does herePhotographs

At a glance

  • A Double-crested Cormorant is a dark, heavy-bodied waterbird that dives completely underwater after fish.
  • It chases fish underwater using its large webbed feet for propulsion, rather than spearing prey from ambush like an Anhinga.
  • After diving, cormorants often perch with their wings spread. Their feathers become waterlogged more readily than those of most waterbirds, which can aid underwater diving.
  • Cormorants have a hooked bill and shorter tail; Anhingas have a straight daggerlike bill and long fan-shaped tail.

How to know it

Color

Dark brown to blackish, with orange-yellow bare skin around the base of the bill.

Bill

Long and narrow with a noticeable hook at the tip.

In the water

Swims low and repeatedly disappears underwater during feeding dives.

Breeding crest

Adults briefly grow two small tufts on the head during breeding season; they are absent or difficult to see most of the year.

Size

Wingspan about four feet.

What to look for

Look for a dark waterbird repeatedly diving in open water or perched with its wings spread. The hook-tipped bill, orange facial skin and relatively short tail separate it from an Anhinga.

Easily confused with

Anhinga — Anhingas are slimmer, with longer fan-shaped tails and straight daggerlike bills. Double-crested Cormorants are heavier-bodied, with shorter tails and bills that hook at the tip.

Where to find it here

Scan open pond water for diving birds, then check snags, banks and other exposed perches for cormorants resting or drying their wings.

Double-crested Cormorants occur in Florida year-round. Palma Sola has only a few records, so sightings here should be considered occasional.

When

Year-round Florida resident. Palma Sola currently has too few records to define a local seasonal pattern.

How it lives

Cormorants pursue fish underwater rather than waiting for them. Their large webbed feet provide most of the propulsion, and the hooked bill grips the catch.

Because the plumage becomes waterlogged, a bird often rests afterward on an exposed perch with its wings spread while the feathers dry.

What it does here

A pursuit predator of fish in both freshwater and coastal habitats.

Cormorants nest colonially in trees, shrubs, on the ground or on human structures. In large tree colonies, accumulated droppings can damage or eventually kill nesting trees, changing the vegetation beneath and around the colony. At Palma Sola the species is an occasional visitor rather than a known nesting colony.

Native and widespread across North America. In Florida it occurs on ponds, lakes, rivers, estuaries and coastal waters throughout the year.
