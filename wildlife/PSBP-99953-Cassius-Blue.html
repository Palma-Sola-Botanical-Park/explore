Cassius Blue

Leptotes cassius
On this page

At a glance

  • A tiny pale-blue butterfly barely bigger than a thumbnail, flickering low over flower beds.
  • The underside is white with fine gray bands and small black-and-orange eyespots beside slender tails — together they suggest a head at the wrong end of the butterfly.
  • Males shine pale blue above; females are duller, with more dusky gray and brown.
  • Plumbago is one of its caterpillar host plants, which keeps this butterfly close to garden plantings.

How to know it

Size

Barely larger than a thumbnail — a wingspan of about an inch.

Upperside

Males pale blue; females duller blue-gray with brown.

Underside

White to pale gray with fine gray bands and one or two small black-and-orange eyespots near the tail.

What to look for

Look low and look small. A pale flicker of blue close to the flowers, rather than anything crossing the path at head height.

Where to find it here

Low around the flower beds and any plumbago plantings, fluttering close to the blooms.

Present year-round in Florida's warm climate, and most numerous during the warmer months.

How it lives

Active by day, with a low, fluttery flight close to the ground and flowers. Adults sip nectar from small blooms.

A false head

At rest it holds its wings closed and slowly slides the hindwings against each other, twitching the little tails and eyespots. The eyespots, tails and their movement create a false head at the rear of the butterfly, which can misdirect a strike away from the real one.

What it does here

Garden plants as host plants

The caterpillars feed on the flowers and buds of legumes and on plumbago. Both are ordinary garden plants, which is why this butterfly stays close to cultivated beds rather than needing wild ground.
