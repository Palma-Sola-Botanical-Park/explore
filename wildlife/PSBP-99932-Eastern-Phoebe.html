Eastern Phoebe
Sayornis phoebe Tyrannidae
📷 Photo by Rob Carr · CC-BY-NC · via iNaturalist · 📅 Nov 14, 2025
⚡Quick Hits
  • A plain, grayish-brown bird that sits upright on an exposed perch, wags its tail, and darts out to catch a flying insect — that is an eastern phoebe.
  • The tail-wagging is almost constant and is the quickest way to recognize it — few other small birds pump their tail so persistently.
  • It calls its own name: a burry, two-note 'fee-bee' that it repeats from a favorite perch.
  • A winter visitor to Florida, it breeds farther north and returns each fall.
🔎How to Spot It
Shape

Upright posture, medium-sized flycatcher. Plain grayish-brown above, pale below.

Tail

Wags persistently — the best field mark.

Bill

Small and dark — a flycatcher bill.

Voice

A burry 'fee-bee' repeated from a perch.

Voice

A burry, emphatic 'fee-bee' — says its name.

What to Look For

A plain gray-brown bird on an exposed perch, wagging its tail and darting out after insects.

🍽️What It Eats

Flying insects — caught by sallying from a perch. Some berries in winter.

📍Where & When
Where in the park

Exposed perches near open ground — fences, low branches, building edges. Watch for the tail-wag.

When to see it

Winter visitor — typically October through March.

🤝Watching It Respectfully

Observe and enjoy.
