Gulf Fritillary

Dione vanillae
On this page

At a glance

  • A bright orange butterfly about as wide as your palm, usually in a sunny, flowery spot.
  • It often feeds with its wings closed, so the silver underside is the view you usually get.
  • The caterpillar is bright orange with rows of black, branching spines, and it eats passionflower leaves.
  • In autumn, some head south down the Florida peninsula.

How to know it

Orange above

Bright orange with black spots and markings, and long, narrow wings. The wingspan runs from about two to nearly three inches.

Silver below

The underside is brown with large, elongated silver-white spots that can look almost metallic when they catch the light.

Where to find it here

Sunny and flowery

Look in sunny, flowery spots. If you find a passionflower vine, check the leaves for the orange caterpillars.

All year

It flies all year in Florida, and more are on the wing in autumn.

How it lives

Feeding with wings closed

It is active by day and often sips nectar with its wings held shut, which is why the silver underside is the view most visitors get.

The caterpillar

Bright orange with rows of black, branching spines, it eats passionflower leaves.

Autumn

Several generations follow one another through the year. In autumn some adults move south down the Florida peninsula.

What it does here

A plant it needs

Adults visit many kinds of flowers, but the caterpillars need passionflower. They share it with the Zebra Longwing, which also lays its eggs on passionflower vines. Look for one around a passionflower and you may find the other.
