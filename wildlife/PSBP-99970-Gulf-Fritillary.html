Gulf Fritillary

Dione vanillae
On this page

At a glance

  • A bright orange butterfly with black markings above and an underside scattered with elongated, mirror-like silver spots.
  • Its caterpillars eat only passionflower vines — the same host the Zebra Longwing depends on, so one native passionvine supports both butterflies.
  • The caterpillar is bright orange with rows of black branching spines. The spines look alarming but do not sting.
  • Despite the name it belongs to the longwing group rather than the true fritillaries.

How to know it

Upperside

Bright orange with black spots and markings.

Underside

Brown with elongated, iridescent silver-white spots — visible when the butterfly nectars with its wings closed.

Shape

Somewhat long-winged, like its longwing relatives.

Size

Wingspan around three inches.

What to look for

A bright orange butterfly in a sunny, flowery spot. If it settles with wings closed, look for the silver flecks underneath.

Easily confused with

Monarch — The monarch is broader-winged with heavy black veins and no silver; the gulf fritillary is narrower with bright silver spots on the underside.

Where to find it here

Look in sunny, flowery spots, and check any passionflower vine for the spiny orange caterpillars.

A year-round Florida resident, with numbers rising in autumn as butterflies move south down the peninsula.

How it lives

Active by day in sunny, open areas, often nectaring with the wings held closed so the silver underside shows.

Several generations are produced each year, and adults join a southward movement into the Florida peninsula in autumn.

What it does here

A common garden pollinator, visiting lantana, Spanish needles and other open blooms.

One vine, two butterflies

The caterpillars eat nothing but passionflower vines. The Zebra Longwing uses the same host, so a single native passionvine planting supports both species — one of the highest-value butterfly plants a Florida garden can grow.

Take care

Harmless to people. The caterpillar's branching spines look intimidating but do not sting.

Watch without handling. Native passionflower vine is the surest way to keep both this butterfly and the Zebra Longwing around.

Conservation

Common and widespread, and a frequent visitor to butterfly gardens.
