Tropical Checkered-Skipper

Burnsius oileus
On this page
At a glanceHow to know itWhere to find it hereHow it livesWhat it does hereTake carePhotographs

At a glance

  • A small, fast butterfly checkered in gray, white and dark charcoal, darting low over open ground and landing flat to bask.
  • Males carry a dusting of blue-gray hairs on the body and wing bases that gives them a frosty look.
  • Its caterpillars feed on mallows — fanpetals and wireweed — plants that grow as common weeds in open ground.
  • Skippers hold their wings differently from other butterflies, often basking with them spread flat against the ground.

How to know it

Pattern

Bold checkered gray, white and dark markings across the wings.

Male

Frosty blue-gray hair on the body and wing bases.

Size

Small — a wingspan of an inch to an inch and a half.

Flight

Fast, low and darting, typical of skippers.

What to look for

Watch bare or weedy sunlit ground for a small checkered butterfly that lands flat and holds still, then vanishes in a fast low dash.

Where to find it here

Open, sunny, weedy areas and garden edges. Watch for the fast, low flight rather than the colors.

Present year-round in southern Florida and most numerous in the warm months.

How it lives

Fast, darting flight close to the ground. Males patrol low over open ground, and adults bask with the wings spread flat.

Adults sip nectar from small low flowers, including frogfruit and Spanish needles.

What it does here

A pollinator of low flowers along paths and open ground.

Weeds are the host plants

The caterpillars eat mallow-family plants — fanpetals and wireweed — which most gardens treat as weeds. A patch of scruffy open ground left alone is what keeps this butterfly present.

Take care

Harmless to people.

Watch without handling. Leaving some low weedy ground unmown is the most useful thing for this species.

Conservation

Common and widespread.
