Tropical Checkered-Skipper

At a glance

  • A small, fast butterfly checkered in gray, white, and dark charcoal — darting low over open ground and landing flat to bask in the sun.
  • Males develop a dusting of blue-gray hairs on the body and wing bases that gives them a frosty, cool-toned look.
  • It is a spread-winged skipper (subfamily Pyrginae) — the 'fighter jet' family of butterflies. While grass skippers hold their wings angled up like a tent at rest, spread-winged skippers bask with wings laid completely flat.
  • Common in sunny, weedy, open areas — exactly the kind of wild edges a botanical park preserves.

How to know it

Pattern

Bold checkered gray, white, and dark pattern on the wings.

Male

Frosty blue-gray hair on the body and wing bases.

Flight

Fast, low, darting — typical skipper.

Posture

Basks with wings spread completely flat — the signature of a spread-winged skipper (Pyrginae), not a grass skipper.

Voice

Silent.

Size

wingspan about 1 to 1.5 inches

What to look for

A small, fast, checkered gray-and-white butterfly basking flat on the ground or low vegetation in an open, sunny, weedy area.

Where to find it here

Open, sunny, weedy areas and garden edges. Watch for the fast, low flight.

Year-round in southern Florida, most numerous in warm months.

How it lives

Fast, darting flight close to the ground. Basks with wings open. Males patrol low over open ground.

Caterpillars feed on plants in the mallow family (Malvaceae) — especially fanpetals and wireweed (Sida rhombifolia and Sida acuta), which grow as common 'weeds' in open ground. Adults sip nectar from small flowers, particularly Sida species, Frogfruit (Phyla nodiflora), and Spanish Needles (Bidens alba).

What it does here

Pollinator of low flowers including Frogfruit and Spanish Needles; caterpillars feed on mallow-family plants (Sida species and relatives), making roadside 'weeds' an essential part of its life cycle.

Caterpillar host plants are mallows (Malvaceae). Adults nectar at many low flowers.

Native to the southeastern U.S., Caribbean, and tropical Americas. Year-round resident in southern Florida.

The tropical checkered-skipper is one of the more recognizable skippers thanks to its bold checkered wing pattern of gray, white, and dark marks. Males get an additional dusting of blue-gray hairs that gives them a distinctive frosty appearance.

It belongs to the spread-winged skippers (subfamily Pyrginae). When basking, these butterflies lay their wings completely flat — a quick field clue that instantly separates them from grass skippers, which angle their wings upward like a tent.

It is a butterfly of disturbed, open ground — roadsides, gardens, weedy lots, and park edges — where its caterpillar host plants grow. Look for fanpetals and wireweed (Sida species) in sunny weedy patches; what looks like a nuisance weed is a nursery for this skipper.
