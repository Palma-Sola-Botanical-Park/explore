Yellow-rumped Warbler

Setophaga coronata
On this page

At a glance

  • Yellow-rumped Warblers can digest the waxy fruits of bayberries and wax myrtles, foods many other birds cannot use efficiently. That extra winter food helps them remain farther north than most other warblers.
  • Unlike many warblers that search mainly for insects in foliage, Yellow-rumped Warblers also catch insects in flight, forage on the ground and switch heavily to fruit during winter.
  • The birds seen in Florida are usually the white-throated 'Myrtle' form. Farther west, the 'Audubon's' form has a yellow throat.
  • The birds at Palma Sola are winter visitors. They breed far to the north and leave Florida again in spring.

How to know it

Size

A sturdy warbler about five and a half inches long, with a fairly large head and a narrow tail.

Winter plumage

Winter birds are gray-brown above and pale below, with streaking on the breast and sides. Look for the bright yellow rump, small yellow patches along the sides, a white throat and two pale wing bars.

Spring males

By spring, males can become much more strongly patterned in gray, black and white, while retaining the yellow rump and side patches.

Similar species

Palm Warblers are also common Florida winter visitors but usually forage lower and repeatedly bob or wag the tail.

Voice

The most useful sound in Florida is a short, flat check call, often given while the bird is foraging or flying. The male's breeding song is a soft series of whistled notes, but visitors here are much more likely to hear the call.

What to look for

Look first for the yellow rump, often easiest to see when the bird flies. Winter birds also show a white throat, yellow patches along the sides and two pale wing bars. Listen for a short, flat check call from shrubs and trees.

Easily confused with

Palm Warbler — Palm Warblers are also common Florida winter visitors but usually forage lower and repeatedly bob or wag the tail. Yellow-rumped Warblers show the bright yellow rump that gives the species its name.

Where to find it here

Check fruiting wax myrtles and other shrubs, especially during winter. Birds also forage through tree canopies and along woodland edges, sometimes flying out from a branch to catch an insect. Listen for the short check call when a flock is moving through.

A winter visitor to Florida, generally present from fall into spring. Palma Sola records are still too few to define a local peak.

When

Winter visitor to Florida. Recorded at Palma Sola, but local observations are too few to establish how regularly it occurs in the park.

How it lives

Yellow-rumped Warblers forage at several heights, searching foliage and bark, catching insects in short flights and sometimes feeding on the ground. They migrate at night.

Their diet changes with the season. Insects dominate during the breeding season, while fruit becomes especially important in winter.

What it does here

Wax myrtle is especially important winter food. Yellow-rumped Warblers can digest its wax-coated fruits, helping them survive when insects are scarce. They also eat fruits from poison ivy, grapes and other woody plants, making fruiting shrubs and vines important winter habitat.
