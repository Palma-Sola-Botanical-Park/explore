Florida Red-bellied Cooter

Pseudemys nelsoni
On this page

At a glance

  • A large, dark basking turtle named for the reddish wash on its shell and underside.
  • Females sometimes lay their eggs inside an alligator's nest mound, where the guarding gator shelters them without meaning to.
  • Adults are almost entirely vegetarian, grazing on underwater plants.
  • Told from the park's other big turtles by the red arrow between the eyes and the reddish bars along the shell.

How to know it

Shell

High-domed and dark, with reddish vertical bars on the side plates.

Underside

Washed with orange to red.

Head

Dark with yellow stripes, including an arrow-shaped mark between the eyes.

Size

Large, with females bigger than males.

From above, look for the reddish bars on the shell and the arrow-shaped mark on the head. The reddish underside is useful when you can see it, but a basking turtle often will not show it.

Easily confused with

Peninsular Cooter — The peninsular cooter has yellow hairpin-loop stripes on the head; the red-bellied cooter has a reddish belly and a red arrow mark between the eyes.

Pond Slider — Sliders are smaller with a colored ear patch behind the eye; the red-bellied cooter is larger and lacks it.

Where to find it here

Basking on logs and banks at the ponds on sunny days, usually among sliders and peninsular cooters. Look for the reddish tones in the shell.

A year-round resident, most visible when the sun is out.

How it lives

Day after day it alternates basking on a log or bank with diving to feed, and it is wary — approach and it is gone into the water.

Adults graze on submerged plants and algae; young turtles take some insects and small animals as well.

What it does here

Adults graze underwater plants and algae, which is unusual for a pond turtle and makes this one of the pond's plant-grazers. Young turtles take insects and other small animals before shifting over to a plant diet.

Alligator nest mounds

Females sometimes dig their nests straight into the mounds built by American Alligators. The mound is warm, and a female alligator guarding her own eggs may incidentally discourage predators that would otherwise dig up the turtle eggs alongside them.

Take care

What to do

Watch from a distance and do not handle or feed them. A basking turtle driven off its log has lost the sun time it came for, and like any turtle it may nip if picked up.
