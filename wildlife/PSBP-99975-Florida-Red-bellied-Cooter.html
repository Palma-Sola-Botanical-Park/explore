At a glance

  • The Florida red-bellied cooter is a large, dark basking turtle named for the reddish wash on its underside and shell.
  • It is a Florida native, at home in the state's ponds, lakes, and slow rivers.
  • Adults are mostly vegetarian, grazing on underwater plants — turtles that essentially tend the pond's vegetable garden.
  • It has an unusual nesting trick: females sometimes lay their eggs right in the warm nest mounds of alligators, where the guarding gator unintentionally protects them.

How to know it

Easily confused with

Peninsular Cooter — The peninsular cooter has yellow hairpin-loop stripes on the head; the red-bellied cooter has a reddish belly and a red arrow mark between the eyes.

Pond Slider — Sliders are smaller with a colored ear patch behind the eye; the red-bellied cooter is larger and lacks it.

Shell

High-domed and dark, with reddish vertical bars on the side scutes.

Underside (plastron)

Washed with orange to reddish color.

Head

Dark with yellow stripes, including an arrow-shaped stripe between the eyes.

Size

Large, with females bigger than males.

Voice

Essentially silent.

What to look for

A big, dark, high-domed turtle basking at the pond, showing reddish bars on the shell. The red-tinted underside and the arrow-shaped head stripe help separate it from the similar sliders and peninsular cooter.

Where to find it here

Basking on logs and banks at the ponds on sunny days, often among sliders and peninsular cooters. Look for the reddish tones on the shell.

Year-round resident. Most visible basking on warm, sunny days.

When

Most visible basking on warm, sunny days.

How it lives

Active by day. Alternates basking on logs and banks with feeding dives. Wary, sliding into the water when approached. Nests on land, sometimes within alligator nest mounds.

Adults are mostly herbivorous, grazing on submerged aquatic plants and algae; young turtles also take some insects and other small animals.

What it does here

A plant-grazing turtle that helps shape underwater vegetation, with eggs and young that feed many predators — and an unusual nesting link to alligators.

Adults graze largely on submerged aquatic plants and algae, making this cooter one of the pond's natural plant-grazers.

Native to Florida and a bit of southern Georgia. It is one of the common large freshwater turtles across the Florida peninsula.

The red-bellied cooter is one of the big, dark turtles hauled out to bask alongside sliders and other cooters on sunny days, told apart by the reddish tones on its shell and the red wash on its underside (the plastron).

Like its cousin the peninsular cooter, the adult is largely a plant-eater, grazing submerged vegetation — a useful role in keeping pond plant growth in balance.

Its most surprising habit is where it nests. Females will sometimes dig their nests directly into the mounded nests of American alligators. The female gator, fiercely guarding her own eggs, ends up shielding the turtle eggs too — an accidental partnership that gives the cooter's eggs protection they would not have on an open bank.

Take care

What to do

Watch from a distance and do not handle or feed it. Let basking turtles keep their sunning spots.
