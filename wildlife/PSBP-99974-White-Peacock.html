White Peacock

Anartia jatrophae

At a glance

  • A pale butterfly that stays low, close to the ground, over damp open ground near water.
  • The wings are chalky white to tan, with scalloped orange-trimmed edges and small dark eyespots.
  • The caterpillars eat frogfruit and water hyssop, two low, mat-forming plants easy to mistake for weeds. Both grow in the park.
  • A male picks a patch of ground about fifty feet across, chases other butterflies out of it, and returns to the same perch. That was measured in a study in southwest Florida.

How to know it

Wings

Chalky white to pale tan with faint brown markings, scalloped edges trimmed in orange, and small dark eyespots. The wingspan is about two inches.

Look down

It flies low and close to the vegetation and rarely rises far above it. A pale butterfly working just over damp ground is worth a second look.

Where to find it here

Damp open ground

Look where the ground is wet and open, around pond edges, ditches and low, sunny groundcover.

Year-round

Florida has it all year, in several generations, and it is most numerous in the warm months.

How it lives

A patch to guard

A male picks a small patch, perches in it, and chases off other males and other insects that wander in. In a study near Ochopee, in southwest Florida, marked males kept to areas under fifty feet across and came back to their old perches after every chase.

What it does here

Caterpillar plants

The caterpillars eat water hyssop, which we call Herb-of-Grace, and frogfruit. These are creeping native plants that spread along wet margins and are easy to mistake for weeds, and both grow in the park.

Why it stays low

An adult can sip nectar from many flowers, but its caterpillars can eat only a few plants. So the butterfly stays near the low, damp ground where those plants grow. That is why this one is hard to spot from standing height.
