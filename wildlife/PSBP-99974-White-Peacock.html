White Peacock

Anartia jatrophae
On this page

At a glance

  • A pale, chalky-white butterfly with faint tan markings, dark eyespots and scalloped, orange-edged wings.
  • It stays low, flying just above the ground in damp, open places like pond edges, ditches and wet fields.
  • Its caterpillars feed on low native groundcovers — frogfruit and water hyssop — the same plants that carpet moist pond margins.
  • Males defend small territories around the host plants, patrolling a patch of damp ground.

How to know it

Color

Chalky white with faint light-brown markings.

Wings

Scalloped edges trimmed in orange, with small dark eyespots.

Flight

Low and close to the ground, rarely rising far above the vegetation.

Size

Wingspan around two to two and a half inches.

What to look for

A pale, whitish butterfly working low over damp open ground near water. Look down rather than up.

Where to find it here

Flying low around the moist edges of the ponds and in damp open ground, especially near frogfruit and other low groundcovers.

Present year-round in Florida and most numerous in the warm months.

How it lives

Active by day, flying low to the ground. Males defend small territories around host plants, and several generations follow one another through the warm months.

Adults sip nectar from low-growing flowers such as Spanish needles and frogfruit.

What it does here

A pollinator of low, moist-ground flowers.

Groundcover is the habitat

The caterpillars eat frogfruit and water hyssop — creeping native plants easy to mistake for weeds. Where those groundcovers are left to spread along a damp margin, this butterfly follows; where the same ground is mown short or cleared, it does not.

Take care

Harmless to people.

Watch without handling. Low plantings of native frogfruit and water hyssop near water are the best way to invite them in.

Conservation

Common and secure in Florida, though always tied to the moist, low habitats it needs.
