Green Anole

Anolis carolinensis
On this page

At a glance

  • The Green Anole is Florida's only native anole. It can change from bright green to brown, and adult males display a pink throat fan called a dewlap.
  • Color change is influenced by temperature, light and the lizard's physiological state. It is not the rapid background-matching trick associated with chameleons.
  • Where introduced Brown Anoles become abundant, Green Anoles often shift toward higher perches in shrubs and trees, reducing overlap between the two species.
  • Its expanded toe pads are covered with microscopic structures that help it cling to leaves, stems and smooth surfaces while climbing.

How to know it

Color

Bright green to brown, sometimes changing between the two. It is the only anole at Palma Sola that turns true green.

Throat fan

Adult males have a pink to reddish dewlap that they extend during displays.

Build

Slender, with a pointed snout and long tail. Brown Anoles are usually stockier and more heavily patterned.

Size

Usually about five to eight inches from snout to tail tip.

What to look for

Look on shrubs, vines and tree trunks for a slender lizard that may be bright green or brown. Adult males sometimes perform head bobs and push-ups while extending a pink dewlap.

Easily confused with

Brown Anole — Brown Anoles remain brown, tan or gray and are usually stockier and more heavily patterned. Green Anoles can turn bright green and are more slender. Where both occur, Green Anoles also tend to use higher vegetation.

Where to find it here

Look on shrubs, vines, tree trunks and leafy garden plantings, especially where vegetation provides both sunny perches and dense cover. Where Brown Anoles are abundant, Green Anoles often use higher vegetation.

Present in Florida year-round and most active during warm weather. Palma Sola currently has too few records to say how regularly it is seen here.

When

Year-round Florida resident. Palma Sola currently has only one documented observation, so local frequency is not yet established.

How it lives

Active by day, climbing through vegetation to hunt small prey, basking in exposed spots and retreating into cover when threatened.

Males communicate with head bobs, push-up displays and extensions of the pink dewlap.

What it does here

A small predator of insects and spiders in shrubs and trees, and prey for birds, snakes and larger animals.

Plants are the Green Anole's hunting ground, basking platform and shelter. Broad leaves, vines, shrubs and tree branches provide surfaces for climbing and places to ambush insects. Dense, vertically layered vegetation also gives the lizard more choices of perch height and escape cover.

Native to the southeastern United States and the only anole native to Florida.

Also known as

Carolina Anole · American Chameleon
