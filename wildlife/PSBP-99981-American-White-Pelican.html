American White Pelican

Pelecanus erythrorhynchos
On this page

At a glance

  • One of the largest birds in North America — almost entirely white, with a wingspan approaching nine feet.
  • It never plunge-dives the way a Brown Pelican does. It feeds from the surface while swimming.
  • Groups will sometimes work together, driving fish into shallower water where they are easier to scoop up.
  • Florida is wintering territory. Birds breed on inland lakes far to the north and west, then come south for the cool months.

How to know it

Color

Bright white overall. The black flight feathers show strongly on spread wings and all but disappear on a bird sitting on the water.

Bill

Long and yellow-orange, with the pouch beneath it.

Size

Enormous — a wingspan of up to about nine feet.

What to look for

Look for a group rather than a single bird, either resting on open water or soaring high in a line. The size alone settles it.

Brown Pelicans are gray-brown and plunge-dive from the air. American White Pelicans are much larger, mostly white, and usually feed while swimming.

Easily confused with

Wood Stork — In flight both are white with black wing edges, but the white pelican has a huge orange bill and pouch and short legs; the wood stork has long trailing legs, a bare dark head, and a down-curved bill.

Where to find it here

On open pond water in the cooler months, usually in a group rather than alone.

A winter visitor, typically present from late autumn into early spring and then gone north to breed. Sightings here are occasional and seasonal.

How it lives

Active by day and highly social. It fishes from the surface rather than from the air, and groups will sometimes work together, driving fish into shallower water where they are easier to take.

Between feeding bouts it rests on open water or sandbars, and flocks soar high on rising warm air, often in V formation.

What it does here

Open water, not vegetation

This is one of the few birds here whose habitat is defined by the absence of plants. It needs large, quiet, open water to fish cooperatively, and undisturbed sandbars or shorelines to rest on — dense marsh vegetation is of no use to it.

Take care

No meaningful danger to people.

Watch from a distance and do not feed it. White Pelicans are easily flushed, and discarded fishing line is a serious hazard — pack it out.

Conservation

Not currently threatened, though the species is sensitive to disturbance at its northern breeding colonies and to fishing-line entanglement.
