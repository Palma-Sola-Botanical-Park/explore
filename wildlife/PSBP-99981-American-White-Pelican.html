American White Pelican

Pelecanus erythrorhynchos
On this page

At a glance

  • The American white pelican is one of the largest birds in North America, with a wingspan of up to nine feet — a giant, snow-white bird with black wingtips and a long yellow-orange bill.
  • It is a winter visitor to Florida, not a year-round local; the adults breed far to the north and west and come south to escape the cold.
  • Unlike the diving brown pelican, the white pelican does not plunge from the air; it floats on the surface and scoops fish up with its bill.
  • It often fishes as a team, with groups herding fish into the shallows and dipping their bills together — a striking bit of cooperative hunting.

How to know it

Easily confused with

Wood Stork — In flight both are white with black wing edges, but the white pelican has a huge orange bill and pouch and short legs; the wood stork has long trailing legs, a bare dark head, and a down-curved bill.

Color

Bright white overall, with black flight feathers that show mainly in flight.

Bill and pouch

Long and yellow-orange.

Size

Enormous — among the largest birds on the continent.

Feeding

Floats and scoops; does not dive.

Voice

Mostly silent, especially on the wintering grounds.

Size

50 to 65 inches; up to roughly 9 feet

What to look for

A huge white bird with black wingtips, floating in groups on open water or soaring high in slow circles. If a pelican is plunge-diving at the coast, that is the smaller, darker brown pelican; the white pelican scoops from the surface, usually inland.

Where to find it here

On open pond water in the cooler months, usually in a group. A seasonal treat rather than a bird you will see in summer.

Winter visitor, typically present from late fall into early spring, then gone north to breed. Sightings are occasional and seasonal.

When

Breeds far to the north and west; gone for the summer.

How it lives

It fishes cooperatively. A group will work the surface together, herding fish, scooping them up in those enormous pouches — no diving, unlike the brown pelican.

High overhead they soar in flocks, often in a V. They spend the season here feeding and resting, then head north to breed.

What it does here

A large surface-feeding fish predator that, as a seasonal visitor, links Florida's waters to the interior North American lakes where it breeds.

Breeds on inland lakes of western Canada and the northwestern United States, and winters along the Gulf Coast, including Florida, and other warm regions. In Florida it is a non-breeding winter visitor.

Despite sharing a name with the familiar brown pelican, the white pelican lives very differently. It is far larger, mostly white, and tends to favor inland lakes and shallow coastal waters, often soaring in high, wheeling flocks.

Its feeding is a group effort. Rather than diving, white pelicans swim together and herd fish into shallow water, then dip their great bills in unison to scoop them up — a coordinated technique that makes them effective without ever leaving the surface.

Because Florida is a wintering ground rather than a breeding home, white pelicans appear mainly in the cooler months and then head north to nest. They are mostly silent here, drifting in serene formations on the water and in the sky.

Take care

What to do

Watch from a distance and do not feed it. White pelicans are shy and easily flushed, and discarded fishing line is a serious hazard to them — pack it out.
