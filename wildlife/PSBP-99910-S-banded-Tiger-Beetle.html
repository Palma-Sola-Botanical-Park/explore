S-banded Tiger Beetle

Cicindelidia trifasciata
On this page

At a glance

  • S-banded Tiger Beetles have turned up on offshore platforms far out in the Gulf of Mexico, evidence that these small beetles can be carried remarkable distances over open water.
  • A pale, winding band across each wing cover forms the S-shaped marking that gives this species its name.
  • Adults hunt on foot, sprinting across open ground after ants, flies and other small invertebrates and seizing them with large curved jaws.
  • This is a beetle of open ground, not dense vegetation. It uses bare or sparsely vegetated mud and soil along coastal and brackish wetland edges.

How to know it

Size and shape

A small, long-legged beetle about half an inch long, with large eyes and prominent curved jaws.

Wing covers

The dark brown to reddish-brown wing covers have pale markings, including a winding band across the middle that forms the S-shape behind the common name.

Head and legs

The head is large, with prominent eyes and curved jaws. Long legs hold the body above the ground and are built for rapid running.

Similar species

Several tiger beetles occur in Florida's coastal wetlands. The Saltmarsh Tiger Beetle and Margined Tiger Beetle can occur in similar habitat, but their pale wing-cover markings differ. A clear photograph of the back is useful for confirming the species.

Size

About half an inch long.

What to look for

Watch open, damp ground for a small beetle running in quick bursts. When it stops, look for the pale S-shaped marking across the dark wing covers. Large eyes, long legs and prominent jaws give it the characteristic tiger-beetle build.

Easily confused with

Other coastal tiger beetles — The Saltmarsh Tiger Beetle and Margined Tiger Beetle can occur on the same open ground. Their pale wing-cover markings differ, but a clear photograph of the back is usually needed to confirm which species you have.

Where to find it here

Look for patches of open, damp ground near wetland edges. Tiger beetles need bare or sparsely vegetated surfaces where they can run after prey, so they are less likely in dense groundcover.

Look during warm daylight hours, when adults hunt on exposed ground. Species-specific seasonal timing at Palma Sola is not yet well established.

When

Recorded at Palma Sola, but there are not enough local observations to define a reliable seasonal peak.

How it lives

Adults hunt across open ground in short, rapid runs. When approached they may run or fly a short distance and land again.

Adults chase ants, flies, small beetles and other invertebrates across open ground, catching them with large curved jaws.

The larva's burrow

Females place eggs in suitable soil. After hatching, each larva constructs its own burrow and remains there while it grows.

What it does here

Both adults and larvae are predators of small invertebrates. Tiger beetles in turn are eaten by larger insects, birds and other predators, linking the small animals of bare wetland surfaces to the larger food web.

This beetle depends on places where vegetation remains sparse. Open mud and damp soil give adults room to chase prey and larvae places to excavate burrows. Dense plant growth can make otherwise suitable ground unusable.

Native to Florida and other coastal areas around the Gulf of Mexico, Atlantic coast, Caribbean and parts of the American tropics. In Florida it is associated especially with open, moist ground near saline and brackish wetlands.
