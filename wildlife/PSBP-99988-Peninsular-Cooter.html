Peninsular Cooter

Pseudemys peninsularis
On this page

At a glance

  • A large, dark freshwater turtle, most often seen basking on a log or bank — frequently several piled together.
  • Look closely at the head. Yellow stripes form looping, hairpin-like marks behind the eyes — no red ear patch, and no red arrow between the eyes.
  • Adults are almost entirely vegetarian, which is unusual among Florida's pond turtles.
  • Approach too quickly and you get a row of quiet plops as the whole line slides back into the water.

How to know it

Shell

Domed and dark olive to brown, often with faint yellowish markings, growing dull and worn with age.

Head

Fine yellow stripes run across the dark head and neck, forming looping or hairpin-shaped marks behind the eyes.

Size

Large — clearly bigger than a slider when full grown, and females larger than males.

Size, the dark domed shell and the yellow head stripes point to a cooter rather than the smaller, more boldly marked pond slider.

Easily confused with

Florida Red-bellied Cooter — The red-bellied cooter shows a reddish wash below and a red arrow-shaped mark on the head; the peninsular cooter has yellow hairpin-loop stripes and no red.

Pond Slider — Sliders are smaller and wear a colored patch just behind the eye; the peninsular cooter is larger with no ear patch.

Where to find it here

Basking on logs, banks and floating debris at the ponds on sunny days, or as a dark head crossing the open water.

A year-round resident, most visible when it is warm and bright.

How it lives

Much of the day is spent basking — a cooter uses the sun to run its body temperature — alternating with dives to feed. Wary of people, and quick to drop off a log at any movement.

Adults graze on submerged aquatic plants and algae. Young turtles also take insects, snails and other small animals.

What it does here

Basking raises the turtle's body temperature, which supports digestion and other body functions. Drying in the sun can also help reduce algae and some external parasites on the shell and skin.

Adults graze submerged native vegetation such as eelgrass and pondweeds, along with algae, which makes them one of the pond's grazers. Young turtles eat insects and snails before shifting over.

Cooters are long-lived and slow to mature. A large adult may have been using the same pond for decades.

Take care

What to do

Watch from a distance and do not handle or feed them. If basking turtles begin dropping into the water as you approach, give them more room.
