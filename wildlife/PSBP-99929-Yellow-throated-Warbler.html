Yellow-throated Warbler

Setophaga dominica

At a glance

  • It forages more like a creeper than many warblers, moving along branches and probing bark crevices, pine cones and clusters of needles with its unusually long bill.
  • In Florida, Yellow-throated Warblers often work through palm crowns, creeping among the fronds and probing for insects hidden between them.
  • Early naturalist Mark Catesby called it the Yellow-throated Creeper, a name that describes its methodical branch-crawling behavior better than its modern name does.
  • The bill is longer than on most warblers, helping it reach insects hidden in bark cracks, pine cones and tightly packed foliage.

How to know it

Summary

A gray-and-white warbler with a bright yellow throat, black face markings, a long white eyebrow and two white wing bars. Black streaks run along the sides of the white underparts.

Bill

The bill is noticeably long and pointed for a warbler, an especially useful clue when the bird is probing bark or foliage.

Sexes and ages

Females and young birds resemble adult males but are usually somewhat duller.

Similar species

Blackburnian Warblers can also show a bright throat, but their back and facial pattern are different. Black-and-white Warblers creep along branches in a similar way but are entirely black and white.

Voice

The song is a clear series of descending whistles that speeds up before rising at the end. Males often sing from high in the canopy. The call is a sharp chip.

Size

About five and a half inches long, with a wingspan around eight inches.

What to look for

Scan high branches and palm crowns for a gray-and-white bird moving deliberately along limbs and probing into bark or foliage. Look for the bright yellow throat, black face pattern, long white eyebrow and unusually long bill.

Easily confused with

Black-and-white Warbler — Black-and-white Warblers also creep along trunks and branches but lack the yellow throat. Blackburnian Warblers can show yellow or orange on the throat but have a different facial and back pattern.

Where to find it here

Look high in pines, live oaks and palms. In Florida, Yellow-throated Warblers often search palm crowns, creeping among the fronds and probing for insects. They also work bark, pine cones and clusters of needles in the upper branches of trees.

Yellow-throated Warblers occur in Florida during winter and migration, with resident populations in parts of the state. Palma Sola currently has too few observations to establish how regularly they occur here.

When

Occurs in Florida during winter and migration, with resident populations elsewhere in the state. Palma Sola records are too limited to define a local seasonal peak.

How it lives

It moves methodically along branches, probing bark cracks, pine cones and clusters of needles with its long bill. It also makes short flights to catch insects.

Outside the breeding season it also works over flowers, taking the insects drawn in by nectar.

What it does here

Trees are both hunting habitat and, during the breeding season, nest sites. Yellow-throated Warblers probe bark, pine cones, needle clusters and palm fronds for insects. During the nonbreeding season they also forage around flowers such as agave and coconut palm, where insects gather. In places with Spanish moss, nests may be tucked into hanging strands high in trees.
