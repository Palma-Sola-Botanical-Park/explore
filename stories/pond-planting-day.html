A planting day on the Big Pond

Forty pots, a wheelbarrow of mulch, and the Wednesday crew.
Told by the people who were there · February 2026 · Part of Protecting our pond banks

SAMPLE TEXT. The plants arrived on a Tuesday in black nursery pots, a hundred of them, lined up on the shell path like a small crowd waiting for a gate to open. By Wednesday afternoon they were in the ground.

Before the first shovel

SAMPLE TEXT. The rock had gone in the year before. Limestone riprap, placed by hand along the waterline where the small waves from the afternoon wind do their work. Above it, the bank was bare and waiting.

The afternoon

SAMPLE TEXT. Someone had to be in the water. The lowest row goes in right at the edge, where the soil is soft and the rock is slick, and that means standing knee deep with a trowel while the pots come down the bank hand to hand.

SAMPLE TEXT. The students worked in pairs: one digging, one setting the plant and firming the soil, then both moving one step along the bank. Forty minutes in, the line of pots on the path was half gone. The mulch went on last, a ring around each plant to hold the water in.

I did not know you could plant something in a pond. I thought it would just float away.

A Bright Futures volunteer that afternoon

What we planted

SAMPLE TEXT. Scorpion Tail along the top of the bank, where it gets salty wind but never stands in salty water. Below it, the rushes and the groundcover that can take a wet foot. A few months later the first pink puffball blooms showed up in the groundcover, which nobody had planted on purpose.
