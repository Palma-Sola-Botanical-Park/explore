At a glance

  • A king palm from the wet forests of eastern Australia, where it grows along streams and in gullies from near Townsville south to Bateman's Bay.
  • The flowers are the giveaway: pink to lilac, when most palms flower white or cream. They come out in sprays below the crownshaft.
  • Its leaflets are green on both sides. The look-alike Alexandra Palm is silver underneath.
  • It grows at the bottom of the office steps, in the Shade Garden beside the ramp.

Where it comes from

Eastern Australia

Piccabeen Palm is native to the east coast of Australia, from Mount Elliot near Townsville in Queensland south to Bateman's Bay in New South Wales. It is a palm of rainforest and wet forest, growing in colonies where water is reliable: along streams, in gullies and in swampy ground.

Two Australian names

Bangalow and Piccabeen are both Australian names for it, taken from Aboriginal languages of the east coast. The German botanist Hermann Wendland described it in 1858, and in 1875 he and Oscar Drude gave it its own genus, Archontophoenix, chieftain palm. The species name honours Allan Cunningham, the botanist who explored eastern Australia in the 1820s.

Where to find it here

Shade Garden

Go down the steps from the office into the Shade Garden. The palm stands beside the ramp, its ringed trunk rising out of the ferns, with the crown above the white railing.

What it does here

The fourth king palm

Three king palms stand in a row on the path inside the 17th Avenue fence: Purple King, Myola King and Peach River King, each from its own patch of Queensland rainforest. This is the fourth, and the one most people mean by king palm, the one Florida nurseries sell. It grows apart from the others, in the shade by the office, which suits a palm that starts life under a forest canopy.

How to know it

Find the crownshaft

A smooth green to purplish-green sleeve, the crownshaft, sits between the grey ringed trunk and the fronds.

Look under a leaflet

Turn a leaflet over: both faces are green. On the Alexandra Palm, the one it is most often confused with, the underside is silver.

Lilac flowers

The flower sprays come out below the crownshaft, and the flowers are pink to lilac. No other common palm in Florida flowers that colour.

The twist in the frond

Toward its tip a frond twists gently, so the outer leaflets stand at an angle instead of lying in one flat plane.

How it grows

A wet-forest palm

At home it stands in wet forest, so it wants steady water. Young plants take deep shade; older ones grow faster and fuller in brighter light so long as the roots stay moist. In the wild it reaches 80 feet.

It cleans its own trunk

Old fronds let go at the crownshaft and fall whole, leaving a ring on the trunk. There is never a skirt of dead leaves.

Birds carry the seed

The fruit are red, about half an inch across, and birds carry the seed. That is how it has gone wild in New Zealand and in southern Brazil. It is not on Florida's invasive plant lists.
