At a glance

  • A silver fan on a pale column. A grown palm has one smooth, whitish trunk under a crown of stiff, waxy, gray fans.
  • It gets big. In Cuba it can reach about sixty-five feet, with a crown more than twenty-five feet across. Ours has a long way to go.
  • Mind the stalks. The leaf stalks have coarse, sharp teeth along both edges.
  • The flowers crowd together. Tiny whitish flowers sit so close along their branches that they can look like little clusters.
  • The fruit is yellow-green. It is about an inch long, and in Cuba bats carry the seeds away.

Cultural significance

A leaf worth harvesting

In Cuba the leaves are big and tough, and people have used them for roof thatch, mats, brushes and baskets. Cutting leaves for thatch, along with clearing land for farms, has left the palm in scattered patches. Take too many leaves and you hurt the palm that grew them.

A place in the name

Fallaensis points to Falla, in Ciego de Ávila province. The palm was first described from material collected nearby at Ranchuelo.

Where to find it here

Field south of the Plumerias

Ours stands in the field south of the Plumerias. It is young and still small, so you could walk right past it. Look for a stiff, silvery fan close to the ground. Come back and watch it grow.

Where it comes from

Cuba is its only home. It grows there in seasonally dry forest, woodland and open savanna, on heavy, fertile clay.

How to know it

Look for the gray

The fans are broad, stiff and waxy, gray to gray-green, nearly round and flat. Each is cut about a third of the way to its base into stiff segments. Once it has a trunk, look for a pale, smooth one with faint scars where old leaves fell.

Gray is not enough

The Bailey Palm, a close relative, can look gray too, so color alone will not settle it. Some botanists once counted the two as a single species.

How it grows

Flowers shoulder to shoulder

Branched flower stalks rise from among the leaves, standing up at first and drooping later. The whitish flowers sit one by one along the fine branches, packed so close they look grouped.

Fruit in the fall

The fruit is ovate, about an inch long, and ripens yellow-green. In Cuba the palm flowers in May and June, and the fruit takes about four months to ripen, in late September and October.

Take care

Keep hands clear of the leaf-stalk edges. The sharp teeth can cut.
