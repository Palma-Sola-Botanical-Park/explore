Silk Floss Tree

Ceiba speciosa

At a glance

  • The swollen trunk bears large conical spines, especially conspicuous on younger trees.
  • It can shed its leaves before covering bare branches with large pink flowers.
  • Large pods split open to expose black seeds buried in white silky fibres.
  • Young green bark contains chlorophyll and can photosynthesise even while the tree is leafless.

Cultural significance

Toborochi

In Bolivia the tree is known as toborochi and is associated with a story of a pregnant woman sheltered inside its swollen trunk. It has become a cultural symbol in Santa Cruz.

Floss and wood

The silky fibres around the seeds have been used for stuffing, and the light wood for items such as canoes. True kapok, which the floss closely resembles, comes from a relative — Ceiba pentandra.

Where it comes from

Native to subtropical and tropical South America, including parts of Brazil, Bolivia, Paraguay, Argentina and Uruguay.

Where to find it here

By the Office.

A question on our trunk

Our mature tree has gray bark, but green still runs through some of the trunk grooves. We do not know why. One possibility is algae growing in channels that stay damp after rain, rather than chlorophyll in the tree itself.

What it does here

Autumn nectar

The large flowers open when many other plants are past their peak, providing nectar for insects and visiting birds.

What one tree can attract

In one study of a single tree in Mexico, researchers recorded dozens of insect species, several bird species and a nectar-feeding bat at the flowers. That is one tree in one setting, but it shows how much activity a flowering Silk Floss can concentrate.

Compare the family

Kapok and Baobab also grow at Palma Sola. Comparing their trunks makes the family relationship easier to see: each has evolved a very different version of a thickened tropical trunk.

How to know it

Trunk

Swollen and bottle-shaped, with stout conical spines. Young bark can be green; older bark becomes gray.

Flowers

Large pink to pink-purple flowers with pale centres, carried conspicuously on branches when the canopy may be nearly bare.

Pods and floss

Large green capsules split to expose black seeds surrounded by white silk.

Even without flowers or fruit, the combination of a swollen trunk and broad conical spines makes this tree easy to pick out.

How it grows

Green bark

Young trunks and branches contain chlorophyll, allowing them to keep photosynthesising once the leaves have fallen.

Flowers on bare branches

The tree is deciduous and can shed its leaves before flowering, leaving the blossoms unobscured by foliage.

Seeds on silk

Mature capsules split open and expose seeds surrounded by light fibres that aid dispersal.

Why the spines?

One hypothesis proposes that the large trunk spines once discouraged browsing by South American megafauna such as giant ground sloths and gomphotheres. It remains a hypothesis, not a settled explanation.

Full sun and moist but well-drained soil support fast growth. Large surface roots can develop around the base, so this is not a tree for a tight space beside pavement.

Take care

The spines

The large conical spines are the main hazard. Do not lean against or climb the trunk.
