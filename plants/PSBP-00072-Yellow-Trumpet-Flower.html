Yellow Trumpet Flower

Tecoma stans

At a glance

  • Carpenter bees cheat it. Too big to crawl down the flower tube, a bee cuts a slit near the base and drinks the nectar without ever touching the pollen. Look for the slits.
  • It is the national flower of the Bahamas and the official flower of the U.S. Virgin Islands, where it is called Ginger Thomas.
  • Botanists have argued since the 1870s whether it is a Florida native or an early garden escape on the Keys. Either way, it does not grow wild this far north, so ours was planted.
  • The long, slender pods look like bean pods, but it is no bean; it is a cousin of the trumpet trees and the Jacaranda. The seeds inside are papery and winged, about 100,000 to the pound.

Cultural significance

Two flags

The Bahamas chose it as the national flower because it grows wild there and blooms through the year. In the U.S. Virgin Islands it is the official flower and goes by Ginger Thomas. In Texas and along the Mexican border it is sold as esperanza, Spanish for hope; in Mexico it is tronadora, and a tea of the leaves is an old folk remedy for diabetes.

Where it comes from

Tropical America, and maybe the Keys

It grows wild from Mexico and the Caribbean south to Argentina. The first botanist to collect it on Key West, in the 1840s, labeled it introduced, and the argument over whether it is a Florida native has gone back and forth ever since. The state's plant atlas today says not.

What it does here

Flowers with every flush

Each new flush of growth brings flowers, so it blooms on and off most of the year, heaviest in spring and fall. Hummingbirds and butterflies work the yellow trumpets the honest way, down the tube past the pollen.

A moth's nursery

The Plebeian Sphinx moth lays its eggs on it. The caterpillars hide along the stems by day and eat the leaflets at night, so chewed leaves are the sign to look for.

How to know it

Yellow trumpets, toothed leaflets

The flowers are yellow trumpets two inches long with five flared lobes and faint red lines in the throat, in clusters at the branch tips. Each leaf is a row of five to thirteen pointed leaflets with sharply toothed edges, in pairs along the stem.

Pods like beans

After the flowers come slender pods four to ten inches long that hang in bunches, split, and let the winged seeds go on the wind.

How it grows

Shrub or small tree

Where frost cuts it back it stays a shrub of a few feet; where it never freezes it makes a small tree of twenty feet or more. It takes drought and sandy soil, likes full sun, and grows easily from seed, flowering within two years.
