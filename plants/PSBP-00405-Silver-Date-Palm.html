Silver Date Palm

At a glance

  • The sharp needles at the base of each leaf are really leaflets that the palm has shaped into spines. One leaf carries soft gray-green blades above and a row of stiff points below.
  • Male and female flowers grow on separate palms, and only a female bears fruit. This one was carrying orange fruit in July 2026.
  • Lethal bronzing, a palm disease with no cure, was first found in Florida near Tampa in late 2006. It can kill palms like this one within months of the first symptoms.

Where it comes from

A palm of South Asia

Silver Date Palm comes from South Asia. It is one of the date palms, a group that stretches from Africa across to Asia and includes the palm that bears the dates sold in stores.

Where to find it here

Outside the back gate

This palm stands outside the fence, just beyond the back gate. Head out the gate and turn left.

What it does here

Fruit inside the crown

In July 2026 a branching brown stalk hung inside the crown, loaded with small orange fruit. It is easy to miss behind the leaflets, so look in, not only at the outer edge.

Orange to dark

The fruit is about an inch long, with a thin layer of flesh over one hard seed. It ripens from orange to dark red or purple.

How to know it

Silver, low and wide

From a few steps back, a mound of gray-green leaves, wider than it is tall, is the quickest way to pick this palm out. The silver cast is the likely source of its name.

Folded leaflets

Each leaflet is folded lengthwise into a narrow V. Rows of them run down both sides of the central stalk, which is what gives the leaf its feather shape.

Diamonds on the trunk

This palm has no trunk to show yet. As a date palm's trunk rises, old leaf bases leave it patterned with diamond-shaped scars.

How it grows

A seed with a groove

The single hard seed has a groove running down one side. A date palm seedling begins with one simple, pleated blade, and the divided leaf comes later.

Sun and dry spells

Full sun suits it, and once it is established it handles dry spells.

Take care

Needles at the base

The lowest leaflets of each leaf are stiff needles. Keep your face and hands clear, especially where a low leaf reaches out.
