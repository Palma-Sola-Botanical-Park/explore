Screw Pine

Pandanus utilis

At a glance

  • Screw Pine stands on stilts. Roots sprout from the trunk well above the ground and grow down into the soil, bracing the tree like tent poles.
  • On the Indian Ocean islands where it began, its long, strap-shaped leaves were woven into sacks and baskets for shipping sugar, coffee and spices.
  • Male and female flowers grow on separate trees. The heavy, round fruit heads, a bit like green pineapples, belong to the females.

Cultural significance

A crop of leaves

On Réunion, an Indian Ocean island, Screw Pine was grown as a crop for its leaves. By one account, a prize in 1855 rewarded planting 44,000 of them, and jute sacks eventually ended the trade.

Where it comes from

A home nobody has settled

Most accounts put its home on the islands of Mauritius, Réunion and Rodrigues, east of Madagascar, though Madagascar was long thought to be the source. It has been planted so long and so widely that botanists have not agreed. Today it grows in warm coastal gardens around the world.

Where to find it here

Behind the office and on Welcome Island

Four Screw Pines stand behind the office, and they are featured in Winter Nights under the Lights. Another grows on Welcome Island, in front of the Galleria, beside a mature Spindle Palm.

What it does here

Branches, not one crown

Unlike a palm, Screw Pine's trunk forks into branches, each ending in a dense rosette of leaves. Standing under one, you can see the framework beneath the foliage.

Fruit in late summer

Round green fruit heads were hanging from the trees here from late June into September 2026.

How to know it

A crown of spirals

Long, stiff, strap-shaped leaves gather at the branch ends. Follow their bases around a rosette and you can see the spiral behind the name.

Teeth on the straps

Small, sharp teeth line the leaf edges. The leaves are often several feet long, so a low branch reaches well beyond its woody support.

A fruit made of pieces

Each round fruit head is packed with separate fruits, and their angular outer faces fit together like tiles. The head turns yellow-orange as it ripens.

How it grows

Not a pine

Despite its name, Screw Pine is a flowering tree and no relation to pines.

Room to spread

A landscape tree usually reaches twenty to thirty feet, with branches spreading nearly as wide. It likes sun to part shade and well-drained soil, and takes salty air well, but frost badly.

Take care

Mind the clearance

Keep clear of the sharp leaf edges and tips. Watch for heavy fruit heads when you pass under the crowns.
