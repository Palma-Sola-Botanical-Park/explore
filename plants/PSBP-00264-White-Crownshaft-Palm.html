White Crownshaft Palm

Cyphophoenix alba

At a glance

  • A rainforest palm from one corner of one island: the northeast of Grande Terre, the main island of New Caledonia in the southwest Pacific.
  • The name promises a white crownshaft. Ours is young and has not grown one yet; grey fibres wrap the stem where the white sleeve will be.
  • Botanists have moved it twice. Described as Veillonia alba, it went into Cyphophoenix in 2008; a 2024 DNA study put it back on its own.
  • Slow, and its seed is hardly ever offered, so it is rare even in collections.

Where it comes from

Northeastern New Caledonia

This palm grows wild only in the northeast of Grande Terre, the long main island of New Caledonia, about 750 miles east of Australia. It stands in humid forest on schist, where it is locally common: the local Red List assessed it as Least Concern in 2016. Every palm in its genus is found only in New Caledonia.

Two names

It was first named Veillonia alba, after Jean-Marie Veillon, the botanist at the French research station in Nouméa who collected it, and alba, white, for the crownshaft. A 2008 revision of New Caledonia's palms folded it into Cyphophoenix. In 2024 a study of the DNA of the island's palms found that it stands apart after all and revived Veillonia, so you may meet it under either name.

Where to find it here

The palm row south of Cypress Pond

Walk the row of palms along the south side of Cypress Pond, the row that starts with the Red Cabbage Palm at the west end. This is the seventh palm from that end, between the Pindo Palm and the Licury Palm, with a Plumeria beside it. There is no path along the row; you are welcome to walk across the grass.

What it does here

One of two Cyphophoenix

The park grows two palms of this small New Caledonian genus: this one and the Lifou Palm, Cyphophoenix nucele, from an island off the coast. Both came here through a longtime volunteer who collects palms that are hardly grown anywhere. For now this one is a stiff green fountain in a row of larger palms; the white crownshaft is years away.

How to know it

Now: stiff upright fronds

Ours has no trunk yet. Look for stiff fronds held upright and arching, with the leaflets set horizontally along the stalk, each one firm and drawn to a point.

Later: the white crownshaft

Grey fibres wrap the base of the stem. On an older palm they give way to a crownshaft about four feet long, coated in shiny white wax and shading into reddish-brown felt where the fronds join.

A whitened trunk

The upper trunk of a mature palm goes whitish too, above a grey-brown lower trunk ringed with leaf scars about four inches apart.

How it grows

A forest palm, slowly

A palm of humid forest, it wants steady water, shelter from wind and some shade while young. It grows slowly at every stage.

Seed, seldom offered

Male and female flowers share one spray below the crownshaft, so a lone palm can set fruit. The fruit are brown, egg-shaped and finely bumpy. Seed is seldom offered, which is the main reason the palm stays rare.
