Groundcover & Wildflower

Wedelia

Sphagneticola trilobata
On this page

At a glance

  • This is the plant our volunteers pull more than any other. It has reached nearly every corner of the park, and we pull it by hand, with no chemicals, one runner at a time.
  • It spreads in pieces. A stem roots wherever it touches the ground, and a broken piece left lying starts a new plant, which is why mowing it or dumping the clippings only moves it along. It sets few seeds.
  • It came from Mexico and Central America as a garden groundcover and was already loose in south Florida by 1903. It smothers whatever grows lower than it, and it has done the same around the warm world, from Hawaii to Australia.
  • It is often mistaken for our native Beach Sunflower. The native has a bigger flower with a dark eye and rough leaves; this one's flower is an inch across and all yellow, with glossy, fleshy, three-lobed leaves.

Where it comes from

From the tropics, by way of the nursery

Its home stretches from Mexico down into the north of South America. In Florida it was sold for medians and slopes, and it got out into damp spots and shaded hammocks, so Florida now frowns on planting it.

What it does here

Everywhere, by hand

If you see a volunteer on their knees pulling yellow daisies, this is why. A runner pulled and left on the ground will root again, so it goes into the bag.

Mostly leaves in the shade

In shade it keeps growing but barely blooms, so a shaded mat is mostly leaves. In the sun it flowers nearly all year.

How to know it

A flower an inch across

The flowers are yellow to orange-yellow, about an inch across, one to a stalk, with eight to thirteen petals. Look closely and each petal ends in tiny teeth.

Three lobes and a glossy shine

The leaves are glossy, a little fleshy, in pairs along the stem, and usually cut into three lobes with toothed edges. The stems run along the ground and root as they go.

How it grows

Hard to discourage

It takes sun or shade, wet or dry ground, and salty air. Frost kills it to the ground and it grows straight back.
