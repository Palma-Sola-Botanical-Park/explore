Smooth Rattlebox

Crotalaria pallida
On this page

At a glance

  • Count the leaflets. Each leaf is three small leaflets joined at one point. Its cousin, the Showy Rattlebox, has a single plain leaf.
  • The ornate bella moth, bright pink and black, lives mostly on this plant. Its caterpillars eat it and store its poison, then pass it to their eggs, so predators avoid them.
  • Farmers planted it on purpose and plowed it under to feed the soil, like a natural fertilizer.
  • The moth's caterpillars eat the leaves for a short while, then bore into the unripe pods to eat the seeds.

Where it comes from

Probably Africa, planted everywhere

It is probably from tropical Africa, though it has been carried around the warm world for so long that nobody can say for sure. It was also grown as ground cover in plantations. The earliest Florida specimen on record is from 1934.

Where to find it here

In the northmost part of the Royal Palms, near the bougainvillea.

What it does

The moth that flies by day

The ornate bella moth has bright pink hindwings edged in black and, unlike most moths, flies in daylight. The poison it took from this plant as a caterpillar stays with it through the pupa and into the adult. That makes it repellent to most predators. A lacewing larva, which eats other insects' eggs, samples two or three eggs from a cluster of about twenty and leaves the rest alone, put off by the poison in them. At a research station in south-central Florida, scientists studying the moth found it in abundance around stands of this plant, which they knew by its old name, Crotalaria mucronata.

A milder rattlebox

The seeds carry the same kind of liver poison as the Showy Rattlebox, but in smaller amounts. Chicks fed its seeds fell ill only after several weeks.

How to know it

Three leaflets

Each leaf is three oval leaflets, hairless above and lightly hairy beneath. The flowers are yellow pea flowers in upright clusters at the stem tips. The pods are about an inch and a half long and swollen, green at first and brown when dry, with thirty or forty seeds that rattle inside.

A shrubby herb

It is an upright plant that lives a year or a few, and the base turns woody.

How it grows

Sandy ground, warm weather

It likes full sun and poor, sandy soil, and as a bean-family plant it makes its own nitrogen. Frost knocks the tops back. Florida's plant experts ask growers to keep it from escaping into wild land.
