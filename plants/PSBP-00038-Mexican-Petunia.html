Mexican Petunia

Ruellia simplex

At a glance

  • Those purple trumpet flowers are short-lived. The plant is not. Mexican Petunia spreads by both underground stems and seed, which is how a pretty garden flower became a Florida invasive.
  • It's classified as invasive with no recommended uses anywhere in Florida.
  • The purple funnel-shaped flowers are attractive to pollinators, but that does not offset the ecological cost of dense escaped stands.

Where it comes from

Its native range is Mexico through tropical America. Florida is introduced range.

Where to find it here

Serenity Garden

Project placement records put this plant in the Serenity Garden.

What it does here

An invasive-awareness plant

The useful story here is recognition: visitors can learn the common landscape plant before encountering it outside the park.

How to know it

Purple trumpets

Flowers are violet to purple and funnel-shaped, opening above narrow leaves.

Long narrow leaves

Leaves are opposite, lance-shaped and strongly veined.

Colony-forming habit

Established plants commonly form dense clumps as underground stems spread.

How it grows

Built to spread

It tolerates a wide range of light and moisture conditions. That flexibility, combined with vegetative spread and seed, helps explain its success in Florida.

Do not move the wild type

It's rated Invasive (No Uses) statewide. The park record does not yet confirm whether its specimen is a sterile cultivar, so this page does not assume sterility.
