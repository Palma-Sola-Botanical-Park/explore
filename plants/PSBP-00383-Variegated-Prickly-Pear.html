Variegated Prickly Pear

Opuntia cochenillifera

At a glance

  • Spineless is not harmless. Every dot on those smooth pads is a cushion of glochids, barbed bristles finer than a hair that come off at a touch and stay in your skin for days.
  • The species name means cochineal-bearing. The Aztecs farmed a scale insect on these pads for its red dye, and after the conquest that dye was Mexico’s second export after silver. It is still in lipstick.
  • Most prickly pears open their flowers wide for bees. This one folds them into a red tube in winter, for hummingbirds passing through.
  • The green-and-cream pads on ours are a nursery mutation. Grow it from seed and you get plain green.

Cultural significance

A cactus grown for its bugs

Farmers in Mexico kept this cactus for the tiny cochineal insect that feeds on it. Dry the insects, crush them, and you have carmine, the reddest dye the old world had ever seen. Moctezuma took it in tribute; Spain kept the method a state secret for two hundred years. It still colours lipstick, yoghurt and soft drinks today.

Where it comes from

Mexico

Mexico, in dry forest and scrub, though it has been grown for so long and carried so far that nobody is certain where the truly wild plants begin. The variegated form exists only in gardens.

Where to find it here

In the Cacti and Succulents garden. Walk in from the office end and it is about halfway down, on your right: a branching stack of flat pads splashed cream and green.

What it does here

The lean-in plant

People lean in to the variegation, which is exactly the moment to mention the glochids. In winter the red tube flowers are for hummingbirds, and birds take the red fruit after. And if you ever see white cottony patches on a pad, that is cochineal scale, doing what it did for the Aztecs; crush one and your finger comes away red.

How to know it

Flat pads

Broad, flat, jointed stem segments six inches to a foot long, building a branching plant that turns tree-like with age.

Variegation

Irregular cream and pale green over the pads, sometimes pink at the edges. No long spines, or very few.

Red tube flowers

Reddish-pink, an inch or two long, with the petals folded into a tube and the stamens poking out, in late fall and winter.

How it grows

Sun, sand and drainage; it rots in wet ground. A pad that falls off roots where it lands, which is how the variegated form is kept going. One thing to watch for in Florida is the cactus moth, whose larvae hollow a pad out from inside. A pad that looks collapsed gets removed and binned, not composted.

Take care

Do not touch the pads or the flowers. The glochids are nearly invisible, barbed, and miserable to get out. Other prickly pears are eaten as nopales; this one is an ornamental, and nobody should be eating it.
