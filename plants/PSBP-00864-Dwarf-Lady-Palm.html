Dwarf Lady Palm

Rhapis subtilis
On this page

At a glance

  • It grows as a clump. Many thin stems crowd together and make a rounded bush.
  • Count the segments. Each fan is split into anywhere from two to eleven, and it varies a lot from leaf to leaf.
  • The fans are small, up to about sixteen inches across.

Where to find it here

In the Shade Palms

Ours is a full, rounded, substantial plant in the Shade Palms. It has had time to settle in, so it is a good example of how the palm looks when it is happy.

Where it comes from

Southeast Asia, in Thailand, Laos and nearby countries. It grows in forest, near rivers at low elevations in some places and on limestone slopes in others.

How to know it

Pick a leaf and count

The fan spreads from a central point into long segments, and here the plant shows the kind of randomness that turns up all over nature. Count the segments on a few leaves and you can get anything from two to eleven.

How it grows

It likes some shade

It does best in partial shade and moist, well-drained soil, which suits a spot in the Shade Palms. Many stems rise from one root mass, all growing together.

Palm people say: clustering
Solitary
One trunk for life, as on the Royal Palm. It cannot sprout another, so if the top is lost, the palm is lost.
Clustering (this one)
Many stems rise from one root mass. Lose a stem and the clump carries on.
