At a glance

  • It was domesticated twice over, independently, in two separate parts of ancient China. Genetic work published in 2022 finally sorted that out.
  • Florida grows more lychee than any other state on the mainland, and there are seed-grown trees around Sarasota from the 1940s and 1950s still fruiting.
  • New leaves come out bronze-red and harden to dark glossy green, so for a few weeks in spring the tree wears two colours at once.
  • A lychee cannot pollinate itself. Without bees and flies working the flowers there is no fruit at all.

Cultural significance

Worth a courier service

Lychee goes over within days of picking, and that perishability shaped its history. A Tang emperor is said to have run a relay of horses hundreds of miles to bring fresh fruit to the court, which tells you both how good it is and how badly it travels. Most of the world still meets it tinned.

Slow to settle in Florida

It arrived here from India somewhere around 1870, and the first Florida fruit is said not to have ripened until 1916. Forty-odd years of waiting. Growers did not take it seriously until the 1940s, and the state now leads the mainland.

Where it comes from

Southern China

Native to low subtropical forest in southern China and probably northern Vietnam, with wild trees still in the hill country of Yunnan and Hainan. It has been cultivated there for more than two thousand years.

Where to find it here

Before the gazebo

Come into the Rare Fruit garden from the north, by the restrooms, and follow the crushed shell path. The lychee is on your right before you reach the gazebo. It is a full, rounded, densely leaved tree with interpretive signs standing in front of it.

What it does here

Everything depends on the insects

The flowers are hermaphroditic and still cannot fertilise themselves, so every lychee in the garden hangs on bees and flies finding the bloom. A cool or wet spell at the wrong week in spring is the difference between a crop and none. It is the most weather-dependent fruit tree here.

How to know it

Dense, rounded, and drooping

A full symmetrical dome of a canopy, usually on several trunks, with the branch ends hanging down. Even out of fruit it reads as a tidy, heavy, dark green tree.

Leaves in pairs along a stalk

Each leaf is divided into a few long, narrow, pointed leaflets, very glossy on top and dull beneath. Look for the pale new growth against the old dark leaves.

Come back in spring

The bronze-red flush is worth timing a visit for. It lasts a few weeks, and in that window the tree is the most striking thing in the garden.

How it grows

Slow, and very long-lived

Thirty to fifty feet in time, and in no hurry about it. The oldest known lychee, in Fujian, is reckoned to be over twelve hundred years old and is still bearing fruit.

Take care

The flesh only

Ripe lychee flesh is excellent and perfectly safe. The seed is the part to discard — it holds a compound that can drop blood sugar sharply, which has caused real harm where unripe fruit was eaten in quantity on an empty stomach. Peel it, eat the flesh, throw the seed away, and keep the seeds away from dogs, which risk a blockage as well.
