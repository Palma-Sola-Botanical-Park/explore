Red Mangrove

Rhizophora mangle
On this page

At a glance

  • Red Mangrove seeds begin germinating while still attached to the parent tree. The long green propagules eventually drop and can float away before taking root somewhere else.
  • The looping prop roots brace the tree in soft, flooded sediment and let it live where most trees cannot.
  • Where the prop roots reach into the water, the spaces between them make sheltered habitat for fish and other aquatic animals.
  • Mangroves are protected in Florida, and their trimming is regulated by state law.

Where it comes from

Between land and sea

Red Mangrove is native to Florida, the Caribbean, tropical America and West Africa. It grows along tropical and subtropical shorelines, estuaries and other places regularly influenced by salt or brackish water. In Florida, freezing temperatures help set its northern limit.

Where to find it here

Two ponds

Look for Red Mangrove at the Front Pond and on the right side of Cypress Pond. The Front Pond tree was planted in 2021 as part of a pond-preservation project. At Cypress Pond, Red Mangrove has grown into a much denser thicket beneath the Bald Cypresses.

When

Red Mangrove stays green year-round. Look beneath the leaves for small pale flowers and, especially, the long green propagules hanging from the branches.

What it does here

Connected to the bay

Palma Sola's ponds connect through pipes to Palma Sola Bay. Their water is brackish, a mixture of fresh and salt, and fish can occasionally move through those connections into the park.

When the fish come in

Most days that connection is easy to miss. Occasionally fish enter the ponds from the bay, and fish-eating birds arrive in unusual numbers to hunt them. Red Mangrove is at home along this kind of water's edge, where its growing root system adds structure and sheltered spaces.

A tree that changes its shoreline

Red Mangrove roots slow water and trap sediment and organic material around them. That ability to stabilize a shoreline is why the Front Pond tree was planted during a pond-preservation project.

Watch two plantings develop

The older growth at Cypress Pond has become a dense thicket beneath the cypress canopy. The Front Pond tree, planted in 2021, is much younger. Together they show how much a Red Mangrove can change the edge of a pond as it grows.

How to know it

Prop roots

Start with the roots. Red Mangrove sends large arching roots from its trunk and lower branches toward the water and sediment. That exposed framework is its easiest identification clue.

Pencil-like propagules

Look for long green structures hanging straight down from the branches. They may look like oversized beans or pencils, but they are developing seedlings.

Thick leaves

The leathery, oval leaves grow opposite one another along the stem, glossy dark green above and paler underneath.

Look at the whole edge

A single young Red Mangrove can look like a small shoreline tree. Given time, branching stems and overlapping prop roots turn that open edge into a dense thicket, as the growth at Cypress Pond shows.

How it grows

A seedling on the parent

Red Mangrove does not wait for its seed to fall before germination begins. The embryo develops while still attached to the tree, producing the long structure called a propagule. Once released, it may lodge in nearby sediment or float away with the water before establishing elsewhere.

Built for flooded ground

Mangrove sediment is often waterlogged and low in oxygen. The spreading prop-root system braces the tree in soft ground while the exposed parts of the roots stay above the mud.

Living with salt

Red Mangrove can grow in brackish and salt water because its roots keep out most of the salt as they take up water. It does not require seawater, but this ability lets it hold shorelines that many other trees cannot.

Regulated in Florida

Florida regulates the trimming and alteration of mangroves. The rules include exemptions and specific trimming provisions, so the practical lesson is not simply "never touch a mangrove": an established mangrove needs different management from an ordinary landscape tree.
