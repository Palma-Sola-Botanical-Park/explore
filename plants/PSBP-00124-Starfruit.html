At a glance

  • A starfruit picked green turns yellow on the counter but never gets any sweeter. Its sugar builds only while the fruit hangs on the tree.
  • The star is built into the fruit. Its lengthwise ribs, usually five, become the points when you slice across.
  • At night the leaflets tend to fold together.

Cultural significance

A fruit that took years to sweeten

Carambola has been grown in tropical Asia so long that nobody can say where it was wild. A nursery in Manatee County, Royal Palm Nurseries, listed carambola in its 1887 catalog. The first fruit grown in Florida were tart, and sweeter varieties came later from Thailand, Taiwan and Malaysia. The word reached English by way of Portuguese in the 1500s.

Where it comes from

A tropical Asian tree

Carambola comes from tropical Asia, and the wild range is unsettled. It belongs to the wood-sorrel family, which also holds the clover-like weed wood sorrel.

What it does here

Flowers on the twigs

The small pink flowers sit in clusters along twigs and small branches, not out at the tips. We have photographs of them here in June and July 2026.

Ribbed from the start

By late July 2026 small green fruit hung among the leaves, already ribbed. A fruit takes roughly three to four months to ripen after its flower opens.

How to know it

One leaf, many leaflets

Trace a leaf back to the branch. Several oval leaflets share one central stalk, so what looks like a spray of leaves is one compound leaf.

Flowers in miniature

The pink flowers have five petals. Their size is a surprise beside the much larger fruit that follows.

How it grows

Two kinds of flower

Carambola flowers come in two forms, with a long or a short pollen-receiving style. Pollen from the other form often improves fruit set, though some varieties set fruit well on their own.

Keeping a variety

Growers graft the varieties they want onto seedling roots, because a seedling can differ from its parent.

Cold and wind

Young leaves die at 30 to 32 degrees, and mature trees take about 20 to 24. The tree is easily hurt by wind, and dry spells cut flowers and fruit.
