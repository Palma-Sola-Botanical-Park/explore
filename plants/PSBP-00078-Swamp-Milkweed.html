Swamp Milkweed

Asclepias incarnata
On this page

At a glance

  • A native milkweed with rounded clusters of small pink flowers.
  • Monarch caterpillars eat milkweed leaves, and this one is on their menu; the adults come to the flowers for nectar.
  • As its name says, it likes wet ground.

Where it comes from

It is native across eastern North America, from the Great Plains to the Atlantic coast, Florida included, in marshes, wet meadows and along streams.

What it does here

Two kinds of food

Monarch, Queen and Soldier caterpillars can feed on its leaves, taking in the plant's bitter toxins and carrying them into adulthood. Adult butterflies, bees and moths come to the flowers for nectar.

How to know it

Pink clusters

Rounded clusters of small pink to rose flowers, each with five petals folded back and a little crown in the middle.

Narrow leaves in pairs

Narrow, lance-shaped leaves in opposite pairs on upright stems.

Upright pods

Slender upright pods that split to release seeds on silky white floss.

How it grows

It dies back in winter and grows again from the roots in spring. Its seeds ride the wind on their silk.

Take care

Poisonous

All parts are poisonous if eaten, and the milky sap can irritate skin and eyes. Wash your hands after touching it, and keep pets away.
