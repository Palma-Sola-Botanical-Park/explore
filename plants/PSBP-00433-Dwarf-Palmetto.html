Dwarf Palmetto

Sabal minor

At a glance

  • It is a real palm with a real trunk, underground. What looks like a clump of fans coming out of the ground is the top of a stem that grows down and sideways instead of up.
  • Native to Florida and across the South to Texas and Oklahoma, and among the hardiest palms in the world: it grows wild where winters see snow.
  • The flower stalks stand up above the leaves, so its cream flowers and black fruit are easy to spot.
  • Ours is one of five native palms in a line along the park's southern edge.

Cultural significance

Palmetto leaves

The fans of Florida's low palmettos were woven into baskets, hats and fans and cut for thatch long before anyone planted one for looks. Dwarf Palmetto was one of them.

Where it comes from

The southeastern United States

Native from the Carolinas through Florida and west along the Gulf to Texas and southern Oklahoma, in moist hammocks, floodplain woods and swamp edges under hardwoods.

How far north a palm can go

Wild Dwarf Palmettos grow in places that freeze hard every winter. Along with the Needle Palm, it is among the hardiest palms in the world, and one of the few that would survive a winter far north of here.

Where to find it here

The Natives, along the south fence

At the back of the park, the Natives area runs along the southern boundary, and five native palms stand in a line there: Dwarf Palmetto, Florida Thatch Palm, Key Thatch Palm, Needle Palm and Scrub Palmetto, in that order from the Big Pond walkway end. Beyond the fence is county land, outside the park.

What it does

The native palm bench

Five palms that grow wild in Florida, planted in a row so you can compare them: two Sabals, this and Scrub Palmetto; two thatch palms from the Keys; and Needle Palm, among the hardiest palms there are. Dwarf Palmetto is the one that shows how much of a palm can hide underground.

How to know it

No trunk to see

A rounded clump of fan leaves two to six feet high, rising straight from the ground. The trunk is there, under the soil.

A flat fan, not a folded one

The leaf stalk barely runs into the blade, so the fan lies nearly flat, blue-green, cut into stiff segments. Cabbage Palmetto's fan folds strongly down the middle and hangs threads from the segment edges; this one has neither.

Flowers above the leaves

In early summer a branched flower stalk rises well past the leaves. By autumn it carries small, round, black fruits.

It is not Saw Palmetto

Saw Palmetto spreads by creeping stems into wide patches and has saw teeth along its leaf stalks. Dwarf Palmetto grows as one plant from one buried stem, and its leaf stalks are smooth.

How it grows

Shade and moisture

In the wild it grows under hardwoods on damp ground. Part shade and a spot that stays moist suit it, though an established plant takes drought.

One stem, slowly

A single stem, underground, and slow. It never spreads into a colony.

Cold is no problem

Cold is no trouble. It is hardy far beyond the subtropics, which is more than can be said for most of the palms around it.

Fruit for birds

The small black fruits are taken by birds. They are harmless but there is nothing to eat in them: a hard seed under a thin dry skin.
