Snake Plant

Dracaena trifasciata

At a glance

  • Cut a yellow-edged Snake Plant leaf into pieces, and each piece that roots grows into a plant with no yellow edge. The yellow border is a separate layer of cells, and the new plant grows from the green core.
  • A clump that looks like several plants can be one, joined underground by a stem that runs sideways and sends up new leaves as it goes.
  • Snake Plant was tested as a rope fiber. During and after World War II, U.S. researchers tested it as a source of rope fiber, with experimental plantings in Florida.

Cultural significance

The air-cleaning story

In 1989 NASA sealed Snake Plants in a box about 30 inches wide for a day, and the plants did pull some pollutants out of the air. A 2019 review found that matching a room's ordinary air exchange would take roughly ten to ninety plants on every square foot of floor. The air-cleaning reputation comes from the box, not from rooms with doors and windows.

Where it comes from

An African plant with two names

Snake Plant comes from tropical Africa, from Nigeria across to the Congo basin. It was long called Sansevieria trifasciata, and that name is still on many garden labels. Botanists now place it in Dracaena, the dragon trees.

Where to find it here

Along the entrance fence

Turn left at the entrance and follow the fence. A shaded grove stands on your right, shaded largely by a Wild Lime, with Snake Plants and crotons growing under its canopy as you look toward the gazebo by the pavilion. The Wild Lime has thorns, and volunteers working there get jabbed, so keep your arms in.

What it does here

Faint bands

Look along the leaves here for faint dark and pale bands running across them. They are the reason for the name: the pattern recalls snakeskin.

Breathing at night

Most plants open their leaf pores by day. Snake Plant opens its pores at night and holds the carbon dioxide for later, so far less water escapes in the heat. That, with thick water-storing leaves, is how it handles dry spells.

How to know it

Many versions

Some garden forms have yellow edges, some are nearly silver, and some stay short in nest-like rosettes. Leaf width and color vary a lot, so check the stiff, upright shape of the blades.

A flower stalk, now and then

Now and then a slender stalk rises among the leaves with small greenish-white flowers about an inch long. In Java they have been recorded opening toward evening, when they turn fragrant.

How it grows

Slow and steady

It grows slowly, in deep shade or bright sun alike.

Rot is the risk

Wet roots rot. Its reputation for surviving neglect makes sense when neglect means letting well-drained soil dry out, not leaving it soaked.
