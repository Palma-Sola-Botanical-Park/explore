Trinidad Palm

Sabal mauritiiformis

At a glance

  • Big fan leaves droop in deeply separated segments, usually pale or whitish underneath.
  • In Belize this is Bayleaf, grown and harvested for roofing leaves.
  • The small fruit ripens purplish black.

Cultural significance

Growing the roof

Belize Botanic Gardens established a Bayleaf plot to grow roofing leaves instead of taking them from forest palms. The leaves are still harvested there for thatch.

Fiber in the leaf

In leaves from one studied palm, thick-walled fibers surrounded the vascular bundles in the blade and stalk. In the Yucatán, younger leaves are also worked into hats and crafts.

Where to find it here

Outside the front gate

Leave by the front gate and turn right along 17th Avenue. It stands in a row with two other Sabals, the Puerto Rico Hat Palm and the Bermuda Palmetto.

Where it comes from

Native from Mexico through Central America into northern South America, including Venezuela and Trinidad. Much of its range lies in wet tropical country.

What it does

A living tent

In Colombia, tent-making bats cut the main veins of leaf segments so the fan hangs around a shelter. Water can still reach the hanging segments through smaller connecting veins. Modified leaves did not deteriorate faster than intact ones in the February-to-November 2016 comparison. Trinidadian bats also roost in tents made from this palm’s leaves.

How to know it

A fan with a central rib

The leaf stalk continues into the fan as a rib. Narrow segments hang from a short joined base, making a softer, droopier fan. Mature trees have a tall, ringed trunk. The hanging divisions are joined in small groups, which gives the fan a layered look.

How it grows

Cross-pollination worked better

In a Colombian pollination study, stingless bees were important carriers of pollen between palms. Self-pollinated flowers set some fruit, but cross-pollinated flowers set much more.

When it flowers, the flower clusters rise up beyond the leaves, then arch over as the fruit fills.

Ours is still young, its fans rising straight from the ground. A grown one carries them on a tall, slender, ringed trunk.
