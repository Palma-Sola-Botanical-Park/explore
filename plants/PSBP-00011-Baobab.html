At a glance

  • The massive trunk is living water-storing tissue — not a hollow tank.
  • It stands leafless for a good part of the year, which is where the "upside-down tree" description comes from.
  • The large white flowers open at night.
  • The fruit holds a dry, powdery pulp that has been food across Africa for centuries.

Cultural significance

Food from a dry fruit

The hard-shelled fruit encloses a naturally dry pulp, eaten as it is or mixed into drinks and food. Leaves and seeds are used as well, differently in different places.

A village tree

An old baobab can become a gathering place as much as a source of food, fiber and shade. How that works varies widely across African cultures — there is no single baobab tradition.

Where it comes from

This is the African baobab, native across dry parts of sub-Saharan Africa and also found on the southern Arabian Peninsula.

Madagascar is famous for its baobabs, but most of the iconic Madagascan trees are different species in the same genus. This is not one of them.

Where to find it here

In the Serenity Garden.

When

The bare-branch silhouette is a winter and early-spring sight. Leaves return with the warm season.

What it does here

Compare the trunks

Silk Floss Tree and Kapok grow here too, all three in the same subfamily. Standing at each in turn is the easiest way to see how differently related trees have solved the problem of a thickened tropical trunk.

How to know it

Trunk

Thick, swollen and smooth-barked, widest low down and tapering upward.

Leaves

Mature leaves divide into several leaflets radiating from one point, like fingers from a palm.

Flowers and fruit

Large white flowers hang from the branches; elongated hard-shelled fruits follow, holding dry pulp and seeds.

The easiest clue is seasonal — for months of the year the heavy trunk and branches stand almost bare.

How it grows

A trunk that stores water

Spongy living tissue in the trunk holds water taken up in wetter periods. Its water content rises and falls with the seasons rather than sitting at a fixed level.

Leaves are seasonal

It is deciduous in response to a strongly seasonal native climate. Dropping leaves through the dry months sharply cuts water loss.

Night flowers

The flowers open after dark and are pollinated in Africa by night-flying animals.

It wants warmth, sun and excellent drainage. Ground that stays wet suits it far worse than ground allowed to dry out between waterings.
