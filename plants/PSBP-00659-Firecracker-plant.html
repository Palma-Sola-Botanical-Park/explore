Firecracker plant

Russelia equisetiformis

At a glance

  • The flowers are built for one customer. Each narrow red tube is about the length of a hummingbird's bill, and too deep and tight for most bees to work properly.
  • It barely stops flowering. In a frost-free winter it can carry coral-red tubes straight through the months when a hummingbird most needs them.
  • Where a stem droops far enough to touch the ground, it roots. The clump creeps outward that way without ever setting seed.
  • There are about fifty wild species of Russelia, from Mexico down into Colombia. This is the one almost everybody grows.

Cultural significance

Named for a doctor in Aleppo

The genus honours Alexander Russell, an eighteenth-century Scottish physician who spent years in Aleppo and wrote its natural history. The species name is the better one: equisetiformis means shaped like a horsetail, which is exactly what the stems look like.

Coral fountain

Let it grow over a wall or the lip of a container and the stems fall in a sheet of green flecked with red, which is where its other name comes from. British gardeners gave it an Award of Garden Merit in 1993, by which point it had already been a greenhouse plant for over a century.

Where it comes from

Seasonally dry Mexico and Guatemala

Native to country that goes months without rain, which explains the whole design. Narrow green stems lose far less water than broad leaves would, so the plant traded its leaves away and kept working.

Where to find it here

They welcome you at the office

There are two of them growing side by side, one red and one white, in the planting that greets you at the office area - in with the Giant Milkweed, the Beautyberry and the Giant Bird of Paradise. You will walk straight past them coming in from the car.

What it does here

A hummingbird stop at the front door

Between these and the Pentas along the office pavers, the first thing a visitor passes is a run of red tubular flowers that hummingbirds work. Ruby-throated Hummingbirds come through Tampa Bay in autumn and some stay the winter. They do not linger, so seeing one is luck - but if you have a moment on the way in, watch the outer stems, where the flowers hang clear of everything else.

We know it wants cutting back

These have got large and frankly a bit unkempt, and we have been meaning to take them in hand for a while. The trouble is deciding when. A shrub with a season tells you when to prune it - wait until it has finished. This one never finishes. There is no week in the year you can cut it without cutting off flowers that something is feeding on, so we keep putting it off, and it keeps getting bigger.

How to know it

A fountain of green wire

Many slender, ribbed, bright green stems arch up from the base and spill outward. From any distance the plant reads as texture rather than foliage.

Coral-red tubes, and white ones

Inch-long tubular flowers in loose drooping sprays along the outer stems. Ours are a pair - one carrying the usual coral-red, the other white - so do not let the colour talk you out of it being the same plant.

Go looking for a leaf

You will not find much. On the flowering stems they have shrunk to scales you could miss entirely. It is one of the few plants where the honest answer to "where are the leaves" is that it gave up on them.

How it grows

Heat and drainage

It wants warmth, bright light and soil that drains, and it will flower for most of the year once it has both. Two to four feet tall, spreading wider than that.

It spreads by lying down

Stems root where they touch soil, so a clump widens on its own and cuttings strike almost without trying. It has naturalised in some disturbed ground around Florida, which is worth knowing if you plant it somewhere it can wander.

Take care

Not a plant to eat

No part of it is a recognised food. Nobody has documented serious poisoning from it either, and it is not on the standard lists of plants dangerous to dogs and cats - but it has never really been studied, so the honest position is simply to leave it alone rather than to call it safe.
