Carnaúba Palm

At a glance

  • Its fan leaves are coated in wax. The gray-blue cast is the same carnauba wax that ends up in polishes and candy glazes.
  • Carnauba is the hardest natural wax, melting at about 180 degrees Fahrenheit, which is why it gives a polish its hard shine.
  • The name carnaúba is often traced to a native Brazilian word for a tree that scratches, after the hooked spines on its leaf stalks.
  • Ours is young, still a low fan of leaves with no trunk.

Cultural significance

From leaf to wax

In northeastern Brazil, harvesters cut the leaves in the dry season with hooked blades on long poles and lay them out in the sun. Beaten, the dried leaves shed their wax as a fine powder, which is melted, strained and cooled into hard yellow flakes for polishes, coatings and cosmetics.

The tree of life

Brazilians call it the tree of life for how much of it gets used: the wax, the trunks for building, the leaves for thatch, hats and baskets. It is the state tree of Ceará.

Named for an astronomer

The genus Copernicia honors Copernicus. The species name prunifera means plum-bearing, for its small round fruit.

Where to find it here

Come down the stairs from the Galleria and head down the left side of the Galleria Field toward the Big Pond. It stands near the water with the Kukui Tree and the Jacaranda.

Where it comes from

It comes from northeastern Brazil, where it grows in dense groves on river floodplains in a region with a long, hot dry season. Its roots reach water while its leaves face months of drought, and the wax is part of how it copes.

What it does

The wax before the polish

Here you can see the plant behind a familiar product. The sheen on its fans is the wax, still on the leaf; tilt your head and the gray-blue catches the light.

Young for now

Ours is still a low clump of fans with no trunk. With age it lifts its crown on a single gray trunk, and the stubs of old leaf stalks wrap the lower part of it in a rough spiral.

How to know it

Gray-blue fans

Round, stiff fan leaves, silvery gray-green, held on long stalks.

Yellow stalks

The leaf stalks are yellowish, with hooked spines along their edges.

How it grows

Wax against drought

The wax slows water loss from the leaves, which helps the palm stay green through a long dry season when much of the country around it goes brown.

Flowers and fruit

Mature palms send long, branched flower stalks out from among the leaves, followed by small round fruits that ripen dark.

Take care

Spiny stalks

Hooked spines line the leaf stalks. Keep your hands clear of them.
