At a glance

  • Around the beginning of September, tight clusters of vivid purple fruit appear directly around the stems. At Palma Sola, that is when Beautyberry suddenly seems to be everywhere.
  • American Beautyberry is a Florida native. Ours grow around Cypress Pond and the office, and some appear to have seeded themselves into new spots.
  • The fruit is edible, though fresh Beautyberries are tart and not especially flavorful; they are more often made into jelly, with a lot of sugar.
  • Beautyberry really does contain compounds that repel some biting insects in laboratory tests. That is not the same thing as showing that rubbing crushed leaves on your skin works like commercial bug spray.

Cultural significance

The bug-repellent story

Crushed Beautyberry leaves have a history of use against biting insects: in Mississippi, farmers rubbed them on draft animals and tucked them under harnesses to keep flies and mosquitoes off. USDA researchers later isolated compounds from the leaves, including callicarpenal and intermedeol, and found they repelled ticks and mosquitoes in laboratory tests; in one test, two of the isolated compounds matched DEET against tick nymphs on a piece of cloth. Those experiments tested purified compounds, not crushed leaves on skin.

Where it comes from

A Florida native

American Beautyberry grows naturally throughout Florida and much of the southeastern and central United States, as well as Bermuda, the Bahamas and Cuba. In Florida it occurs in hammocks, flatwoods, woodland edges and ground near water.

Where to find it here

You may find more than you expect

Healthy Beautyberry shrubs grow around Cypress Pond, where many reach waist to shoulder height. Several also grow around the office, including one anchoring the sidewalk side of the same planting bed as our Giant Milkweed. Others seem to be turning up on their own, including a newer plant across the office sidewalk.

When

Look for small pinkish flowers during summer, but the plant becomes much easier to notice when the fruit ripens. In 2026 the purple clusters appeared around the first of September, almost at once. In Florida the fruit generally colors from August into October and can hang on well into winter if wildlife doesn't eat it first.

What it does here

A native that moves around

We didn't deliberately put every Beautyberry where you see one. Some appear to have arrived on their own from seed, gradually adding new shrubs around the park. One has even appeared across the sidewalk from the office planting bed, in a spot where there isn't much room for another shrub.

The berries get questions

When the purple fruit appears, our high-school volunteers notice it, and regularly ask whether they can eat it. They can: Beautyberry fruit is edible, although the fresh fruit is tart and not especially flavorful. Birds and other wildlife are considerably more enthusiastic about it.

We tried the leaves

One buggy afternoon, when the bug spray couldn't be found, our high-school volunteers tried the often-repeated advice to crush Beautyberry leaves and rub them on exposed skin. The leaves were dry and not very aromatic, didn't provide much coverage, and we couldn't tell whether they actually helped. The underlying insect-repellent research is real; our experiment was a useful reminder that a laboratory result and a handful of crushed leaves are not the same thing.

How to know it

Look along the stem

The purple fruits don't hang in loose bunches from the ends of branches. Dense clusters wrap around the stem at the points where the leaves attach. That is the easiest Beautyberry clue once the fruit colors.

Before the purple

Small pink to lavender flowers appear in the same positions from June into August. They are much easier to overlook than the fruit that follows.

Look at the whole shrub

Beautyberry is usually a loose, arching, many-stemmed shrub rather than a compact hedge. Established plants can reach roughly eight feet high and wide, although many of ours around Cypress Pond are currently waist to shoulder height.

How it grows

Fruit comes on new growth

Beautyberry flowers and fruits on the current year's growth. New stems produce small summer flowers, followed by the purple fruit clusters that become conspicuous toward fall.

It's comfortable in very different places

In the wild, American Beautyberry grows in wooded areas, open ground and near water. It tolerates both fairly dry conditions and wet soils, which helps explain why it can turn up successfully in different parts of the park.

Take care

Yes, the purple fruit is edible

Beautyberries can be eaten, although the fresh fruit is tart and not especially flavorful and is more often used for jelly or preserves. As with any plant in the park, be certain of the identification rather than sampling an unfamiliar purple fruit.
