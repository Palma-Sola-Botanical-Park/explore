Pink Powderpuff

Calliandra tergemina
On this page

At a glance

  • The pink is not petals. Each puff is a ball of long, silky stamens, the thread-like parts that carry pollen, and the flower has almost no petals at all. The scientific name, Calliandra, is Greek for beautiful stamens.
  • A hard freeze can kill it to the ground, but it regrows from the roots. Then it waits to flower until late summer or fall.
  • It belongs to the bean family. When the puffs are done, flat, strap-shaped pods like a bean's hang among them.

Cultural significance

Two names, one look

The group is called powderpuff in much of the world and fairy duster in the American Southwest, both for the same soft, dusty look of the stamens.

Where it comes from

Tropical America

The powderpuffs are a group of well over a hundred shrubs and small trees, nearly all from tropical America. They sit in the mimosa branch of the bean family, where the stamens, not the petals, are what you see. The pink one grows wild from Mexico through Central America, Belize included, into northern South America.

Where to find it here

Grows on Welcome Island, largely on its own schedule.

What it does here

Who visits

Studies of other powderpuffs, in Mexico and Brazil, found hawkmoths and bats working the flowers at night and hummingbirds by day. Here a bee has been photographed on a puff.

How to know it

Puffs and feathery leaves

The flower is a rounded puff of pink stamens, so fine that the whole head looks soft. The leaves are divided twice, a row of small leaflets on each side of a row of side stalks, which makes the foliage feathery.

Strap pods

After the flowers come flat pods, long and narrow, ripening brown.

How it grows

Sun, and a mild winter

It flowers best in full sun and is evergreen only where winters are mild. A freeze in the low twenties can kill the top, and it regrows in spring. Reported heights run from about three feet to ten, so how big one gets depends on the plant and the gardener. Light pruning after a flush of flowers keeps it tidy.
