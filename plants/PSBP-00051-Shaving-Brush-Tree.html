Shaving Brush Tree

Pseudobombax ellipticum

At a glance

  • The 'shaving brush' is not made of petals. It is hundreds of long threads, the flower's pollen-bearing stamens, fanned out as the petals curl backward. Each thread can be about five inches long.
  • Nahuatl, the language of the Aztecs, called this tree xiloxóchitl, 'corn-ear flower'. In Mexico today it is also called cabellos de ángel, 'angel hair'. Both names describe the same flower.
  • Its seed capsules are packed with soft white fluff, like kapok, the fluff that once stuffed life jackets. People have filled pillows and jackets with this tree's fluff.

Where it comes from

Dry, rocky country

It grows wild in Mexico and Central America, in dry forest and on rocky ground, and drops its leaves in the dry season. Its relatives include the kapok tree, the silk floss tree and the African baobab.

Where to find it here

Behind the office

Shaving Brush Tree grows on Bird of Paradise Island, behind the office. From the office, go around to the other side of the island to find it. It is hemmed in by Giant Bird of Paradise and a Yellow Elder.

What it does here

Marked 'Album'

The sign here calls it 'Album', which is Latin for white and is the name gardeners use for a white-flowered form. Shaving Brush Trees flower anywhere from white to deep pink, so the tag says which one to expect.

Flowers on bare branches

In the dry season the tree drops its leaves, and the brushes open on bare branches where nothing hides them. A tree that is easy to overlook in leaf becomes hard to miss.

How to know it

A hand of leaflets

Five to seven broad leaflets spread from the end of one stalk, like fingers from a palm. New leaves can come out reddish, though the young leaves here are pale green.

Green in the bark

The green markings on the trunk are living tissue that captures sunlight, as leaves do.

How it grows

Bats and bees

In Mexico the flowers suit nectar-feeding bats, and bees crowd the freshly opened flowers for pollen.

Fluff on the wind

When the capsules split, the fluff carries the seeds off on the wind.

Pot and ground

A container-grown plant can develop a conspicuously swollen stem. A tree in the ground builds a larger branching framework, which is why a small potted one may look nothing like a mature tree.
