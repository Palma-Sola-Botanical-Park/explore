Southern Magnolia

Magnolia grandiflora

At a glance

  • Turn a leaf over. The top is glossy dark green, the underside rust-brown and felted, and nothing else here looks quite like it.
  • The flowers shut at night with beetles inside, dust them with pollen and let them go at dawn. Magnolias were working this out long before there were bees.
  • The flowers are the size of a dinner plate and smell of lemon and sweet spice from some distance off.
  • The fallen leaves take a very long time to rot and the roots run shallow and wide. It is a magnificent tree and a real nuisance underneath.

Cultural significance

The petals are food

The thick white tepals are edible, fresh or pickled, and they do not taste the way a big white flower looks. The flavour is strong and hot, somewhere between ginger and cardamom. The cones and seeds are not food.

A southern emblem

It is the state tree of Mississippi, and across the South it is the tree people mean when they say magnolia.

Where it comes from

Native, but from further north

It grows wild along the southeastern coastal plain from Virginia down into central Florida and west to Texas, in moist sheltered ground that escapes regular fire. Its natural Florida range really belongs to the northern half of the state, so around Bradenton this is a native tree planted south of its home ground, doing well.

Where to find it here

Two of them, well apart

The park has two Southern Magnolias, and they are far enough apart that most visitors only ever meet one. The easier one to find stands beside a raised wooden deck, close enough that you can look into the crown from the rail rather than craning up from the lawn.

What it does here

Green all winter

It holds its leaves through the year, so when a good deal of the park has thinned out this is still a solid dark mass. Birds use that cover, and so does anyone looking for shade in August.

The mess is part of the deal

Those heavy waxy leaves drop all year and break down slowly, and the surface roots make it close to impossible to keep grass going underneath. That is simply what this tree asks for in exchange.

How to know it

The rusty underside

This is the quickest check and it works all year. Flip a mature leaf: the back is coated in dense brown felt.

Cones, then red seeds

After flowering, a knobby green cone hardens up through the summer. In fall it splits and bright red seeds swing out on thin silky threads before the birds and squirrels get them.

Worth checking at dusk

If you pass a magnolia in flower late in the day, look for a bloom that has folded itself shut. There are beetles inside it, and they will not be let out until morning.

How it grows

Big, dense and slow to hurry

Sixty to eighty feet in time, with a broad pyramidal crown. Left alone in the open, the lower branches sweep all the way to the ground and the tree becomes a solid green wall.
