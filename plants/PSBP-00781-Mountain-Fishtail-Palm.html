At a glance

  • Each leaf is divided twice, and every leaflet ends in a ragged, bitten-off edge: the fishtail that names the genus.
  • A single leaf can grow more than 20 feet long.
  • It grows one trunk. Its cousin the Clustering Fishtail Palm makes a clump of stems instead.
  • Leave the fruit alone. Its pulp is full of needle-shaped crystals that irritate skin and mouth.
  • Ours is young, and it stands at the park's southern edge with open land beyond it.

Cultural significance

Fiber for tinder

Where it grows wild, the fibers that wrap the leaf bases have been gathered as tinder. You can see them on ours: a brown mesh where each leaf stalk hugs the trunk.

Where it comes from

It is native to southern and southeastern Asia: southern China, parts of India, mainland Southeast Asia, Sumatra and Java. It grows in rainforest from the lowlands up into the mountains, and in disturbed ground.

Two names

Botanists do not all agree on what to call it. Some now fold it into the Jaggery Palm, Caryota urens. Others keep it as its own species, Caryota maxima, the name we use.

Where to find it here

It stands on the south side of the Big Pond's south walkway, near the park's southern edge.

What it does

A tree on the edge

Right now this palm stands near the park's southern boundary. We hope the land beyond it will one day become part of the park. If it does, a palm planted at the edge will find itself close to the middle, and possibly quite tall!

How to know it

Divided twice

Stand under a leaf and look up. The main stalk carries side branches, and the branches carry the leaflets, so the leaf is divided twice. The leaflets hang in one plane, which gives the big leaf a layered look.

Ragged tips

Each leaflet is a wedge, wide at the end and jagged, as if something had bitten it off. That fishtail edge is the mark of the whole genus.

One trunk

Look for a single upright trunk. On older palms it carries slender rings of leaf scars, spaced well apart. The Clustering Fishtail Palm, Caryota mitis, has similar leaves but grows as a clump of stems.

How it grows

One flowering, then the end

A mature palm sends out huge hanging flower clusters, three to five feet long, one at each ring of leaf scars. They start near the top of the trunk and work downward, ripening from the highest to the lowest. The small flowers sit in threes, a female between two males. When the lowest cluster has fruited, the palm dies.

The fruit

The round fruits are about an inch long, ripen pink to dark reddish purple, and usually hold a single seed.

Massive in the wild

In its native forests this palm can be massive, up to 100 feet tall on a trunk about a foot thick. Time will tell how it does as a transplant on the west coast of Florida, and we look forward to finding out.

Take care

Do not handle the fruit with bare hands or put it in your mouth. The pulp holds needle-shaped calcium oxalate crystals that irritate skin and the mouth.
