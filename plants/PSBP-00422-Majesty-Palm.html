Majesty Palm

Ravenea rivularis

At a glance

  • The 'rivularis' in its scientific name, Ravenea rivularis, means 'of streams'. In the dry west of Madagascar it grows along rivers and in canyons, sometimes standing in shallow water.
  • In the wild this palm is at risk. Seed is collected from wild trees to sell to plant nurseries, and some of that collecting is illegal.
  • The potted palm you know can grow into a tree of about 30 feet when planted outdoors in Florida, but it stays short for a long time first.

Cultural significance

A count that changed

In 2010 about 900 mature trees were estimated in the wild. Since then more stands have turned up, and the palm is now known from ten places in the west of Madagascar. No one has a current total, and the stands are scattered and mostly still declining.

What threatens the stands

Wildfire, mining and oil prospecting threaten some of them, and the stands inside protected areas all sit at the edges of those areas. Madagascar limits how much seed can be exported each year, but illegal harvest continues.

Where to find it here

Along the south side of the Big Pond, between the Christmas Palm and the Triangle Palm.

Where it comes from

Water in dry country

Most of western Madagascar is dry. This palm lives where the water is: riverbanks in lowland forest, the floors of deep canyons, and swamp forest, from about 450 to 2,750 feet above sea level.

What it does

A crown you can look across

This palm is still young. Its crown sits low, with upright new leaves in the middle and arching older ones around them, so you can see the whole shape without looking up. The thick base is wrapped in old leaf bases.

Compare the neighbors

The Christmas Palm on the same walk has a smooth green sleeve, called a crownshaft, where the leaves meet the trunk. This palm has none: its leaves come straight off the trunk.

How to know it

A fountain of feathers

Many narrow leaflets line a single central rib on each frond. New leaves rise upright from the middle, and the older ones arch outward.

Thick at the foot

Young palms are thick at the base and wrapped in overlapping leaf bases. Older ones have a single gray, ringed trunk, often swollen at the bottom.

Pale leaves mean hungry

Washed-out, yellowing older leaves are the usual sign of a hungry palm. Nitrogen shortage is common in this palm in Florida.

How it grows

A palm that wants water

It grows beside rivers, and in Florida it does best with steady moisture and regular feeding. Palm weevils can be a problem.

Two sexes

Male and female flowers grow on separate palms. Only females carry the small red fruit, and only when a male is near enough to pollinate them. Until a palm flowers, its leaves don't tell you which sex it is.
