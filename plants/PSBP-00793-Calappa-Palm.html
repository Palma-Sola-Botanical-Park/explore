Calappa Palm

Actinorhytis calapparia

At a glance

  • A rainforest palm from New Guinea and the Solomon Islands. It loves water and cannot take drought.
  • Grown up, it is one slender trunk with strongly arching fronds above a slim crownshaft, the smooth column made by the wrapped leaf bases.
  • Look at the foot of an older palm: a cone of roots can build up around the base of the trunk.
  • It starts flowering at four or five years old, and the flower clusters hang below the leaves.
  • Actinorhytis means ray and wrinkle, after the folds that run all through the inside of its seed.

Cultural significance

A charm, a toy, a chew

This palm is often planted around villages in Southeast Asia. Carrying one of its fruits was thought to protect you, children have played with the hard seed kernels, and the kernel has been chewed in place of betel nut.

Many names

In Malaysia it goes by pinang penawar, and the Bagobo people of the Philippines call it tangalo.

Where to find it here

It stands in the Shade Palms, the first palm along the north path toward Tides End. It is having a hard time: this corner lost many palms to salt water from Hurricane Helene in 2024. We are working on getting it going, and we hope the worst is behind it.

Where it comes from

It is native to New Guinea and the Solomon Islands, where it grows in lowland rainforest. People have carried it from there, and it is now planted throughout the islands between Malaysia and New Guinea. Its genus is a small one, with just two species.

What it does

Its flowers are pollinated by bees.

How to know it

From the ground up

Start at the base. On an older palm, a cone of roots can build up where the trunk meets the soil. Above it, a single bare trunk is ringed with old leaf scars, and at the top a slim crownshaft holds the leaves.

Leggy when young

The fronds arch strongly and carry many narrow leaflets, set evenly along the midrib. On a young palm the leaf stalks are long; they get shorter as the palm matures, so a young one looks leggier than an old one.

Flowers and fruit below the crown

The flower clusters come out of the trunk below the leaves and spread or hang. On the lower part of each branch, a female flower sits between two males; toward the tip there are only males. The fruit is oval and smooth, green at first and red when ripe, about the size of a small hen's egg, with one seed inside.

How it grows

Thirsty by nature

In the wild it lives in lowland rainforest and wants steady moisture. It cannot take drought or soil that dries out, though it still likes good drainage.

Slow to show off

It starts flowering at four or five years old, with male and female flowers on the same palm. Given time, it can reach about 50 feet on a slender trunk. Ours is still knee-high, so this is a palm to come back for.
