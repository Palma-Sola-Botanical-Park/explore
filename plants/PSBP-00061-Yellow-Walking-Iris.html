Yellow Walking Iris

Trimezia steyermarkii
On this page

At a glance

  • Bright yellow flowers mottled with brown, each open for about a day, with new ones following for weeks.
  • It walks: a little plant forms at the tip of a spent flower stalk, its weight bends the stalk down, and it roots where it lands.
  • For years it was labeled African Iris around the park. The sign in the photo at the top of this page still says so.
  • Once settled it takes both flooded ground and drought.

Where to find it here

On Welcome Island, near the Green Buttonwood.

Where it comes from

It comes from southern Mexico through Central America to Colombia and Venezuela.

Not a true iris

It belongs to the iris family but not to the genus Iris. The fan of flat leaves and the three-part flower are family traits it shares with the African Irises, which come from another continent altogether and never carry a plantlet on the stalk.

What it does

A case of mistaken identity

It sat under an African Iris label for years, and the mix-up is easy to understand: both make fans of sword-shaped leaves and yellowish, iris-like flowers with dark marks. The African Iris has paler, creamy flowers with distinct dark blotches. This one is a brighter yellow with brown mottling, and only this one walks. We do have a real African Iris, by the pond on Welcome Island, if you want to compare the two.

Daily turnover

A clump in bloom opens a few new flowers each day and drops yesterday's, so the display is never quite the same two mornings running. Each flower gets one day.

How to know it

Fans of leaves

Long, narrow, sword-shaped leaves in fans, like other irises.

Yellow and brown

Bright yellow flowers held above the leaves on a thin stalk. The three big outer petals are plain yellow; the three small inner ones curl and are mottled brown.

Look for a baby

Check the flower stalks for a small plant growing at the tip, still attached to its parent.

How it grows

How it walks

After flowering, a small plant forms at the tip of the flower stalk. As it grows, its weight bends the stalk to the ground, the young plant roots where it touches, and the clump creeps outward one plantlet at a time. Underground it also spreads by rhizomes, so a patch widens from below as well as by walking.

Once settled it takes both wet ground and dry spells, which suits ground that floods in the summer rains and bakes in spring. A hard freeze browns the leaves, and it grows back from the roots.
