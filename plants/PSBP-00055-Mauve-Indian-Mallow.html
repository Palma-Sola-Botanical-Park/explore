Mauve Indian Mallow

Abutilon hulseanum
On this page
At a glanceWhere it comes fromWhere to find it hereWhat it does hereHow to know itHow it grows

At a glance

  • A Florida native with orange-pink, hibiscus-like flowers.
  • It grows tall and loose, more a framework of stems than a dense shrub.
  • Ours were planted at the edge of Cypress Pond as part of our native bank planting.

Where it comes from

It is native to Florida and Texas, south through Mexico and into the Caribbean, in open and disturbed ground.

Where to find it here

At Cypress Pond, beside the crushed-shell path. Face the water and look to the left of the native Golden Cannas.

What it does here

Part of the bank planting

We bought four of these from a Florida native nursery and planted them at the pond edge, alongside the native Cannas, as part of our work to plant the banks with natives.

How to know it

Loose and upright

Tall, loosely branched stems, often leaning, with plenty of air between them.

Soft leaves

Heart-shaped leaves covered in fine, soft hairs.

Flowers, then seed heads

Orange-pink flowers with five petals and a central column of stamens, followed by round, ribbed seed heads.

How it grows

Each plant is short-lived, but it seeds itself readily, so a planting can carry on as a colony.
