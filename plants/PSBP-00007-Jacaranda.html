At a glance

  • A mature tree can cover itself in violet-blue flowers while the branches are still nearly leafless.
  • The foliage is finely divided and fernlike, so the tree is recognisable even when it is not flowering.
  • Flat woody seed capsules stay on the branches long after the flowers are gone.
  • It is planted widely around the world even though its wild populations face conservation pressure in its native range.

Cultural significance

Jacaranda cities

Jacarandas have become part of the identity of cities far outside South America. Pretoria is famously associated with streets lined in purple bloom, and Grafton in New South Wales holds a long-running Jacaranda festival.

A tree can become culturally local without being botanically native. These urban traditions all grew up around an introduced South American species.

Where to find it here

In the Galleria Field.

When

The main violet-blue display comes in spring and into summer. Outside bloom, go by the fernlike foliage and the flat pods that stay on the tree.

Where it comes from

Its core native range is in Bolivia and northwestern Argentina, in dry inter-Andean valleys and the forests below them. Botanical sources disagree over whether parts of Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay belong in the native range as well — centuries of cultivation have blurred where the wild boundaries were.

Common abroad, pressured at home

Jacaranda is cultivated across the warm regions of the world while habitat loss reduces the populations it evolved in. A tree can be among the most planted on earth and still be losing ground where it belongs.

What it does

Large bees work the tubular flowers for nectar and pollen, and other flower visitors use them too.

How to know it

Flowers

Lavender-blue to violet trumpet-shaped flowers carried in large clusters.

Leaves

Finely divided, twice-compound leaves that look like oversized fern fronds.

Pods

Hard, flattened, rounded woody capsules that persist long after flowering.

Stand back and use the silhouette: the crown is broad and spreading, often wider than the tree is tall, and the trunk commonly bends rather than rising straight.

How it grows

Flowers before leaves

In cooler conditions the tree loses much of its foliage, and the next flowering flush can arrive before the new leaves are fully back — which is why a tree in bloom can read as solid colour.

Feeding it can cost you the flowers

Heavily watered or fertilised trees are reported to put their energy into foliage rather than flowers, and cool weather appears to encourage the display. Full sun gives the heaviest bloom.

Once established it handles seasonal drought well. It does not tolerate salt.

It spreads elsewhere

Not considered invasive in Florida, but it has become invasive in places including South Africa and parts of Australia — worth knowing about a tree this widely planted.
