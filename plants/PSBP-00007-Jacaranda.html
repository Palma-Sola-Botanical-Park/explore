At a glance

  • The whole canopy can turn violet-blue before a single leaf opens — the flowers arrive first.
  • The leaves are very finely divided and fernlike, so the tree is recognisable out of bloom.
  • Flat woody seed pods, about two inches across, hang on long after the seeds have gone.
  • It is listed as Vulnerable in its wild range while being one of the most planted ornamental trees on earth.

Cultural significance

The Jacaranda cities

Pretoria is called the Jacaranda City for its 70,000 street trees. Grafton in New South Wales holds an annual Jacaranda festival, and the trees line whole boulevards in Lisbon. In each of those places it is a beloved import that arrived from South America and stayed.

And Pretoria will lose them

Because the tree spreads readily in South Africa, planting new ones there is now prohibited — but removing the mature street trees proved politically impossible. The Jacaranda City will gradually lose its jacarandas to old age, with nothing allowed to replace them.

The name comes from a Guaraní word meaning fragrant.

Where it comes from

Native to the dry inter-Andean valleys and piedmont forests of northwestern Argentina and southern Bolivia. Sources disagree about whether southern Brazil, Uruguay and Paraguay belong in the native range — Kew lists only Bolivia to northwest Argentina — and that disagreement is itself telling: centuries of cultivation have blurred where the wild boundaries were.

Common everywhere, threatened at home

The IUCN lists it as Vulnerable in its native range, where logging and agricultural clearing are reducing it, while millions of cultivated trees flourish on every warm continent. A tree can be among the most abundant on earth and still be losing the only ground it evolved on.

Where to find it here

In the Galleria Field.

When

Flowering runs April through August, most often peaking in May around Tampa Bay. Out of bloom, look for the fern-like foliage and the flat pods.

What it does here

The long tubular flowers are built for large bees — carpenter bees especially — and also draw butterflies and hummingbirds. The fallen blossoms go on feeding insects at ground level, and the woody pods hold seed for birds into autumn.

How to know it

Flowers

Dense clusters of lavender-blue trumpet-shaped flowers up to two inches long, lightly fragrant.

Leaves

Finely divided and twice-compound, like a giant fern frond, casting light filtered shade.

Pods

Flat, round, woody capsules about two inches across that stay on the tree year-round.

The trunk often bends or arches rather than growing straight, and the crown spreads wider than the tree is tall. That silhouette identifies it from a distance.

How it grows

Cold and poor soil make the flowers

Flowering is triggered partly by mild cold stress, so Bradenton's occasional cool winters may actually improve the display — several nights in the upper thirties is reportedly what it wants. It also flowers best in poor soil. A well-fed, well-watered Jacaranda puts its energy into leaves instead.

Flowers before foliage

It is deciduous in cooler winters, and the bloom can arrive on bare branches before the new leaves open. That is why a flowering tree reads as solid colour.

Grown from cuttings, not seed

Seedlings can take many years to bloom and may not come true, so cultivated trees are usually grafted or grown from softwood cuttings.

It spreads elsewhere

Not invasive in Florida and not listed as a concern by UF/IFAS. But it is invasive in South Africa and parts of Australia, where it displaces native vegetation — worth knowing about a tree this widely planted.

Full sun for heaviest flowering, well-drained soil of almost any texture, high drought tolerance once established. Salt tolerance is low.
