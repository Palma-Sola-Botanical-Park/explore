Candelabra Bush

Senna alata

At a glance

  • Candelabra Bush is also called Ringworm Bush. Ringworm is a fungal skin rash, and across the tropics people rubbed these leaves on it. Lab tests have since found the leaves do fight those fungi.
  • The flowers keep their pollen in tiny pores, so a big carpenter bee has to buzz each flower to shake it loose. A study in India found carpenter bees were the only visitors to this plant.
  • Two yellow butterflies, the cloudless sulphur and the orange-barred sulphur, lay their eggs on senna plants, and their caterpillars eat the leaves.

Cultural significance

A leaf with a long medical record

In the Philippines the plant has its own name, akapulko, and a published review reports clinical trials of leaf preparations on skin fungus. The chemicals behind it, anthraquinones such as rhein, are also what make the leaves a strong laxative if eaten.

Where it comes from

Tropical America, and everywhere warm

It comes from tropical America, from Mexico and the West Indies south to Paraguay, and it now grows wild in the tropics around the world.

Where to find it here

Near the front gate. We tried to remove it, but it seems to still be here.

What it does

Two kinds of stamens

Senna flowers have two jobs divided between their stamens: the short ones feed the bee, and the long ones deposit the pollen.

How to know it

Ladder-like leaves

Each leaf is a long stalk set with eight to fourteen pairs of big oval leaflets, like the rungs of a ladder.

Candles

The flowers are bright yellow, five petals each, in stiff upright spikes above the foliage. Before they open, the spike is a tight, pointed bud.

Winged pods

The pods are long and narrow, with a thin wing along the length of each side, and they ripen brown and hang on the plant.

How it grows

Fast and short-lived

It is a quick shrub or small tree of six to twelve feet, often with several stems, and it does not live long. It wants full sun and moist, rich soil. Frost cuts it back. Florida's plant experts advise against planting it.

From seed

New plants come up from seed.
