Turk's Turban

Clerodendrum indicum

At a glance

  • The flower seems to fool bees. A yellow tip looks like a dab of fresh pollen, so bees land on it. It is really the part that catches pollen, so a bee drops its pollen there and leaves with none.
  • The red star under each blue fruit is not a petal. It is the flower's green base, which grew larger and turned red after the white petals dropped.
  • The long white tube under each flower's petals looks like a stalk, but it is the flower itself, about three inches long. A long pinkish thread reaches out of it.

Where it comes from

From tropical Asia

Turk's Turban comes from tropical Asia. Tube Flower, one of its other names, refers to the long white tube of the flower itself.

Where to find it here

The Shade Garden wall

Just outside the office, Turk's Turban grows with Firebush, and together they make the leafy wall along one side of the Shade Garden. The paver path leads through that taller planting into the lower, shadier garden.

What it does here

Where the trick was seen

The pollen trick was watched in West Bengal, India, where stingless bees were the only insects that pollinated the flowers.

A flower that lasts four days

A flower there opened in the late afternoon and lasted about four days, its petals curling back as a long pinkish thread reached out of the tube.

Nectar on the outside

Some of its nectar sits outside the flower, on tiny hairs and on the cup beneath. In India, insects drank from these open places without carrying pollen between flowers.

A sticky obstacle

Ants were found stuck in sticky secretions on that cup, which may keep crawling insects off. The plant can feed some visitors while making the climb hard for others.

How to know it

Blue knobs on a red star

The fruit is a few rounded, blue-black knobs joined together, sitting in an open, five-pointed red cup at the branch tips.

Leaves in tiers

Pointed leaves grow in groups around upright stems, each with a pale midrib and a network of fine veins.

How it grows

Upright stems, hanging clusters

The stems stand upright, with flower and fruit clusters at the tips. As a cluster grows, its branching stalks arch down beneath the leaves. Moist, well-drained soil suits it.
