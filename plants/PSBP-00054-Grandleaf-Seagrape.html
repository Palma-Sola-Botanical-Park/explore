Grandleaf Seagrape

Coccoloba pubescens

At a glance

  • Its round, quilted leaves can be well over a foot across, big enough to hold over your head in a shower, which is why it is also called Eve's Umbrella.
  • It is a cousin of the Seagrape on Florida's beaches, but a different species, from the islands of the eastern Caribbean, and seldom planted in this country.
  • Young trees carry bigger leaves than old ones, the reverse of what most trees do.
  • Visitors who find a fallen leaf often ask if they can take it home.

Cultural significance

Eve's Umbrella and Tin Roof Tree

Both of its nicknames are about one leaf. Eve's Umbrella says a single leaf will shelter you from a shower. Tin Roof Tree says the dark, glossy surface of a big one, pitched over your head, looks like sheet metal.

Where it comes from

It comes from the Caribbean islands, from Hispaniola and Puerto Rico down through the Lesser Antilles. There it grows into a tree of 80 feet with an open, sparsely branched crown. Ours is still a small tree with several trunks.

Buckwheat family

The seagrapes belong to the buckwheat family, alongside rhubarb, garden sorrel and the knotweeds. Nothing about the leaves says so. The clue is the flowers: tiny, greenish white, crowded on upright spikes up to two feet long, like a knotweed's drawn at a larger scale.

Where to find it here

In the Pavilion Field, right beside the pavilion, near its north door.

What it does here

Leaves that start conversations

Its fallen leaves are big enough that visitors stop to pick one up, and many ask if they can take it home. We clear them away regularly, and the question keeps coming.

A first for most visitors

The beach Seagrape grows along every Florida coast. This island cousin is rarely grown in the United States, so most people are seeing it for the first time, and those who know the beach plant usually don't believe the two are related until they look at the flowers.

How to know it

Round, not oval

Nearly round leaves, puckered between the veins, with smooth, wavy edges, on a small open tree with several trunks.

Turn one over

The underside is paler, with yellow to reddish veins.

Biggest on the youngest wood

The biggest leaves grow on young plants and on vigorous new shoots low down. Higher on an older tree they get smaller. Compare a leaf near the ground with one overhead.

Compare with the beach Seagrape

The beach Seagrape has smaller, stiffer, leathery leaves and grows as a dense shrub or small tree.

How it grows

Old leaves turn copper, orange and brown while they are still on the tree, so there are usually a few coloring among the green, and the ground beneath collects them.

Two kinds of tree

Male and female flowers grow on separate trees, so only a female tree makes the grape-like clusters of fruit. We don't yet know which ours is.
