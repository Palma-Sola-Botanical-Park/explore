Golden Shower Tree

Cassia fistula

At a glance

  • Long chains of yellow flowers hang from the branches in late spring and early summer, briefly turning an otherwise inconspicuous tree into something much easier to spot.
  • The dark cylindrical pods that follow can reach about two feet long and stay on the tree through the winter.
  • Break open a mature pod and it isn't one long hollow tube: the seeds sit in a row, separated by partitions, with dark pulp packed around them.
  • It is native to a smaller area than older books say: current Kew taxonomy places its home range in western and southern India and Sri Lanka. People have carried it through tropical Asia and much of the rest of the tropics.

Cultural significance

A tree with a long medicinal history

The dark pulp around the seeds has a long history of use as a laxative. The Latin name fistula means pipe, for the long tubular pod, and an older English name for the tree is Pudding-pipe Tree.

Where it comes from

From South Asia

Golden Shower Tree is native to western and southern India and Sri Lanka, where it grows in country with a real dry season. People have planted it far beyond that original range, including across Southeast Asia, the Caribbean and Florida.

Where to find it here

A little off the main route

We have one Golden Shower Tree. Go around the left side of the Pavilion and past the Butterfly Garden, then look off the path on your left as you continue toward the Rare Fruit area. It is a substantial tree, but outside flowering season it blends easily into the surrounding greenery.

When

Late spring and early summer is the easiest time to notice it, when long clusters of yellow flowers hang from the branches. Later, look for the long, dark cylindrical seed pods.

What it does here

A host tree nobody has checked

In Florida, Cassia trees are larval hosts for three sulphur butterflies: Cloudless Sulphur, Orange-barred Sulphur and Sleepy Orange. It stands right beside the Butterfly Garden, and nobody has yet looked for caterpillars on it.

How to know it

Look for the hanging flowers

The yellow flowers grow in long, loose clusters, a foot or more long, that hang beneath the branches rather than standing upright above the foliage.

Then look for the pods

The fruit is hard to confuse with the flowers that came before it: long, cylindrical pods that ripen dark brown to nearly black and can approach two feet in length.

Follow one leaf

What looks like several leaves is one compound leaf, divided into pairs of oval leaflets each about the size of your hand. Unlike the finely divided foliage of Royal Poinciana, these leaflets are broad and easy to pick out one by one.

How it grows

Flowers become long pods

Golden Shower Tree is in the bean family. After flowering, the developing fruit lengthens into a cylindrical pod. Inside, a series of crosswise partitions separates the many hard seeds, with dark pulp filling the spaces around them.

It may briefly look bare

The tree can drop its leaves for a short period before replacing them. Here in Florida it grows best with plenty of sun and well-drained ground.

Take care

Don't sample the pods

The pulp inside the pods has been used medicinally as a laxative, and the seeds are poisonous. These aren't pods to taste while exploring the park.
