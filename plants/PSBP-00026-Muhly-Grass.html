Muhly Grass

Muhlenbergia capillaris
On this page

At a glance

  • Muhly Grass is native to Florida and much of the eastern and central United States.
  • In fall, fine branching flower panicles rise above the foliage and create the familiar pink-purple haze.
  • It grows as a dense clump rather than spreading by long running stems.
  • Small birds eat the seeds, and the low foliage gives small animals cover close to the ground.

Where it comes from

Its native range spans the central and eastern United States into Mexico, Guatemala and the Caribbean. In Florida it occurs naturally in habitats including flatwoods, marshes, coastal uplands and dunes.

Where to find it here

Along the pond banks

The park is planting Muhly Grass around all three ponds as part of its pond preservation project — the dense, fibrous root system helps hold the banks in place. Look for the clumps of narrow, wiry leaves right at the water's edge.

What it does here

Bank stabilization, not just fall color

Beyond the fall flower display, Muhly Grass is doing real work here: its dense root mass helps keep pond banks from eroding, the same reason it's planted this way.

A plant with a strong seasonal switch — for much of the year Muhly Grass reads as a neat green mound. In fall, the flower stalks extend well above the leaves and change the plant's entire outline. That makes it useful for showing visitors that grasses can have a conspicuous flowering season even when the individual flowers are tiny.

How to know it

Start with the clump

The leaves rise in a dense bunch rather than from a creeping lawn-like mat.

Look above the leaves in fall

The flower panicles are open, finely branched and pink to purple, giving the top of the plant a smoky appearance.

Narrow, wiry leaves

The blades are long, slender and somewhat stiff.

The flowers are the texture

There is no single large blossom to find. The effect comes from hundreds of tiny flowers spread across a very open panicle.

How it grows

Sun brings the best flowering

UF/IFAS recommends full sun for the strongest fall display, although the species tolerates a range of Florida conditions.

Established plants handle dry periods well

Despite occurring naturally in some wet habitats, established Muhly Grass is drought tolerant in landscapes.

Cut back, then let it rebuild

UF/IFAS notes that plants can be cut back in late winter before the next spring flush.
