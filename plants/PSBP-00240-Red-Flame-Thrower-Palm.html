Red Flame Thrower Palm

Chambeyronia macrocarpa

At a glance

  • The red may be sunscreen. A new frond comes out too soft to protect itself, and the pigment that makes it red is thought to shield the tissue from strong light until it hardens off and turns green. Thought to - nobody has pinned it down for this particular palm.
  • You will probably miss it. A new leaf holds its colour only briefly before going green with the rest of the crown, and it happens on no schedule you can plan around.
  • Its name means large fruit, and it earns it — egg-sized, ripening deep red, nearly as showy as the leaves that made the palm famous.
  • Even the red is not guaranteed. This is a variable species, and one recognised variety opens its new leaves green - which rather undercuts the name.

Where it comes from

Worth knowing

The genus name encodes a specific historical debt: botanist Eugène Vieillard named Chambeyronia to honor Charles Marie-Léon Chambeyron, the French naval officer who mapped much of the New Caledonian coastline and helped Vieillard collect specimens from the island's interior forests.

New Caledonia, and nowhere else

It grows wild only in New Caledonia, in humid forest from close to sea level up into the hills, and it is not fussy about the soil it does it in. Plenty of New Caledonian palms are down to one valley and hanging on. This one is not - it is among the more widespread palms on the island.

Why a new leaf might need protecting

In that forest it grows anywhere from below the canopy to standing clear above it. A leaf opening in the second position takes far more light than one unfurling in shade, which is the usual explanation offered for the red. It is a good explanation. It is not a proven one.

Where to find it here

Back among the Royal Palms

Look for it in the Royal Palm area. That is where the park puts collection palms that want to stay out of full sun, and the Royals give it the shelter a young rainforest palm appreciates in Bradenton heat.

What it does here

Mostly you will miss it

The red does not last. Most days this is a handsome green feather palm and nothing more; then a new frond opens burgundy, red or hot pink and the name suddenly makes sense. Worth a look at the crown every time you pass, because there is no telling you when.

How to know it

Look at the newest leaf

Everything about this palm is in the leaf that has just opened. If one is red, burgundy or hot pink while everything around it is green, you have found it.

Heavier leaflets than you expect

The leaflets are unusually broad, thick and leathery, which gives the whole crown a more solid look than most feather palms carry.

The collar below the crown

A smooth, slightly swollen crownshaft sits where the fronds meet the trunk. Its colour is not fixed - deep green to yellow-green, and some cultivated forms are strikingly striped green and yellow.

Fruit worth a second look

When it fruits, nearly egg-sized drupes hang in clusters just under the crownshaft, green ripening to deep red. Macrocarpa means large fruit, and the name is earned. Not known as a human food.

How it grows

Not a fast palm here

It takes its time in Florida - twenty to twenty-five feet eventually, on a single trunk, considerably taller in a true tropical climate. Nothing about this palm is in a hurry.

Warmth, water, shelter

It wants steady moisture and will not forgive a long dry spell, and a hard freeze is out of the question. Young plants want shade; established ones will take a good deal more sun than you would guess.
