At a glance

  • A wild cousin of the palm that gives us supermarket dates. Its own dates are small and thin-fleshed, but edible.
  • The lowest leaflets on every frond are stiff, needle-sharp spines.
  • The big clump between the restrooms and the Rare Fruit garden shades the walk over from the Butterfly Garden.

Cultural significance

Wine, mats and dates

Across its African range the sap is tapped for palm wine, the leaves are woven into mats and baskets, and the small dates are eaten fresh.

Where it comes from

It is native to most of Africa south of the Sahara, from Senegal to Ethiopia and down to South Africa. There it lines rivers and streams and the edges of forest, where the ground stays damp.

Where to find it here

Walk from the Butterfly Garden toward the Rare Fruit garden. The big clump between the restrooms and Rare Fruit throws welcome shade across the way.

A young clump

Outside the office, on the island planted with bromeliads, as you head toward the crushed-shell path.

Outside the fence

Several more stand along 17th Avenue NW and 99th Street NW.

What it does

One palm, many trunks

A clump that looks like a small grove is most likely a single palm. Each one keeps sending up new trunks from its base, so the big clump near Rare Fruit has been building itself for years.

When the dates ripen, birds work through the clumps for them, and the tangle of trunks and old fronds gives them cover while they do.

How to know it

Count the trunks

Several slender trunks rise from one base and lean outward, each with its own crown of arching feather fronds. That reclining habit gave the palm its name.

Compare with

The Canary Island Date Palms outside the fence are the same family of date palms grown the other way: one massive trunk each, with no clump.

Pure or mixed?

Date palms cross easily with one another, and we suspect many of ours are hybrids. A pure Reclining Date Palm makes a clump of many slender trunks; a cross often has fewer, thicker trunks and a heavier crown. Look closely and decide for yourself.

Dates that darken

When it fruits, the dates start yellow-orange and darken toward a deep purple-brown as they ripen.

How it grows

New shoots keep rising from the base, so a clump thickens for decades. Left alone it becomes a thicket; gardeners often thin it to a few trunks to show off the lean.

Tough for a tropical look

Once established it handles drought, wet ground and some salt spray, and it takes more cold than most palms with this look. That toughness is why it turns up along roads and parking lots all over Florida.

Take care

Spines low on every frond

The spines sit at the base of each frond, right where a hand goes to pull a weed or fetch a ball. Look before you reach into the clump.
