Spindle Palm

Hyophorbe verschaffeltii

At a glance

  • The trunk gives this palm its name: it widens through the middle and narrows again toward the top, like a spindle.
  • It is native to Rodrigues, a small island in the Indian Ocean, where about fifty were left in the wild when the species was last assessed in 2021.
  • We have two: a mature one on Welcome Island in front of the Galleria, and a younger one on the walkway along the south side of Big Pond.
  • Its close relative the Bottle Palm swells near the base instead. Seeing the two forms is the easiest way to learn them both.

Where it comes from

One island

Spindle Palm is endemic to Rodrigues, a small island about 350 miles east of Mauritius in the Indian Ocean; Kew lists that one island as its entire native range. It is Critically Endangered there, with about fifty wild plants counted in 2021, while it is a common garden palm across the tropics.

Where to find it here

Welcome Island

The larger of our two Spindle Palms stands on Welcome Island, the island the driveway circles in front of the Galleria, beside a Screw Pine. It is mature enough to flower, so this is the one to check for stalks and fruit below the crownshaft.

Beside Big Pond

The second grows along the walkway on the south side of Big Pond, on the pond side of the path. Palms planted a few feet apart line this stretch as the walk heads toward the southeast corner and the bridge into the Rare Fruit Garden, which the Manatee Rare Fruit Council tends.

To compare with Bottle Palm

Our Bottle Palms grow at the Back Gate, by the nursery entrance, and in the Butterfly Garden. The spindle shape is there year-round.

What it does here

Right on the bank

The Big Pond palm grows close enough to the water that protecting the bank protects the palm. The rock along the shoreline was bought by a longtime volunteer and set in place by park volunteers and high-school students; new bank plantings, supported by the Tampa Bay and Sarasota Bay estuary programs, are the next step in slowing erosion here.

How to know it

The spindle

Start with the gray, ringed trunk. It widens above the base, is broadest around the middle of its height, then narrows again as it reaches the crownshaft.

The crownshaft

Above the gray trunk is a smooth, waxy green column two to three feet tall, the crownshaft, formed by the tightly wrapped leaf bases.

Flowers and fruit

On a mature palm, heavily branched flower stalks up to two and a half feet long ring the trunk just below the crownshaft. The fruit is about three quarters of an inch across and turns orange, then red, and finally black when fully ripe.

Compare the silhouette

Bottle Palm, a close relative, carries its swelling low on the trunk. Spindle Palm's bulge is stretched higher and through the middle.

How it grows

Built for warm weather

Spindle Palm is a slow-growing tropical palm that does best in sun and well-drained soil, reaching twenty to twenty-five feet on a single trunk under a crown of feather-shaped fronds. It is sensitive to freezing, though it takes a frost better than Bottle Palm does.

One palm can fruit

Male and female flowers occur on the same plant, so a single mature Spindle Palm can produce fruit on its own.
