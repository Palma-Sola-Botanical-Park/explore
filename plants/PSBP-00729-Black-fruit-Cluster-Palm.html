Black-fruit Cluster Palm

Ptychosperma lineare
On this page

At a glance

  • Its leaflets end in ragged tips, as if something bit them off. That is a mark of its genus.
  • The fruit ripens glossy black, where many palms ripen red or orange.
  • It grows as a clump of slender stems, adding new ones from the base.
  • The name Ptychosperma means folded seed, for the deeply grooved seed inside each fruit.
  • It is vulnerable in the wild: its whole range is one corner of southeastern New Guinea, and logging and mining are taking the forest there.

Where to find it here

Turn left at the entrance and follow the fence. The shaded bed on your right, under the Wild Lime and full of crotons, runs from the Pavilion Field to the gazebo by the pavilion. This palm anchors it.

Where it comes from

It comes from the lowland wet forests of southeastern New Guinea, where it grows in swampy ground under taller trees, on flood plains, and even at the edge of coastal mangroves. In the wild its stems reach anywhere from sixteen to fifty feet.

Vulnerable at home

That one corner of New Guinea is its whole range, and logging and mining are taking the forest there, so it is listed as vulnerable in the wild.

What the names say

Ptychosperma, folded seed, describes the grooved seed hidden in each fruit. Lineare, narrow, describes the slim leaflets.

What it does

The palm in the middle of the bed

It rises out of the crotons, Wild Lime and Thryallis around it, thin ringed trunks climbing through the shade.

Its red-fruited cousin, next door

Right beside it in the same bed stands a Macarthur Palm, the same genus with the same ringed stems and bitten-off leaflets, but red fruit. When either clump is carrying fruit, the color is the quickest way to tell which is which.

A relative to compare

Scheffer's Palm, in the same genus, grows in the row of palms south of the Plumeria. Compare the two clumps: the same family look, slender ringed stems and bitten-off leaflets, with different leaves and habit.

How to know it

Several thin trunks

A clump of slender, ringed stems of different heights, each topped with a smooth crownshaft and a few arching feather leaves.

Check the leaflet ends

The narrow leaflets end in jagged, chopped-off tips. Botanists call this praemorse, bitten-off, and it is one of the surest signs of the genus.

Black fruit

When it fruits, small oval fruits ripen from green to glossy black, hanging in clusters below the crownshafts.

How it grows

New stems keep rising from the base, so the clump fills in over time, with young stems coming up among the older ones.

Made for shade

It does well in part shade and steady moisture, the conditions of its forest home. It comes from a place with no winter, so cold is the risk here.
