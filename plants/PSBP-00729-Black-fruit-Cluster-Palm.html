Black-fruit Cluster Palm

Ptychosperma lineare
On this page
At a glanceWhere it comes fromWhere to find it hereWhat it does hereHow to know itHow it grows

At a glance

  • Its leaflets end in ragged tips, as if something bit them off. That is a mark of its genus.
  • The fruit ripens glossy black, where many palms ripen red or orange.
  • It grows as a clump of slender stems, adding new ones from the base.

Where it comes from

It comes from the lowland wet forests of southeastern New Guinea.

Where to find it here

Turn left at the entrance and follow the fence. The shaded bed on your right, under the Wild Lime and full of crotons, runs from the Pavilion Field to the gazebo by the pavilion. This palm anchors it.

What it does here

The palm in the middle of the bed

It rises out of the crotons, Wild Lime and Thryallis around it, the one vertical line in a low, shady planting.

A relative to compare

Scheffer's Palm, in the same genus, grows in the row of palms south of the Plumeria. Compare the two clumps: the same family look, with different leaves and habit.

How to know it

Several thin trunks

A clump of slender, ringed stems, each topped with a smooth crownshaft and a few arching feather leaves.

Check the leaflet ends

The narrow leaflets end in jagged, chopped-off tips.

Black fruit

When it fruits, small oval fruits ripen from green to glossy black.

How it grows

New stems keep rising from the base, so the clump fills in over time. In the wild it grows in wet, swampy forest.
