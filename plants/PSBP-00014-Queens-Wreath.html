Queen's Wreath

Petrea volubilis
On this page

At a glance

  • The darker purple flower drops quickly, but the pale five-pointed calyx behind it stays for weeks.
  • The leaves are rough enough to explain the other name for it, Sandpaper Vine.
  • The long hanging clusters look like wisteria, which it is not related to at all.
  • As the calyx dries it can carry the fruit off on the wind.

Cultural significance

Leaves as an abrasive

In South America the Wayãpi have used the coarse leaves to smooth gourds and wood — the plant's most obvious identifying feature put to work as a tool.

Where it comes from

Native across a wide band of the tropical Americas, from the Caribbean and Mexico through Central America into South America, in seasonal forests, along waterways and on rocky ground.

Where to find it here

By the Office.

When

The main flush is spring into early summer, with another possible later in the year. Even once the dark flowers have dropped, the pale stars keep the vine looking purple.

What it does here

Bees, butterflies and hummingbirds visit the true flowers while they are open — which is a shorter window than the colour on the vine suggests.

How to know it

Feel a leaf

The broad dark-green leaves are rough on both surfaces, close to coarse sandpaper.

Two shades of purple

Darker purple petals sit inside a paler lavender star. The dark flower goes first; the star stays.

Vine

The stems turn woody and twine around a support rather than gripping with tendrils or clinging roots.

From a distance the drooping purple clusters suggest wisteria. Up close, the rough leaves and the five-pointed stars settle it.

How it grows

A display in two stages

Each flower has a short-lived purple corolla inside a longer-lasting coloured calyx. The petals fall, the calyx stays on and slowly fades — which stretches the visible display far beyond the life of the flower itself.

A vine needs something to climb

It grows fast as a woody twiner and will want a substantial arbour or fence as it matures.

Flowering is heaviest in full sun. Established plants take dry spells, but they grow best where the roots drain.
