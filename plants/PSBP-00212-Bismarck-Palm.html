Bismarck Palm

Bismarckia nobilis

At a glance

  • The silver-blue is not a pigment. It is a coat of wax over the whole fan, sunscreen against a Madagascar sun.
  • Male and female flowers grow on separate palms. A female on her own never sets fruit.
  • The only species in its genus, and native only to Madagascar.
  • Ours is young: chest high, with no trunk yet. The park's big one, on Welcome Island, died, and this one was planted farther south in 2026 to start again.

Cultural significance

Satrana

In Madagascar it is satrana, a working palm as much as a handsome one: the leaves thatch roofs and are woven, and the trunk is split for building.

A chancellor's palm

The genus was named in 1881 by two German botanists for Otto von Bismarck, chancellor of the newly united Germany. The palm has carried a chancellor's name ever since.

Where it comes from

Madagascar

Native to the north and west of Madagascar, in open, seasonally dry grassland that burns. It is one of the island's many palms found nowhere else in the wild.

Where to find it here

The palm row south of Cypress Pond

Bismarck Palm grows in the row of unusual palms just south of our Plumeria collection, on the south side of Cypress Pond, roughly halfway along the row. Scheffer's Palm, a clump of glossy green feather fronds, is right beside it.

Go ahead and get closer

There is no formal path through this palm collection yet. You are welcome to walk across the open grass to see the palms and read their signs. Watch for low fronds and other plantings as you go.

What it does here

Starting again

For years a big Bismarck Palm stood on Welcome Island, the first thing people saw as they drove in: a broad silver crown on a stout trunk, the palm many visitors still remember. It died. In 2026 a longtime volunteer planted this one in the palm row to the south, and it is chest high. A mature Bismarck carries its crown thirty feet up or more, so in twenty years this page will be describing a different palm. Watch it.

How to know it

Silver fans

Stiff, waxy fan leaves, silver-blue on both sides, each cut into many pointed segments. On a mature palm a single fan is several feet across.

Teeth on the stalk

The long leaf stalk carries small teeth along its edges, and it runs a little way into the blade, so each fan folds into a shallow V rather than lying flat.

No trunk yet

A young Bismarck sits as a rosette of fans at ground level for years before any trunk shows. That is where ours is. When it comes, the trunk is stout and grey.

How it grows

Sun and drainage

Full sun and well-drained ground. Once established it takes drought well.

Slow, then steady, and big

Slow while it is trunkless, faster once the trunk starts. Thirty to sixty feet tall in the end, with a crown twelve to sixteen feet across, on one trunk: it never clumps.

Silver is the hardy one

The common silver form takes a light frost. A rarer green-leaved form exists and is more tender.
