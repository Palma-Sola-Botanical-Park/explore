Shell Gingers

At a glance

  • The flower moves. Through the day the style, the stalk in the middle, curls up or down, and two kinds of plant take turns being male and then female, so a bee almost always carries pollen from one kind to the other. Botanists call it flexistyly. Nothing else in the park does it.
  • The buds hang in a drooping string of waxy pink shells, and each opens for a single day into a white flower with a yellow, red-streaked throat.
  • In Okinawa the leaves wrap rice cakes and go into tea. The islanders live a long time and the leaves are full of antioxidants, which may or may not be connected, but people like to say so.
  • Flowers come only on canes that are at least two years old and were not knocked back by cold. A mild winter means a show; a hard freeze means waiting.

Cultural significance

A kitchen plant in the islands

In the Ryukyu Islands almost every part is used: leaves to wrap and steam food, leaf tea, the rhizome as a spice, the fibre for paper. The genus is named for Prospero Alpino, a sixteenth-century Venetian physician who first told Europe what a coffee plant looked like.

Where it comes from

East Asia

Southern Japan and Taiwan down through southern China into Southeast Asia, on moist shady forest edges and stream banks. The one by our office is the variegated form, its leaves striped yellow, which is the one most people grow in Florida.

Where to find it here

By the office. A knee-to-waist-high clump of broad yellow-striped leaves on upright canes, with the flower strings hanging from the tips of the older canes in spring and summer.

What it does here

Nectar by day and by night

Bees work the flowers, and in the warm months hummingbirds visit them. In its home islands hawk moths come after dark, when the nectar is strongest, and Florida’s sphinx moths can do the same. The dense clump of canes is cover for lizards and frogs, and the broad leaves funnel rain to the roots, so the ground under it stays damp.

How to know it

Shell-pink buds in a string

Waxy, pink, pointed, hanging in a loose string from the arched tip of a leafy cane. Open one and there is a white tube with a yellow lip streaked red.

Long ginger-scented leaves on canes

Broad, long, in two rows along a cane, and they smell of ginger when crushed. There is no trunk; the tall canes are soft stems from a creeping rhizome.

How it grows

A little shade in the afternoon, moist soil that drains, and it spreads sideways from the rhizome into a broad clump that can be cut and divided. Green forms reach eight or ten feet; the variegated one stays under five. Cold browns it and the flowers skip a year.
