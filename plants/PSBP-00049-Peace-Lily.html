Peace Lily

At a glance

  • The white flag is a leaf, not a petal. The real flowers are the tiny bumps packed along the spike beside it: in one wild peace lily that was counted, about 140 on a single spike.
  • In the early 1980s, University of Florida researchers in Apopka found that one spray of a plant hormone makes a peace lily bloom about three months later, in any season. Growers adopted the trick, and it is one reason potted peace lilies flower all year.
  • A 1989 NASA test found that a peace lily could clear about 80 percent of a polluting chemical, benzene, from a sealed box in a day. A real room is another matter. There it does almost nothing: later studies figured it would take at least 8 plants on every square yard of floor, and perhaps hundreds, to match what ordinary ventilation already does.

Cultural significance

Before the spray

Before the Apopka spray, a breeder could make crosses only three or four months of the year. Afterward growers could flower plants on demand, and more than forty named kinds followed in about twenty years. The spray has costs: some flowers come out distorted, and the leaves that follow can be narrow and strap-like.

One cultivar, one chamber

NASA's test used a single kind, 'Mauna Loa', under grow lights, in a sealed chamber a few feet across, for 24 hours. The same report tested a long list of other houseplants.

Where it comes from

Rainforest floors

Wild peace lilies grow in the rainforests of tropical America, from Mexico to Peru and Brazil, and a few species grow in the Philippines, Sulawesi, New Guinea and nearby Pacific islands. Many live on humid, shady forest floors, often near streams.

Which one is this?

A peace lily in a garden is usually a bred hybrid or selection, and nobody tracks its parentage, so this page names only the genus, Spathiphyllum.

What it does here

Who visits the flowers

In Mexico, two wild species were watched through a flowering season, and pollen-collecting honeybees and stingless bees did most of the pollinating. Male orchid bees, which collect the flowers' scent, visit some other species. Which insects visit the plants here has not been recorded.

A relative of Xanadu

Xanadu, also in the park, belongs to the same family, the aroids, where the tiny flowers all crowd onto a spike inside a leafy hood.

How to know it

A clump of pleated leaves

Long-stalked leaves rise in a clump from ground level. Each blade is glossy and tapers to a point, with a strong midrib and curving side veins that make it look pleated.

An aging flag

A white spathe turns pale green after about ten days and stays another month or more, so a green flag on a plant is an old flower.

Size depends on the kind

Peace lilies run from a foot or so tall to more than five feet, depending on the selection, with spathes from about four to twelve inches long.

How it grows

Bred to bloom more

In nature a peace lily flowers in spring and early summer. Growers have bred and forced selections that flower several times a year.

More of the same

A clump can be divided to make new plants. Growers multiply a named selection in the laboratory from tiny bits of tissue so that every plant matches its parent; plants grown from seed vary.

Take care

Not to eat

Do not chew or eat any part. The leaves and flowers hold calcium oxalate crystals, which cause burning and pain in the mouth and throat, drooling and vomiting.

The plant is also toxic to dogs and cats. Call a vet if a pet chews it.
