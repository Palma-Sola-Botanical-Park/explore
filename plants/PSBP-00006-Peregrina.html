Peregrina

Jatropha integerrima

At a glance

  • A shrub that seems to always have another round of red flowers coming. Most months of the year, some are open.
  • Look along one branch: the leaves may be plain ovals, pinched in the middle like a fiddle, or cut into three lobes, all on the same plant.
  • Butterflies and hummingbirds work the star-shaped flowers.
  • Pretty does not mean edible. Every part of it is poisonous if swallowed, the seeds most of all.

Where it comes from

Western Cuba

Peregrina grows wild only in western Cuba, including the Isle of Youth. From there it has been planted through the tropics, and in South Florida it is one of the most common flowering shrubs in yards and parking lots. The name integerrima means "most whole", for leaves without lobes, which, given the leaves, was a botanist catching it on a good day.

Where to find it here

Two of them

Two of them. One stands on the Front Pond side of the park, beside the drive that leads toward the nursery. The other is on the Welcome Island, the island the driveway circles in front of the Galleria, growing beside the Plumbago.

What it does

Flowers when little else does

It can bloom through much of the year here, so butterflies and hummingbirds get a nectar stop in the months when the seasonal plants are between flushes. Stand by it for a minute and see who comes.

How to know it

Red star-shaped flowers

Five petals, about an inch across, in clusters at the ends of the branches, held up above the leaves.

Leaves that will not pick a shape

Some are plain ovals, some narrow in the middle, some grow three lobes. Young leaves are bronze and soft; older ones are glossy dark green.

Several trunks

Left alone it grows well above head height as a shrub or small tree, with a few grey trunks rising from one base.

How it grows

Heat suits it

It thrives in the warm months and flowers hardest in full sun. A frost can damage it, and it comes back.

Dry spells are fine

Once established it takes dry spells well. It wants good drainage more than water.

Pruning keeps it in bounds

Without trimming it grows into a substantial shrub or a small multi-trunked tree. It flowers on new growth, so cutting it back costs nothing.

Take care

Not for eating

Every part is poisonous if swallowed, the seeds most of all. Cut stems bleed a milky sap that irritates skin and eyes.

Dogs

The seeds and sap are toxic to dogs too. Keep them from chewing it.
