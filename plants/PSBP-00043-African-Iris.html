African Iris

Dietes bicolor
On this page

At a glance

  • Each flower lasts a day or two, but the plant keeps sending up more, so a clump blooms on and off through the warm months.
  • Wild plants grow on stream banks in South Africa's Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal provinces, so the wet edge of a pond is much like home.
  • Gardens around the world grow it, but in South Africa, its home, wild plants are scarce.
  • One of its closest relatives lives only on Lord Howe Island, a speck in the Tasman Sea between Australia and New Zealand. How an iris of this kind got there, no one knows.

Where it comes from

Streams of the Eastern Cape

In the wild it grows along streams in South Africa, from the Eastern Cape into southern KwaZulu-Natal.

Where to find it here

South end of Front Pond, near the viewing bench

Planted by Barney in 2023 along the south end of Front Pond, near the viewing bench.

The row runs about ten yards along the bank.

What it does

Erosion control before it had a name

Barney planted this row to help with pond erosion before that work became an official project backed by grant funding from local estuary programs. The long row also makes the species easy to compare as a clump-forming landscape plant rather than as one isolated specimen.

How to know it

Swordlike leaves

Flat narrow leaves rise in fans from the base.

Pale yellow flowers

Look for three broad outer segments and dark spots near the flower center.

Clumping form

New shoots arise from rhizomes, gradually widening the stand.

How it grows

Tough once established

It tolerates sun or part shade and handles both wet spells and dry periods better than its iris-like appearance suggests.

Wet feet, not salt

It takes wet ground in stride; salty ground is another matter.
