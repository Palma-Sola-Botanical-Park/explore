Pemba Palm

At a glance

  • Pemba Palm is native only to Pemba Island off the coast of Tanzania.
  • It grows as a clump of slender, strongly ringed stems rather than one massive trunk.
  • Ripe fruit turns red and hangs below the crownshaft.
  • This species now sits in Chrysalidocarpus, as C. pembanus — a real nomenclatural change, not just a genus swap, though the park's record keeps Dypsis pembana. It's listed as Vulnerable.

Where it comes from

Pemba Island, Tanzania

This species is endemic to Pemba Island, Tanzania, growing in low-lying wet tropical forest at or near sea level — its entire natural range is one island.

Where to find it here

Big Pond walkway, 3rd from the bridge

On the south walkway along Big Pond, the third palm from the Rare Fruit bridge — Satake, then Açaí, then this one.

Look for the clump

Among nearby solitary palms, several slim green stems rising together provide the fastest first clue.

What it does here

One island in one clump

The park's palm collection includes species with enormous continental ranges and others confined naturally to tiny areas. Pemba Palm represents the latter: its entire native distribution is Pemba Island.

How to know it

Several slender stems

The palm suckers from the base and builds a clump of narrow trunks.

Strong rings

The green to pale stems show conspicuous horizontal leaf-scar rings.

A clear crownshaft

Each stem ends in a smooth green crownshaft below the feather-shaped leaves.

Red fruit

Mature fruit is bright red and becomes a strong identification clue when present.

How it grows

A lowland forest palm

Its native habitat is warm, humid and close to sea level, so this is not a cool high-elevation species despite its relatively tidy landscape form.

Clumps renew themselves

Basal shoots add new stems as the plant expands, allowing an established clump to contain stems of different ages.

A restricted wild range

The species is classified as Vulnerable.
