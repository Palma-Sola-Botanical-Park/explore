At a glance

  • The houseplant of the last decade, and this tree by the office, are the same species. Kept in a pot it stays polite. Put it in a Florida garden and it becomes a real tree.
  • The leaves are the size of a dinner platter, up to fifteen inches long, pinched in at the waist like a fiddle. That is the whole name.
  • A fig has no visible flower. The fig is a hollow ball with the flowers inside, and only one kind of wasp, from West Africa, can get in to pollinate them. That wasp is not here, so ours will never set a seed.
  • Snap a leaf and it bleeds white. The sap can raise a rash.

Where to find it here

By the office: a tree of fifteen feet or so with several grey trunks, big leathery leaves, and a bed of bromeliads and a birdbath at its feet.

Where it comes from

West Africa’s rainforest

Lowland rainforest in West and Central Africa, from Sierra Leone to Gabon, where it starts life as a seedling in another tree’s branches and sends roots down to the ground. It came to Florida as an ornamental and, after the houseplant craze of the 2010s, went into a great many yards, where it turns into a shade tree.

What it does

The windowsill plant at full size

Shade, mostly, and a surprise for anyone who has one on a windowsill at home. It will not fruit here, so it feeds nothing, and it cannot seed itself about, which makes it one of the safest big-leaved tropical trees to plant in Florida. New shoots break straight from the old trunks, bright green against the grey.

How to know it

Fiddle-shaped leaves

Broad, dark, glossy, deeply veined, with a narrowed waist. Among the biggest leaves on any tree in the park.

Pale veins

Cream-coloured veins stand out against the dark blade, above and below.

Milky sap

Any cut weeps white latex. Ours does not fruit; if it ever did, the figs would be green with pale flecks, going red.

How it grows

Warmth and room. It takes sun or part shade, wants drainage, and dislikes wet feet. Twenty-five feet is common in a Florida garden and fifty is possible. The branch junctions can be tight and split in a storm, so it is best out of the wind and pruned early to open them up.

Take care

Keep the sap off your skin and out of your eyes when pruning. Not for eating, and dogs that chew the leaves get a sore mouth and an upset stomach.
