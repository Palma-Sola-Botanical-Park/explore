Brazilian Dutchman's Pipe

Aristolochia gigantea
On this page

At a glance

  • Its flower is a big patterned pouch, burgundy netted with ivory veins, curved like an old smoking pipe.
  • The flowers can be about a foot long; the species name gigantea isn't an exaggeration.
  • Many people say the flower smells like old lemon furniture polish. The scent draws the small flies that pollinate it.
  • It climbs from the nursery fence about thirty feet up into the shade tree above, so look up.

Cultural significance

Best childbirth

The genus name Aristolochia means best childbirth. Pipevines were used in childbirth medicine in ancient Greece, a use we now know was dangerous: the acid they carry damages the kidneys.

Pipes and pelicans

The common name sees the bowl and stem of an old Dutch tobacco pipe in the curved flower. Another name, pelican flower, sees a bird's pouch in the same shape.

Where to find it here

At the nursery: it grows along the nursery fence and climbs into the shade tree over it. In season it flowers all the way up through the branches.

Where it comes from

It comes from the forests of eastern and southern Brazil, where it twines its way up into the trees.

What it does

A host and a trap

Its leaves feed the caterpillars of the Polydamas Swallowtail, a black butterfly with a band of yellow spots, found in South Florida and the tropics. The native Pipevine Swallowtail isn't so lucky: females sometimes lay eggs on this vine, mistaking it for their own host, but the caterpillars die young on its leaves.

Look up

Most people see the flowers at fence height and stop there. In season the vine blooms all the way up the tree and out along the branches, thirty feet over your head.

How to know it

The pouch

The flower has no true petals. The patterned pouch is a swollen, colored tube of sepals, with a narrow opening leading inside.

Heart-shaped leaves

Light green, heart-shaped leaves alternate along twining stems.

Ribbed pods

After a flower is pollinated, a ribbed capsule forms. It dries, splits open and scatters winged seeds on the wind.

How it grows

Inside the flower

Flies follow the scent into the pouch and are held there for a while by downward-pointing hairs. The flower is female first, so a fly carrying pollen from another flower can pollinate it. Then it turns male, dusts the fly with fresh pollen, and the hairs wither to let it out.

Twining up

The vine climbs by twining its stems around whatever it can reach, which is how it gets from a fence to the top of a tree. It grows fast in warm weather and stays evergreen here.

Take care

Poisonous

Every part contains aristolochic acid, which can seriously damage the kidneys. Don't eat any part of it, and keep pets from chewing it.
