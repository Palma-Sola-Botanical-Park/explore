Teddy Bear Palm

At a glance

  • The crownshaft, the smooth sleeve where the fronds meet the trunk, is covered in dense rust-red fuzz. That is the teddy bear.
  • Below it the trunk is pale, smooth and ringed, so the fuzzy collar stands out all the more.
  • It grows wild in one small part of northwestern Madagascar, and the IUCN listed it Critically Endangered when it was assessed in 2012. It is far commoner in gardens than at home.
  • Ours is on the Big Pond walkway, the fifth palm along from the Rare Fruit bridge.

Where it comes from

Northwestern Madagascar

Native to a small area of northwestern Madagascar, where very few wild palms remain. The IUCN assessed it as Critically Endangered in 2012. Most of the world's Teddy Bear Palms are in gardens and collections, grown from seed passed between palm enthusiasts.

A name that moved

Kew now accepts the name Chrysalidocarpus leptocheilos. Dypsis leptocheilos, the name on most labels and on ours, is treated as a synonym.

Where to find it here

Big Pond, south walkway

Take the walkway along the south side of Big Pond. Counting from the bridge into the Rare Fruit Garden at the southeast corner, Teddy Bear Palm is the fifth palm on the pond side: Satake Palm, Açaí Palm, Pemba Palm, Vanuatu Palm, then Teddy Bear Palm, with the Mountain Fishtail Palm next after it.

Look for the collar

From a distance it is the young feather palm with the orange-brown collar.

What it does here

A walk of rare palms

This stretch of walkway is lined with unusual palms bought and planted over many years by a longtime volunteer, most of them never labelled for visitors until now. Teddy Bear Palm is among the rarest of them: a species with a tiny wild range, growing here beside palms from Vanuatu, Brazil and Pemba Island.

See it while it is low

It is still young. As it grows the trunk lengthens and the fuzzy collar rises out of reach, so this is the time to see it up close.

How to know it

Find the fuzz

The crownshaft is felted in reddish-brown hairs, thick enough to look like fur. No other palm on the walkway has it.

Then look down

One trunk, pale grey to white and smooth, with a ring for every frond it has dropped.

A feather crown

Long arching fronds with narrow leaflets: a softer, more open crown than the stiff fan palms nearby.

The look-alike

Redneck Palm, a close relative, is sold under the same name and the two are often mixed up in nurseries. Redneck Palm has a thicker trunk and a deeper red collar. Here the collar is rust brown.

How it grows

Sun and warmth

Full sun to light shade, in warmth. It is tender to cold, and a hard freeze burns the fronds.

It cleans itself

Old fronds let go at the crownshaft and fall whole, leaving a ring on the trunk. There is never a skirt of dead leaves.

Size

Twenty to thirty feet in cultivation, on a single trunk, at a slow to moderate pace.
