At a glance

  • Red petals tipped in yellow around a dark center: firewheel is tough enough to thrive in beach sand, and it reseeds itself with almost no help.
  • For generations it was treated as a Florida native. A 2020 review of the old plant records concluded it is probably an introduction east of Texas, though it has been growing wild in Florida for about a hundred years.
  • The big blanket flowers sold at garden centers are usually a cross between this plant and a western cousin, first made in 1857. This is the plain original.

Where it comes from

The Great Plains

Its home ground is the south-central United States, from the Great Plains into northern Mexico. It reached Florida long enough ago to become thoroughly at home, and for years it was planted on roadsides and in coastal gardens as a native wildflower. A botanist described it growing wild along the Halifax River, near Daytona Beach, in 1929. A 2020 paper in a series on the flora of the southeastern United States went back through the old plant records and concluded that east of Texas it is an introduction, and the state plant atlas now lists it as not native.

Where to find it here

Butterfly Garden

In the Butterfly Garden, in open sunny ground. Look for the red-and-yellow daisies about a foot off the ground.

What it does here

Native is messier than it sounds

Firewheel was sold and planted for years as a Florida native. It still does everything a pollinator garden asks of it. Its origin story changed once botanists looked closely, and this bed is a good place to think about what "native" means.

Nectar, in season

Bees and butterflies visit the flowers through the long bloom, and the plant asks very little in return: no watering to speak of, no rich soil, no replanting.

How to know it

Red centers, yellow tips

The rays look dipped in yellow paint. The center is a dark red-brown dome.

Low, loose growth

It branches freely instead of making one tall stem.

Round seed heads

Spent flowers dry into bristly balls packed with seed.

How it grows

Poor soil is fine

Sand, heat and drought bother it less than rich wet ground.

It reseeds freely

Individual plants may not live long, but a patch keeps coming back from seed.

Full sun for the best flowers

In shade it gets lanky and flowers less.

Garden blanket flowers

The larger blanket flowers at garden centers are usually Gaillardia x grandiflora, a hybrid between firewheel and a western species made in the 1850s. This is the plain species, smaller and looser, and it comes back from its own seed.
