Amazon Lily

At a glance

  • Growers have never gotten it to produce seed that grows, so every Amazon lily is a divided piece of an older plant, passed along.
  • This one has come down through the Baden family since the 1880s. A plaque on Bird of Paradise Island, the bed behind the office, tells the story.
  • The name misleads: it comes from the slopes of the Andes in Peru, not the Amazon lowlands.
  • After hurricane saltwater flooded its bed, it was cut back to bare stubs. It is growing again from the bulb.

Cultural significance

A living family heirloom

Divisions of this plant have been handed down through the Baden family since the late 1800s, and the park's Amazon Lily carries that heritage forward. A plaque on Bird of Paradise Island, facing the office, commemorates it.

Why a family line matters

A plant that never sets seed can travel only one way, in pieces. A Philadelphia seed catalog was already offering Amazon lilies in 1879, so a bulb in an American family by the 1880s fits the history.

Where it comes from

The Andes, not the Amazon

Sources differ on the details, but they agree it grows wild on the Andean slopes of Peru (some add Colombia), not in the Amazon lowlands its name suggests. A collector named Marius Porte found it near Moyobamba in 1855, and Linden named it the next year.

A name that moved

Botanists now file it under Urceolina, a genus that took in Eucharis in 2018, so you may see either name on a label. Eucharis is Greek for gracious.

Where to find it here

Bird of Paradise Island, facing the office

Look low beneath the much taller Bird of Paradise plants; the Amazon Lily is easy to overlook until it flowers. A commemorative plaque marks the spot.

What it does here

It refuses to give up

Saltwater flooding in recent years cut the old plant back to bare stubs, but in 2026 new growth is returning from the bulb, the latest chapter in a heritage that has already lasted well over a century.

How to know it

Glossy broad leaves

The leaves are dark green, broad and strongly arched.

White flowers on a stalk

Several white flowers can rise together above the foliage, each with a conspicuous central cup.

How it grows

Shade plant

This is a bulbous understory plant that prefers bright shade, moisture and protection from intense sun.

Flowering and weak spots

It blooms best slightly crowded, usually twice a year, and a dry spell can set off a new flush. Frost and salt both hurt it.
