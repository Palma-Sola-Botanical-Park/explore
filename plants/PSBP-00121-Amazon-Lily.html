Amazon Lily

Eucharis amazonica

At a glance

  • The plant's white, fragrant flowers rise above glossy leaves from an underground bulb.
  • The park specimen has been passed down through the Baden family since the late nineteenth century, according to the park's own historical record.
  • The name Eucharis amazonica, long used for this plant, is now treated as a synonym of Urceolina amazonica.

Cultural significance

A living family heirloom

Cuttings of this plant have been passed down through the Baden family since the late 1800s, and the park's Amazon Lily continues that heritage today. A plaque on Bird of Paradise Island, facing the office, commemorates it.

Where it comes from

It's a tropical South American amaryllis relative, adapted to humid forest conditions.

Where to find it here

Bird of Paradise Island, facing the office

Look low beneath the much taller Bird of Paradise plants; the Amazon Lily is easy to overlook until it flowers. A commemorative plaque marks the spot.

What it does here

It refuses to give up

Saltwater flooding in recent years shrank the old plant back severely, but in 2026 it's shown new growth returning from the bulb — the latest chapter in a heritage that's already outlasted a century and a half.

How to know it

Glossy broad leaves

The leaves are dark green, broad and strongly arched.

White flowers on a stalk

Several white flowers can rise together above the foliage, each with a conspicuous central cup.

How it grows

Shade plant

This is a bulbous understory plant that prefers bright shade, moisture and protection from intense sun.

Take care

The source safety gate is Yellow. Like other amaryllis-family bulbs, treat it as non-edible and keep pets from digging or chewing it.

Also known as

Eucharist Lily · Lily of the Amazon
