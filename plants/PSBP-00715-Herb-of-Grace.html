Herb-of-Grace

Bacopa monnieri

At a glance

  • White Peacock caterpillars eat this plant's leaves. A patch only a few inches high can help support a much more conspicuous butterfly.
  • A creeping stem can put down roots wherever a joint meets wet soil, spreading the plant sideways into a mat.
  • Herb-of-Grace can grow with its stems underwater. It is also grown in aquariums.

Cultural significance

Known as brahmi

In South Asia, this little wetland plant has a long history in Ayurvedic medicine, particularly in preparations associated with memory. That history now follows it onto bottles of supplements. The traditional use is well established; claims about what a supplement will do for someone's memory are a different matter.

Where it comes from

Herb-of-Grace is native to Florida and much of the world's tropics and subtropics. Here it grows along wetland edges, in swales and beside streams. A plant familiar in South Asian medicine can also belong naturally in a Florida wetland.

Where to find it here

Along the Front Pond fence

Leave the parking lot and follow the fence with the Front Pond on your left. About 30 yards along, look for the low patches of Herb-of-Grace on the north bank. You're welcome to walk around the pond even where there isn't a defined path.

What it does here

Helping hold the bank

Herb-of-Grace and Marsh Fleabane are part of our pond-edge planting with high school volunteers and help from the Sarasota Bay Estuary Program. You can see the erosion on this bank. The planting is part of our work to protect the pond edge: spreading stems and roots help cover and hold soil that would otherwise be exposed.

Food at two stages

We have White Peacock butterflies here. The adults can drink nectar from these small flowers, while their caterpillars need foliage to eat. Leaving low wetland plants room to grow helps provide both kinds of food. A butterfly garden doesn't have to be a bed of tall flowers.

How to know it

Get low

Look for a loose green mat with short upright tips rising above the creeping stems. From standing height, the small flowers are easy to overlook.

Leaves in pairs

The leaves sit opposite one another along the stem. Each is fleshy and shaped like a tiny paddle, broad at the rounded tip and narrow where it joins the stem.

Flowers tucked between the leaves

The white to pale pink flowers grow on short stalks where a leaf meets the stem. Each has five lobes and a violet center. Look closely without picking one; the flower is small enough to disappear among the leaves.

How it grows

Room to spread

Those rooted stems can join up into a broad patch wherever the ground stays moist. It grows best with plenty of sun and water, and can fill gaps quickly. A long dry spell checks that growth.

More than one flowering season

Flowers usually appear from spring through fall and may continue through winter in warm parts of Florida. The plant can stay green here when colder conditions farther north cause it to die back.

Take care

Bacopa preparations can cause nausea and stomach cramps. Leave the plants here for the butterflies rather than trying them as a remedy.
