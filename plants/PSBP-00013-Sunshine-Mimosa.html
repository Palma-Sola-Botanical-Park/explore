Sunshine Mimosa

Mimosa strigillosa
On this page

At a glance

  • Touch a leaf gently and the paired leaflets fold shut within seconds.
  • A Florida native that spreads into a mat only a few inches tall.
  • Bacteria living in its roots pull nitrogen out of the air into a form the plant can use.
  • Little Sulphur caterpillars feed on the foliage.

Where to find it here

At the Big Pond and the Front Pond.

When

The pink powder-puff flowers show through the warm growing season. In cool months the mat thins out and can look nearly gone before it returns.

Where it comes from

Native to Florida and the wider southeastern and south-central United States, on open sunny ground.

What it does

Holding the pond banks

The park uses Sunshine Mimosa on the pond banks, where creeping stems and roots cover bare ground and hold the soil. The plantings were supported by the Tampa Bay Estuary Program and the Sarasota Bay Estuary Program.

A caterpillar plant

Little Sulphur butterflies lay eggs here, so the caterpillars get leaves to eat — a different and scarcer thing than nectar for the adults.

Nitrogen underground

Like many legumes it works with bacteria in root nodules to fix nitrogen, which is how it manages on poor soil.

How to know it

Touch a leaf

Touch the paired leaflets and watch them fold. It is fast enough to use as a field mark.

Flowers

Small round pink-lavender heads held just above the mat, the long stamens giving the powder-puff look.

Growth form

Reddish creeping stems lie flat and root as they go.

Do not read the winter look as a dead planting. The mat goes sparse in its semi-dormant season and fills back in when growth resumes.

How it grows

Leaves that move

Touch triggers a rapid pressure change in specialised cells at the base of each leaflet, and the leaflet folds. The response is called thigmonasty.

Built to spread

Creeping stems and underground growth let a single planting widen into a broad mat, and deep roots carry established plants through dry spells. It spreads readily, so it wants a place where that is welcome or a barrier to hold it.

Sun or light shade, and soil anywhere from moderately dry to moist. On the pond banks it gets enough water to move fast.
