Showy Rattlebox

Crotalaria spectabilis

At a glance

  • Caterpillars of the ornate bella moth eat its unripe seeds and keep the poison. As adult moths, with bright pink hindwings, they are still poisonous, so predators leave them alone.
  • Even the eggs carry the poison, passed on by both parents. A male gives the female some of his when they mate, and she adds hers to the eggs.
  • People planted it on purpose, early in the 1900s, to trap nematodes, tiny worms that attack crop roots. It poisoned the livestock that ate it, and its seeds have turned up in harvested grain.
  • When the pods ripen and dry, the seeds come loose and rattle inside, which is where the name comes from.

Where it comes from

Southern Asia, and a farm experiment

It comes from southern and southeastern Asia. It was brought to the southeastern states early in the 1900s as a crop to plant for the soil and to cut down nematodes. The farmers who tried it learned that livestock eat it, and it poisons them. The earliest Florida specimen on record was pressed in 1928.

Where to find it here

Grows next to the office and simply dominates that spot.

What it does

A perfume made of poison

A male bella moth makes a scent from the plant's poison, and the more he carries, the more scent he gives off. In one experiment, males raised on seed-bearing plants made more scent, carried more poison and grew heavier. Females may choose by it.

Left alone, until hunger

Animals tend to avoid it when other forage is plentiful, and eat it when good forage runs short. That is how a pasture goes wrong. Florida's plant experts now advise against planting it.

How to know it

One plain leaf

The leaves are simple, a single smooth blade, widest toward the tip, smooth above and hairy beneath, two to six inches long. Its cousin the Smooth Rattlebox, which also grows in the park, has a leaf split into three leaflets.

Yellow spikes, rattling pods

The flowers are yellow pea flowers about an inch across, held in tall spikes in late summer and fall. The pods that follow are about two inches long, cylindrical and swollen, and they turn brown as they ripen.

How it grows

A summer annual

It is an annual that grows from seed each year, quickly, to about five feet. It likes full sun and poor, sandy ground, and as a bean-family plant it makes its own nitrogen. Frost kills it, and it comes back from the seeds that fell. It is common along roadsides and in fields and waste ground.

Take care

Do not eat it

Every part is poisonous, the seeds most. The poison damages the liver and there is no treatment. It has killed horses, cattle, chickens and dogs.
