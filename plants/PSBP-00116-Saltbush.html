Saltbush

Baccharis halimifolia
On this page

At a glance

  • A native shrub of Florida's coasts, common along roadsides and the edges of salt marsh.
  • Male and female flowers grow on separate plants. In fall, female plants cover themselves in fluffy white seed heads.
  • Its small fall flowers feed butterflies and bees late in the year.

Where it comes from

It grows along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of the eastern United States, all of Florida included, in marshes, mangrove edges, dunes and disturbed ground near the coast.

Where to find it here

In the Butterfly Garden.

What it does here

A late meal

It flowers in fall, when its small flower heads give butterflies and bees a late-season meal.

A garden brought back

It is part of the Butterfly Garden we cleared and replanted with natives in 2026. The garden is now a registered Monarch Waystation.

How to know it

Airy shrub

A loose, many-stemmed shrub with leathery, gray-green leaves, toothed toward the tip.

Flower or seed?

In fall, look closely at the white heads. Tight little flower heads come first; later, on female plants only, silky white fluff carries the seeds.

How it grows

Fluff on the wind

Each seed rides away on a tuft of silky hairs, so the wind does the planting.

It shrugs off salt spray and brackish ground, which is why it lines coastal marshes.
