Ouricury Palm

Syagrus coronata

At a glance

  • Ouricury Palm is native to northeastern Brazil and northern Minas Gerais.
  • The old leaf bases form a conspicuous spiral pattern around the trunk.
  • It belongs to the same genus as Queen Palm, but comes from much drier Brazilian vegetation and has a more strongly armored, architectural trunk.
  • Licuri fruits are a major food of Lear's Macaw, a parrot endemic to the same broad region of northeastern Brazil.

Cultural significance

Licuri is a food, not just a palm name

In northeastern Brazil, the fruit and kernel are eaten and used locally. The palm is part of rural food systems as well as the Caatinga landscape.

Where it comes from

Northeastern Brazil

Kew gives the native range as northeastern Brazil south to northern Minas Gerais and places the species primarily in seasonally dry tropical vegetation.

Where to find it here

The palm field south of Cypress Pond

In the row of palms next to the Plumeria planting, south of Cypress Pond — the same field that holds the Lala Palm. This is one of the more mature specimens in the row.

What it does here

Compare it with Queen Palm

Both are Syagrus, but their silhouettes differ. Queen Palm has a smoother exposed trunk and looser crown; Ouricury retains leaf bases in a strong spiral and reads as a much more rugged dry-country palm.

How to know it

Start with the spiral

Persistent old leaf bases form a repeating pattern around the upper trunk.

A compact, stiff crown

The pinnate leaves arch but appear more structured than the loose plume of Queen Palm.

Armed petioles

The leaf stalks carry sharp projections and deserve space.

Fruit in bunches

Oval fruits ripen yellow to orange-brown and contain the hard licuri nut.

How it grows

A dry-region Syagrus

Its native Caatinga and neighboring seasonally dry habitats help explain its tolerance of heat and periodic drought.

Slow by Florida standards

UF/IFAS lists this species as a slow-growing South Florida palm, commonly around 30 feet tall in landscapes.

Current conservation status

Kew carries an IUCN assessment of Least Concern for the palm itself, even though animals that depend heavily on its fruit can be much more threatened.
