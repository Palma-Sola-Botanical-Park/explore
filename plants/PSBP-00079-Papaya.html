Papaya

Carica papaya

At a glance

  • Every part of this plant is loaded with papain, an enzyme that digests protein. It is the active ingredient in meat tenderiser, and it turns up in contact lens cleaner and digestive supplements.
  • It is not a tree. The trunk is soft and pithy with no wood in it at all.
  • Most papayas are male or female, not both. A female plant with no male nearby will flower all it likes and never set fruit.
  • The seeds are edible and taste of black pepper. Some people use them as a substitute.

Cultural significance

The enzyme in the bottle

Papain is stripped from the latex of unripe fruit and sold by the ton. It breaks protein down, which is what makes it a meat tenderiser, and the same property gets it into wound dressings and digestive tablets. The plant is making it to defend the unripe fruit; we found other uses.

Where it comes from

Southern Mexico and Central America

That is the whole of the native range. It now grows in every tropical country on earth, and it spread fast, because it is quick, easy, and comes true enough from seed.

Where to find it here

Past the Black Sapote in the Rare Fruit area

Come into the Rare Fruit area from the restroom side and walk the main path. A little beyond the Black Sapote, on your left, is the papaya. The Pond Apple is behind that same sapote, so the two are worth doing in one stop.

What it does here

Quick, generous, and gone

Papaya lives fast. Seed to fruit in under a year, then a few productive years, then decline. Most trees in a garden are an investment measured in decades; this one is closer to a crop that happens to stand fifteen feet tall. The plant you see may well not be the one that stood here three years ago.

How to know it

Fruit straight against the trunk

There are no fruiting branches. The fruit sits in a tight cluster hard against the stem, just below the crown of leaves, which is why a loaded papaya looks like it is wearing a belt.

A trunk of rings

Every leaf that falls leaves a scar, so the trunk is ringed from the ground up — a record of every leaf the plant has ever grown.

Leaves on long hollow stalks

Big, deeply lobed leaves held well out from the trunk on stalks that can run two feet and are hollow down the middle.

How it grows

Faster than almost anything

Among the fastest-growing plants in a Florida garden. It does not thicken the way a tree does; it extends upward and carries its whole working crown at the top, which is why an old one eventually folds rather than falls.

Take care

The ripe fruit only

Ripe flesh is excellent and the seeds are fine. The milky latex in unripe fruit is the part to leave alone — papain at that strength irritates skin and sets off allergic reactions in some people. Keep the seeds away from dogs.
