Triangle cactus

Acanthocereus tetragonus
On this page

At a glance

  • Florida has only a few native cacti, and this is one of the rare ones: the state lists it as threatened.
  • Its stems have ridges: often three on a mature stem, and four or more on younger growth.
  • Its large white flowers open at night and usually close around dawn.
  • Stems that lie on the ground or lean into nearby plants take root where they touch the soil, starting new plants.

Where to find it here

Look in the Cacti and Succulents area, near Century Plant and Spanish Bayonet.

Where it comes from

Triangle cactus belongs to the coastal thickets and hammocks of the tropical Americas, including southern Florida. Think sandy or rocky ground among other plants, rather than a lone cactus in an open desert.

Old names

Florida plants were once split off as a separate species, Acanthocereus floridanus, so older Florida references may use that name.

What it does

The upright stems make it easy to see how much work a cactus stem does. The broad green surfaces capture light and store water; the spine clusters sit along their narrow ridges. A plant can keep growing without a canopy of ordinary leaves.

How to know it

Follow the ridges

Look along the length of a stem without reaching into it. Its sides meet at pronounced edges, lined with regularly spaced clusters of spines. Older branches may lean or arch rather than remaining neat columns.

The cactus’s own flowers are large and white. They open at night, so a daytime visit is more likely to reveal buds or spent flowers. Shiny red fruits may follow.

How it grows

Branches can scramble through supporting vegetation. Where a stem touches suitable ground it may root, extending the patch without starting from seed. Water-storing stems help it through dry weather, but drainage still matters: a wet, airless root zone is a different problem from a passing rain.

Fast, and sold as a houseplant

A stem can grow as much as six feet in a single season, and broken pieces take root, which is why it has been planted as a living fence. The dwarf Fairy Castle cactus sold as a houseplant is a cultivar of this same species.

Take care

Give the stems room. The spine clusters project from every rib, including low branches that are easy to miss when looking at the taller growth.
