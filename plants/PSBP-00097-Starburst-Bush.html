Starburst Bush

Clerodendrum quadriloculare
On this page

At a glance

  • Starburst Bush blooms in winter and early spring, when the days are short. In a university greenhouse trial it flowered under 8 or 12 hours of light a day, but never under 16.
  • Each starburst is a cluster of many flowers. Every pink tube, up to about three inches long, ends in five white tips that spread or curl back, and the tubes stay after the white tips drop.
  • The leaves are green above and deep purple below all year, so the shrub has color even when it is not in flower.

Cultural significance

Two names, two scales

Starburst describes the whole cluster, with flowers pointing outward like a firework. The botanical name, quadriloculare, means four-chambered and describes something much smaller: the flower's ovary, where the seeds start. Francisco Blanco first described the plant in 1837, and he filed it among the privets.

Where it comes from

From the Philippines and New Guinea

Starburst Bush comes from the Philippines and New Guinea, where it grows at forest edges and on disturbed ground. From there it spread to tropical gardens for its flowers and its purple-backed leaves.

What it does here

A long throat for long tongues

The nectar sits deep in the tube, so only long-tongued visitors can reach it. Gardeners see butterflies and hummingbirds working these flowers. Which animals pollinate it at home is not well known.

Winter blooms

Flowering comes in winter and early spring. Our photograph of it in flower was taken in early March.

How to know it

Leaves in pairs

Large, pointed leaves with strong veins sit opposite each other along the stem. Find one pair, then the next, to follow a branch through overlapping foliage.

A big shrub

Several woody stems make a large shrub that can pass for a small tree. Sources give 10 to 20 feet, which puts the flower heads above eye level.

How it grows

A wait

In the greenhouse trial it took about eight to nine months for a cutting to flower.

After the flowers

Prune after it flowers, so new growth has time to form before the next season. New plants come from stem cuttings.
