Coconut Palm

Cocos nucifera

At a glance

  • Look up. One tall trunk carries long, feather-shaped leaves, and the coconuts hang at the very top.
  • A coconut takes about a year to ripen. Different stages share the crown, so green and brown fruit hang together.
  • The coconut is a packed lunch. The liquid and the white flesh are food stored for the seedling.
  • People carried it across oceans. Long voyages by people helped spread coconuts through the tropics.

Cultural significance

Two useful layers

The white flesh is eaten fresh or dried. Dried flesh, called copra, supplies coconut oil. The fibrous husk becomes coir, used in rope and growing mixes.

Trade left a genetic trace

In parts of the western Indian Ocean, coconuts from an Indian Ocean lineage and a Pacific lineage have mixed. That fits an old trade route linking Southeast Asia, Madagascar and eastern Africa, and it suggests people carried coconuts along it, not only the sea.

Where it comes from

Genetic evidence points to separate histories of cultivation in island Southeast Asia and along the southern Indian subcontinent. People moving coconuts over long distances helped spread those lineages across the tropics.

Where to find it here

Three tall coconut palms grow in the Rare Fruit area, but you hardly notice them there. You have to look up, and the fruit all around you keeps your eyes busy. The best view is from the open field behind the Galleria, where all three tower along the opposite bank of the Big Pond.

What it does

A landmark across the pond

The three coconut palms give the Rare Fruit area a visual anchor from across the pond, their tall trunks standing right along the bank. We don't go up to fetch the coconuts, but they do drop from time to time.

How to know it

The whole fruit includes the husk

An intact coconut is much larger than the hard shell sold in a grocery store. The shell has three sunken marks called eyes at one end.

A feather crown, a variable trunk

Many narrow leaflets line each long leaf axis. Some varieties have leaning trunks and others grow straighter, so a lean by itself does not identify a coconut palm.

How it grows

Food absorbed from inside

The liquid inside the seed and the white flesh are forms of endosperm, stored food for the new plant. Part of the seed leaf remains inside as an absorbing organ, transferring that food to the growing seedling.

A beginning hidden by the husk

Germination begins inside the fibrous fruit wall, so growth can start before anything shows outside. The young root and shoot grow out beside the seed.

Warmth and light

Coconut palms need a warm climate and strong light. Freezing limits where they grow, even in Florida.

Take care

Avoid standing beneath a crown carrying fruit, because falling coconuts can injure people.
