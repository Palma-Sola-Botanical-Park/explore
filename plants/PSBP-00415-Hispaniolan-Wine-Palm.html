Hispaniolan Wine Palm

Pseudophoenix vinifera
On this page

At a glance

  • Look at the base. A grown trunk is strongly bottle-shaped, swelling low and narrowing above. Ours is young, but the swelling has started.
  • The leaves are feathers. Each one is about seven to ten feet long, and a grown crown holds roughly two dozen.
  • It can get tall. In the wild the trunk reaches about sixteen to fifty feet. Ours has a long way to go.
  • It made wine. People fermented the palm's sweet sap into wine, and its name points that way.

Cultural significance

A palm that made wine

The sap of this palm is sweet, and people fermented it into wine. Getting at it hurt the tree. Tapping the swollen part of the trunk damages the palm, and cutting the trunk down destroys it.

Where to find it here

At the Butterfly Garden entrance

Ours stands at the entrance to the Butterfly Garden, just before the Bottle Palm. The two trunks look a little alike, so stop and compare them.

Where it comes from

Hispaniola, the island that Haiti and the Dominican Republic share. The palm grows in dry forest there, from Haiti into the southwest of the Dominican Republic, roughly a thousand feet above sea level.

How to know it

A bottle on a short trunk

On a grown palm the trunk narrows above a clear swelling, and on a young one, like ours, you can see the swelling beginning. Look low on the trunk, where the base is fatter and paler.

Gray-green, feathered leaves

The long leaves arch out in a feathery crown. Their leaflets are gray-green on a grown palm and paler, with a waxy look, underneath. Ours is still a brighter green.

How it grows

The swelling comes first

The palm starts to reproduce only after the trunk has swollen. Until then it is a green crown on a pale, rounded base, which is how ours looks today.
