At a glance

  • If you plant these for butterflies, the variety matters. Flower form and nectar production vary a lot between cultivars, and the compact types bred mainly for tidy shape do not all perform like the taller old-fashioned ones. Worth knowing before you spend the money.
  • Most of the country grows pentas as a summer annual that dies with the first cold. Ours have simply never stopped, and bloom straight through the year.
  • Each bloom is a long narrow tube with the nectar at the bottom, and they are packed into dense heads - so a butterfly that lands can work its way through a great many flowers without going anywhere.
  • It is in the coffee family. Wild Coffee, Firebush and Jungle Flame elsewhere in the park are the other cousins.

Cultural significance

A Florida butterfly-garden standby

Pentas flowers for months through heat that shuts down a lot of garden plants, and butterflies use it heavily. That combination is why it turns up in so many Florida pollinator gardens, and why it ended up framing our front walk.

Where it comes from

Grassland and forest edge in East Africa

Native to East Africa and Yemen, in open grassland and along the margins of forest - bright, disturbed, sunny ground, which is exactly the situation it has been given here.

Sunbirds there, hummingbirds here

In Africa these flowers are worked by sunbirds, which fill much the same role hummingbirds do in the Americas. The plant crossed an ocean and found the arrangement waiting for it. Ruby-throated Hummingbirds moving through Tampa Bay in autumn stop at pentas readily, and go for the red ones first.

Where to find it here

Right outside the office

They are planted along the pavers at the office, framing the way into the shade garden. You will pass them without trying.

What it does here

Planted by students, and still theirs

These went in during 2025, put there by high school volunteers, and the same crew has been back to weed and water them ever since. They are not a display that was installed and forgotten. If they look good when you walk past, that is a group of teenagers keeping their word.

The busiest few square feet at the entrance

Because they flower without much of a break, there is nearly always something feeding on them - butterflies and bees through the warm months, hummingbirds in autumn.

Give it two minutes

Stand at the pavers on a warm morning and watch the flower heads. Do not count butterflies - count how many different kinds turn up before you move on. Then tell us what you got.

How to know it

Count to five

Each little flower is a tube that opens into a neat five-pointed star. That is where the name comes from - pentas, five - and it is the quickest way to be sure of the plant.

Domed clusters at the tips

The flowers gather into dense heads about three inches across at the end of each branch, in red, pink, lavender or white. From any distance the clusters are what you see, not the individual stars.

Soft, ribbed leaves

Lance-shaped and medium green, with fine hairs over both surfaces that give them a slightly soft feel, and strong veins standing out underneath. The leaf shape is where lanceolata comes from.

How it grows

Three or four feet, and quick about it

An upright evergreen that grows fast and stays under about four feet - a soft-stemmed perennial rather than a woody shrub, which is why it can be replaced or refreshed easily.

Built for Florida heat

It wants sun, good drainage and regular water while it settles in. After that it keeps growing and flowering long past the point where colder places would have lost it to frost. Stem cuttings root easily, so a planting like this one is simple to keep going.
