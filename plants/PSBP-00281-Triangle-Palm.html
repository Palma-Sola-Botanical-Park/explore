At a glance

  • The crown is arranged in three distinct ranks. Look around the trunk rather than at one frond and the triangle becomes obvious.
  • Triangle Palm is native only to southeastern Madagascar.
  • The pale blue-green leaves and rust-brown material around the newest leaf bases add to the palm's angular look.
  • This species now sits in Chrysalidocarpus, as C. decaryi. The older name Dypsis decaryi remains common in horticulture and is what the park's record keeps.

Where it comes from

Southeastern Madagascar

Triangle Palm is native to southeastern Madagascar, where it grows in seasonally dry tropical vegetation. Its natural range is small enough that current conservation assessments treat the species as Vulnerable.

Where to find it here

Big Pond walkway, toward the far end

On the south walkway along Big Pond, toward the far end from the Rare Fruit bridge, near where the Spindle Palm stands.

Use the crown shape

Even from several steps away, the three-ranked crown is a better clue than trying to identify the trunk.

What it does here

A geometry lesson among palms

Most feather palms arrange their leaves around the stem in a more circular crown. Triangle Palm makes the underlying leaf arrangement conspicuous enough for visitors to see how palm architecture can differ species by species.

How to know it

Walk around it

The leaves and persistent leaf bases are organized in three vertical ranks, producing a triangular upper trunk and crown.

Blue-green fronds

The stiff arching leaves are often noticeably pale or gray-green rather than glossy deep green.

Rust near the top

Brown felt-like material can remain around the bases of newer leaves.

One trunk

The plant is solitary rather than a basal clumper.

How it grows

A dry-country Madagascar palm

Its native ecology and Florida horticultural use both point toward sun and well-drained soil rather than a constantly wet root zone.

It needs room sideways

Mature landscape palms run roughly 25 to 35 feet with broad crowns, so a young plant can eventually occupy much more space than its trunk suggests.

Wild populations remain limited

The species is classified as Vulnerable.
