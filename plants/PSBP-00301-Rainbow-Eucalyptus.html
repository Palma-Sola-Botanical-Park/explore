At a glance

  • The bark peels away in strips. The fresh bark underneath is bright green, and it darkens through blues, purples and oranges as it ages.
  • Unlike most eucalyptus, it comes from rainforest, not dry Australian bush.
  • On Maui, a roadside grove of these trees is one of the famous stops on the Road to Hana.
  • Ours is young, planted at the Front Pond where a Baobab came down in a storm.

Cultural significance

Grown for paper

In the Philippines and elsewhere in the tropics it is planted for pulpwood. It grows fast and straight, and the wood goes into paper.

A roadside celebrity

Groves planted in Hawaii, especially along Maui's Road to Hana, draw a steady stream of people who pull over just to stare at the trunks.

Where it comes from

It grows wild in the rainforests of the Philippines, Indonesia and New Guinea, often along rivers. On Mindanao its range crosses north of the equator, which no other eucalyptus does.

What the names say

The species name deglupta means peeled. Its other common name, Mindanao gum, names its northernmost home.

Where to find it here

At the Front Pond, between the driveway and the water, a few steps in from the pond's edge.

What it does here

After the Baobab

It was planted where a Baobab fell in a storm. It is still a slender young tree, and its trunk is mostly green so far, with the first strips only starting to lift. Come back over the years and watch the colors build.

How to know it

Find an edge

Look for a place where a strip of old bark is lifting away from fresh bark beneath. Please leave the strips on the tree.

Straight up

A single straight trunk with a narrow crown of glossy, pointed leaves.

Tiny capsules

The fruit is a small woody capsule about the size of a peppercorn, packed with tiny seeds.

How it grows

Why a patchwork

The bark doesn't shed all at once. Strips come away at different times, and each newly exposed patch starts bright green, then darkens through blue and purple to orange and maroon before it peels in its turn. Neighboring patches are different ages, so the trunk is never one color.

A rainforest giant

In its home forests it grows fast and can top two hundred feet. It wants heat and rain, and frost is hard on it.
