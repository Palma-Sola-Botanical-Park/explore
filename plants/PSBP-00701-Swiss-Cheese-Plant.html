Swiss Cheese Plant

Monstera deliciosa
On this page

At a glance

  • The holes are made by the leaf itself. Small patches of a young leaf die while it is still growing, and they open wider as the leaf expands.
  • A young plant's leaves are small whole hearts. The slits and holes come only as the plant matures, so a small plant and an old vine can look like different species.
  • The fruit looks like a green ear of corn, covered in scales. It takes more than a year to ripen after the flower.
  • Ropey roots dangle from the stem into the air. When they reach the ground they take root, anchoring the vine even when its leaves are high in a tree.

Cultural significance

A fruit behind the foliage

Most people meet it as a houseplant and never see the fruit. The plant reached England in 1752, and the fruit came to the United States in 1874. A fruit needs twelve to fourteen months after its flower to ripen. Only the ripe pulp is eaten, and when it is ready it slips off the core by itself. The fruit tastes of pineapple and banana.

Where it comes from

From the forest floor upward

It grows wild in the forests of southern Mexico and Central America. There a young vine starts on the ground and climbs a tree trunk, gripping with its roots, forty feet or more into the canopy. A houseplant is that vine kept small.

Where to find it here

Up a palm

Coming from the Galleria, you can't miss it as you enter the open field near the Royal Palm area. Look at one of the palms ahead of you on the left: the Swiss Cheese Plant is climbing it, more than thirty feet up.

What it does here

A flower spike with a hood

The flower is a thick cream-colored spike wrapped in a large cream hood. Each tiny flower on the spike has both pollen-making and pollen-receiving parts. In a greenhouse study the spike warmed over about five days, hottest around dawn on the last one, when the pollen was released. Beetles pollinate its relatives; this one's wild pollinator is unconfirmed.

Few seeds

Fruit in Florida generally has no seeds, so new plants come from cuttings and suckers. A sucker takes two to four years to fruit, a cutting four to six.

How to know it

Holes and slits

Look for two kinds of cut: oval holes beside the midrib, and slits running in from the leaf edge.

Marks along the stem

Broad, ring-like scars on the thick stem mark where old leaves were attached.

How it grows

Filtered light, moist soil

It does best in bright filtered light and moist soil that drains well, and standing water hurts the roots. The stems grow heavy and want a trunk or post to climb.

Take care

Crystals in every part

Every part of the plant holds needle-like crystals that burn the mouth and throat if chewed, and unripe fruit has them too. Dogs and cats get the same burning, along with drooling and vomiting.
