Beach Sunflower

Helianthus debilis
On this page

At a glance

  • A Florida native built for open sand, full sun and salt spray.
  • It sprawls. The stems run several feet across the ground while the plant stays a foot or two tall.
  • The small yellow sunflowers can show up most of the year here.
  • Bees work the flowers, and birds and small mammals eat the seeds.

Where it comes from

The Southeast's own sunflower

Native to the southeastern United States, Florida included. Florida has several native forms adapted to different coasts, with dune plants on the Atlantic side and on the Gulf side. Which one is planted here is not on record.

Where to find it here

Around all three ponds

Along the banks of the Big Pond, Cypress Pond and Front Pond, as low green mats with yellow flowers dotted through them.

What it does here

Holding the banks

This is one of the mainstays of the pond-bank plantings. Under restoration grants from the Tampa Bay and Sarasota Bay estuary programs, the park has been planting the banks to hold them against storms, and Beach Sunflower does that job while flowering.

Cover for open, sunny ground

Its low spreading habit also suits exposed, sunny spots where a taller shrub would block the view of the water.

How to know it

Small yellow sunflowers

Two or three inches across, yellow rays around a dark brown center, on a plant nowhere near sunflower height.

Low and sprawling

Instead of one tall stalk it branches and runs along the ground, rooting where the stems touch.

Rough triangular leaves

Small, dark green, roughly triangular, and sandpapery to the touch.

How it grows

Sun and sand

It comes from dunes, so poor sandy ground in full sun is exactly what it wants.

Salt spray is no problem

The coastal forms shrug off salt spray, though not standing salt water.

It reseeds

Single plants can be short-lived, but seedlings keep a patch going, and it may wander beyond where it was planted.
