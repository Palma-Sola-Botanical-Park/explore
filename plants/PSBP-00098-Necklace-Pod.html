Necklace Pod

Sophora tomentosa

At a glance

  • The seed pods pinch in tight between every seed, so a finished pod hangs like a string of beads. That is the necklace.
  • Spikes of bright yellow pea flowers stand up above soft, silvery, felted leaves for most of the year.
  • It is a beach shrub by birth, from shell mounds and the backs of dunes, and salt spray does not trouble it.
  • The Cassius Blue, a butterfly you could cover with a thumbnail, lays its eggs on it and drinks from it. Two jobs, one plant.

Where it comes from

Florida’s own coast

Native to the Florida coast and the islands beyond it: shell mounds, the edges of coastal hammock, the strand behind the beach. Two look-alike forms are sold under the same name, one native and one from farther off, and telling them apart takes a botanist and a hand lens.

Where to find it here

In the Butterfly Garden, which is exactly where a Cassius Blue host plant belongs. Look for the soft grey-green shrub with yellow flower spikes standing up out of it.

What it does here

Butterfly nursery and nectar bar

This is one of the Cassius Blue’s host plants: eggs on the flower buds, caterpillars in the flowers. The adults drink there too, along with bees and the occasional hummingbird. Watch a flower spike for a minute in the warm months and something usually turns up.

How to know it

Beaded pods

Long, slim pods squeezed in between the seeds, green at first, brown and hard later. Nothing else in the park makes a pod like it.

Yellow pea flowers on upright spikes

Bright yellow, shaped like a sweet-pea flower, in tall clusters at the branch tips.

Silvery felted leaves

Compound leaves with oval leaflets covered in fine soft hairs, which is what tomentosa means. Rub one gently; it feels like flannel.

How it grows

A rounded shrub six to ten feet tall and as wide, open and a little untidy. Full sun, sandy soil, dry feet and salt wind suit it. It flowers over a long season in warm weather and carries pods for months. Frost cuts it back.

Take care

Every part is poisonous, the seeds most of all; they carry cytisine, a nicotine-like alkaloid. Admire the bead pods, do not open them, and keep them from children and dogs.
