Century Plant

At a glance

  • A Century Plant spends years building a huge rosette of succulent leaves, then flowers once at the end of that rosette's life.
  • The flowering stalk can tower about 20 feet above the plant, turning a low rosette into something taller than many trees.
  • After flowering, the original rosette dies. Offsets growing around its base can survive and continue the colony.
  • Every agave tends to get called a century plant, and our garden has several kinds. The Century Plant proper is this one: the broad blue-gray rosette with big teeth along the leaf edges.

Cultural significance

A plant people have used for thousands of years

Agaves have a long history of human use in Mexico and the American Southwest. Their tough leaf fibers have been made into cordage and other materials, and several species have provided food and fermented drinks.

Not the tequila agave

Agave americana is among the agaves traditionally used to make drinks such as pulque and some mezcals. Tequila, however, comes from a different species, the Blue Agave.

Where to find it here

Along the shell path

Our Century Plants grow in the Cacti and Succulents garden, on the side nearest the front of the park. The garden rises in tiers beside a crushed-shell path that runs north to south, with bamboo along the top. The Century Plants are on the lowest tier, right beside the path.

Where it comes from

From dry North America

Century Plant is native from the southwestern United States into Mexico, where it grows in dry country. People have carried it far beyond that range, and it is now cultivated and naturalized in warm regions around the world.

What it does

Not the one with the stalk

The agave on the tier above with the tall flowering stalk is not a Century Plant. It is a Variegated Caribbean Agave, a narrower plant with cream-white leaf edges, and the three of them growing together, young, flowering and dead, show the life cycle every agave shares. Our Century Plants are the broad blue-gray rosettes down by the path.

How to know it

A huge rosette

Long, thick blue-green leaves radiate from the center, forming a rosette that can spread wider than a person is tall.

Teeth and a spear point

Each leaf has teeth along its edges and ends in a hard, sharp spine. You don't need to touch one to confirm the identification.

Look up

When a mature rosette finally flowers, a thick stalk rises from its center and can reach about 20 feet, branching near the top into clusters of yellowish flowers.

Look around the base

Smaller rosettes often appear around an established plant. These offsets, often called pups, are clones of the parent, and they are how a Century Plant carries on after the flowering rosette dies.

How it grows

Years for one flowering

The rosette spends many years storing up what it needs to reproduce. Eventually it switches from making leaves to making one enormous flowering stalk. After it flowers and sets seed, that rosette withers and dies. Century plants don't literally wait a century: depending on the agave species, flowering generally comes after roughly 15 to 60 years.

The plant dies; the colony may not

When the flowering rosette dies, the plant itself may not be finished. Century Plant sends up offsets from underground growth, so younger rosettes, genetically the same plant, can carry on after the parent rosette is gone.

Open at night, closed by day

Century Plant uses a water-saving form of photosynthesis called CAM. Instead of opening the tiny pores in its leaves during the hottest part of the day, it takes in carbon dioxide mainly at night and stores it for use after daylight returns. That cuts water loss and helps explain how such massive, fleshy leaves can work in dry places.

Take care

Give the leaves room

The rigid spine at the tip of each leaf can puncture deeply, and the leaf edges are lined with sharp teeth. Keep clear of the rosettes rather than stepping between them.

Sap can irritate skin

Cut or damaged Century Plant tissue can cause significant skin irritation. Don't cut or handle the leaves.
