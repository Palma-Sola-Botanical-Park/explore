At a glance

  • The stems are conspicuously ringed and can look bamboo-like, especially on clustering plants.
  • The leaflets do not all lie in one flat plane. They point in several directions, giving the fronds a fuller, plumose texture.
  • This species can be solitary or clustering, so trunk number alone is not enough to identify it.
  • It's native to northern and western Madagascar, and now sits in Chrysalidocarpus, as C. madagascariensis — though Dypsis madagascariensis is what the park's record keeps.

Where it comes from

Northern and western Madagascar

Madagascar Bamboo Palm is native to northern and western Madagascar, where it grows in wet tropical vegetation. It's listed as Least Concern.

Where to find it here

Big Pond walkway, mid-row

On the south walkway along Big Pond, roughly the middle of the row, past the Solitaire Palm.

Expect variation

Do not reject the identification simply because one specimen has several stems and another has fewer. This species is naturally variable in growth form.

What it does here

A reminder that species are not templates

Several palms in this stretch of the walkway have clean ringed stems, but this one is useful because its form can vary from solitary to clustering. Visitors can compare the leaf arrangement instead of relying on trunk number alone.

How to know it

Ringed cane-like stems

Green to gray stems carry closely spaced leaf-scar rings.

A three-dimensional frond

The leaflets sit in several planes rather than forming one flat feather.

Variable trunk number

Plants may be solitary or form clumps, so use the fronds and stem texture together.

Look at the whole crown

The many angled leaflets give the plant a fuller, more plumose silhouette than palms with evenly flat leaflet rows.

How it grows

A wet-tropical Madagascar palm

Reliable moisture and warm conditions fit its native ecology, although drainage still matters in cultivation.

Variation is part of the species

Differences in clustering, stem thickness and crown form occur within the species, which is one reason older horticultural material can make it seem more complicated than it is.

Wild status is not currently dire

It carries a Least Concern conservation assessment.
