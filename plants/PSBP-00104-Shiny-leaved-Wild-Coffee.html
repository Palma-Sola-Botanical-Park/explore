Shiny-leaved Wild Coffee

Psychotria nervosa

At a glance

  • It is in the coffee family, and the red fruit looks like a coffee cherry, but it is not coffee. Early settlers roasted the seeds anyway. The drink was bitter and gave them headaches, and the experiment did not last.
  • The leaves are the giveaway: glossy, dark and quilted, with the veins pressed in so deeply the surface looks puckered.
  • At least twenty kinds of bird eat the berries, catbirds and mockingbirds among them.
  • It fruits in deep shade, which almost nothing else does, so it grows where other flowering shrubs give up.

Where it comes from

The hammock understory

Native to central and south Florida, the Caribbean and on down into Central America, always in the shade under live oaks and other hardwoods. It is one of the shrubs that make a Florida hammock feel like a room with the lights turned low.

Where to find it here

In the tangle between the gazebo and the Royal Palm field, in among the crotons, with the Wild Lime for a neighbour. Mind the Wild Lime’s thorns while you look. It is a bit wild back there, which is exactly how this plant likes it.

What it does here

A bird larder in the shade

Most fruiting shrubs want sun. This one sets its white flowers and then its berries under the canopy, and the birds that work the shady corners of the park come to it: catbirds in winter, mockingbirds and thrashers all year. The flowers feed small native bees and butterflies too.

How to know it

Quilted glossy leaves

Dark green, shiny, in opposite pairs, with every vein sunk into the surface so the leaf looks embossed. No other common shrub here has that texture.

Small white flowers, then red berries

Clusters of tiny white tubes at the branch tips in spring and summer, followed by oval berries that ripen red and then darken. They look like miniature coffee cherries, which is the whole reason for the name.

How it grows

Four to six feet tall in ordinary light, taller and lankier in deep shade, and evergreen unless a hard freeze catches it. It wants shade; in full sun the leaves go pale and scorch. Moderate water once settled. A very easy native for a dark corner.

Take care

The berries are for the birds. They will not hurt you, but the settlers’ experience is worth remembering.
