African Milk Weed

Euphorbia trigona

At a glance

  • Not a cactus, not a milkweed, not a tree, despite every one of its names. It is a spurge from central Africa that arrived at the cactus shape on its own, without being related to cactus at all.
  • The white sap is latex, and it is caustic. On skin it burns and blisters; in an eye it is an emergency. Every part that bleeds white is a part to leave alone.
  • The spines come in pairs along the ridges, straight from the stem. A true cactus grows its spines from little cushions. That is the whole difference, once you know to look.
  • It almost never flowers in a garden. In the wild it may wait decades.

Where to find it here

In the Shade Garden, a tall green column of three-sided stems standing by the little statue, taller than you are.

Where it comes from

Central Africa

Seasonally dry forest in Angola, Gabon, the Congo and Malawi, where it stores water in its stems through the long dry season. It went round the world as a houseplant and has run wild in Cuba and India, where fallen pieces of stem root where they land.

What it does

The impostor, again

A cactus impostor among the shade plants, and a chance to learn the trick. Find a spine: it sits on a small shield on the ridge, in a pair, with a little teardrop leaf beside it in the growing season. No cushion, no cactus. Lizards use the thicket of stems for cover. Nothing else here goes near it, which is sensible.

How to know it

Three-sided stems

Sharply triangular in cross-section, dark green with pale marbling down the ridges, rising close and parallel to the main stem like a candelabra.

Leaves that come and go

Small teardrop leaves along the ridge edges in the growing season, dropped later; the green stems do the work all year.

White sap

Any break weeps white. That is the spurge family’s signature and its warning.

How it grows

Fast in sun, slower in shade, up to nine or ten feet as a narrow column. Sharp drainage is everything; wet soil rots it. A piece of stem, dried a few days, roots easily, which is how it is grown and how it escapes. Do not compost the prunings.

Take care

Gloves and glasses for anyone cutting it. If the sap gets in an eye, rinse with water for a good fifteen minutes and get to a doctor. Poisonous to eat, for people and for pets.
