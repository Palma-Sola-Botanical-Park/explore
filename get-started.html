Why we're asking

You're helping build the record

Every nature photo reaches the park through iNaturalist — the beautiful ones too. A photo posted there is both a picture and a record that this species was here, on this date.

Naturalists around the world help confirm what you found — and the record it builds is what the park's work in education and preservation rests on.

Step by step

Make Your Observation

"We're not scientists — we're enthusiasts, volunteers, and community members who care about this place. iNaturalist gives us access to a global network of experts who can confirm or correct our identifications. If you think we've misidentified something, bring the debate to the project. That's exactly how it's supposed to work."

— Randy Carter, Park Volunteer

You know why it matters now — here's the whole thing, step by step. Once the app's on your phone, a single observation takes about thirty seconds.

  1. Start an observation

    A large green button — a + or a camera, depending on your platform and app version.

  2. Take your photos

    An observation is usually several photos of the same organism, big and small: the whole plant, close-ups of the flowers, the leaves, the bark — whatever helps someone identify it. Tap the green checkmark when you're done.

  3. Add an identification

    Most of us accept iNaturalist's suggestion. Override it when you know better — for instance, when one of our signs names the species.

  4. Upload — don't just Save

    Most of us tap Upload straight away. If GPS was weak, check the location landed inside the park first; it usually has. Save keeps the observation on your phone — if you use it, come back and upload when you have signal, or nobody ever sees your work.

Field craft

Tips for Great Observations

Tip from the park's iNaturalist lead

You are not submitting a photo. You are submitting an observation.

An observation is several looks at the same thing — from a distance, and up close. Do a few and you'll see that one photo is never enough. Each one becomes a small puzzle: what would an expert need to see to be sure?

None of this is required, but a little care makes identification far more likely.

  • Get every aspect that might help with the ID — the whole plant or animal, plus close-ups of the telling details: leaves, flowers, buds, fruit, bark, markings.
  • A finger or hand next to a leaf or berry gives a size reference.
  • Keep the plant as the main focus, with as little else crowding the shot as possible.
  • Then take one beautiful one. The shots above are for identification, and they are rarely the ones worth printing. Take one more purely because it looks good: the light, the angle, the whole plant standing where it stands. That is the photograph that ends up enlarged on a sign or in the park's own materials, which we love to do.
Level up

Now Be an Identifier

So far, so easy — anyone can point a phone and shoot. But if you want to really learn your craft and start down the road to becoming a naturalist, this is the good part. Identifying is where snapshots turn into real botany: you confirm what you're sure of, puzzle through what you're not, and your eye gets sharper every single time. That's the reward — real skill, earned one observation at a time.

It's easiest on a computer — a big screen and a real keyboard make it fly — but you can do it right from the app too. Here's how to go deeper:

  1. Join the Palma Sola project

    Go to our project page at inaturalist.org/projects/palma-sola-botanical-park. If you're signed in on the iNaturalist website, you'll see a Join button near the top — click it. Once you've joined, you can see every observation made in the park in one place, not just your own — including which ones still need an ID. That's exactly how we keep track of what's left to confirm.

  2. Help confirm identifications

    Open the Identify tool, look at the photos, and agree with — or gently question — the IDs people proposed. Each confirmation nudges an observation toward a settled identification, and you'll pick up a startling amount of botany doing it.

  3. Read the project journal

    The project's journal and blog is where the deeper conversation lives: what's newly in bloom, which IDs are contested, what we're trying to document next.

This is the scientific level — and honestly, the most rewarding part. There's a real satisfaction in settling an ID and watching it become part of the park's permanent record. Come be a detective with us — we'd love to have you.

Every photo you take and every ID you help settle is what generates the record of our living collection — the plants and wildlife of Palma Sola, a catalog that keeps growing thanks to people like you. See the collection you're helping build →
