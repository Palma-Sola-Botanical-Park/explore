Palma Sola Botanical Park

The Rare Fruit Trail

Fruit trees from the tropics, growing in a corner of Manatee County that rarely sees a frost.

Jackfruit, black sapote, dragon fruit, longan: the Rare Fruit Trail is a grove of tropical and subtropical fruit, planted and cared for by the Manatee Rare Fruit Council since 2008.

We sit close to Tampa Bay, where a hard freeze is rare. That bit of warmth lets trees grow here that would struggle a few miles inland.

How the grove was made

For years there was talk of a fruit exhibit, but no group took it on. Then two things lined up: we found the money to clear an overgrown corner at the edge of the park, and the Council decided to put the surplus from its annual tree sale into planting it.

Most of what stood there was diseased palms. They came out to give the ground full sun, and the fill was graded nearly level so it drains gently toward the pond and the ditches. Manatee County put in underground irrigation, fed with reclaimed water. Council volunteers spread more than a hundred truckloads of wood chips to hold back the weeds and keep the soil moist, and the Bradenton Kiwanis Club helped pay for and lay the shell walk.

Some trees were donated and others the Council bought. A marker beside each one gives its common and botanical names, and the name of the donor.

The Manatee Rare Fruit Council

Who looks after the grove

The Council is a volunteer club that grows, shares and teaches about tropical and subtropical fruit. Members meet once a month, usually with a speaker, and visitors are welcome. Their January meeting is held here at the park.

Their annual fruit tree sale is how the grove got started. Dates for the sale and the meetings are on their website.
