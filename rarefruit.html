The map

Gold dots are rare fruit; green are everything else growing among them. Circles show how precisely the observations were placed. Click any dot for the plant, its photograph, and a link to the observation.

What is here

Every rare-fruit species (flagged in the catalog, or on the Council's list) photographed in the quadrant, with how many observations stand behind it (and the year, if nobody has photographed it this year). Gold means it has a pin in the grove in placements.json; the rest are known from photographs but have no coordinate recorded here.

    Go find these

    On the Manatee Rare Fruit Council's list for this grove, and no record anywhere in the park's catalog. Some may have died; some are certainly still standing and simply unphotographed. This is the list to hand somebody.

    Known, but not photographed here

    The park has a record for these, but nothing in this quadrant. Either they grow elsewhere, or nobody has photographed the grove's plant.

    Data taken from iNaturalist observations pinned inside the park boundary (the grove is a box south of latitude 27.5140 and east of longitude −82.6595, which takes in the Butterfly Garden and the bridge end of the Big Pond walkway), the park's own catalog and placements, and the Manatee Rare Fruit Council's Palma Sola plant list (mrfc.org). A snapshot: it does not update itself. Positions are the median of each species' best-placed observations, so a dot marks roughly where that fruit is, not a specific tree — several species have more than one plant here.

    The MRFC list is a hunt list, not an inventory. Randy: “I know I've found things in their area not listed, and vice versa.” Absence from this map means nobody has photographed it, which is not the same as absence from the park.
