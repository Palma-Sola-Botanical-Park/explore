Support the Park

The park is free because people support it

There is no gate and no ticket, any day you like. Keeping it that way means picking up the trash and pulling the weeds, planting new things and watering them through a Florida summer. It means building the self-guided tours, making and maintaining the QR signs on the trail, and documenting every plant and animal here so that record is free to anyone who wants it.

All of it is paid for by the people who use the park. As a 501(c)(3) nonprofit receiving no federal, state, or local funding, we rely on donations and memberships to do any of it.

Gardening & Nursery

Planting, propagating, weeding, trimming, and yard cleanup. The hands-on core of what keeps the gardens the way they are. No experience needed.

Events & Community

Plant sales, holiday events, fundraisers, art exhibits, cornhole tournaments, and family fun nights. If you like people more than plants, this is your lane.

Education & Tours

Help develop self-guided tours, assist with classes and workshops, and support field trips and family programs.

Maintenance & Office

Light carpentry, painting, pressure washing, repairs, filing, data entry, graphic design, and thank-you cards. The work that keeps everything running.

Special Skills & Outreach

Photography, social media, writing, baking for events, and representing the park at the Farmer's Market or the County Fair. If you have a skill we haven't listed, we probably need it.

Bright Futures Program

High school scholars

High school students earn Florida Bright Futures scholarship hours through weekly sessions at Palma Sola. They plant, maintain, observe, and learn.

Students have planted the native Scorpion Tail along pond shores, maintained the Snake Plant patch near the front gate, learned to distinguish Canna from Heliconia in the office bed, and submitted their own iNaturalist observations.

The program also runs at the Patricia & Dr. Carroll Geraldson Community Garden, a UF/IFAS Extension project in Palmetto.

Ask About the Program →

Eligibility

Any high school student pursuing Florida Bright Futures scholarship hours. No prior plant knowledge required.

Skills Learned

Plant identification, iNaturalist observation, ecological relationships, garden maintenance, and how to read a living landscape.

Citizen Science

You don't have to be a scientist

Anyone can add to the park's scientific record. There are two ways in — one in the gardens, and one you can do from anywhere in the world.

Sign up and we'll be in touch →

We'll reach out and talk it through with you first — signing up doesn't commit you to anything.

In the park · anyone with a phone

Observe

Photograph the plants, insects, birds, and fungi you find and add them to iNaturalist. The community helps confirm what each one is, so you don't need to know before you post. Do it on any walk, or with the Wednesday crew.

How observing works →
At the keyboard · anywhere in the world

Identify & Curate

Confirm what others have found, note whether a plant is flowering or fruiting, and help fill the gaps in the park's catalog. You learn a species at a time, alongside people who enjoy it as much as you do. You don't have to be in Florida, or in the United States.
