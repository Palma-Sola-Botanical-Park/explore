The Butterfly Garden

Flowers for the butterflies, leaves for their caterpillars, and a certified Monarch Waystation.

Nectar and host plants chosen to pull butterflies through the season, and to feed the caterpillars that become them. Come on a warm, still morning.

Fall is a great time to visit the Butterfly Garden. Summer is a double whammy here: the rains make everything in a butterfly garden grow faster than anyone can keep up with, and the grueling heat keeps a lot of volunteers away. Now the heat has broken, the rains are easing (we hope), and our snowbird volunteers are back and putting the garden in shape. You’ll see the difference.

How it works

A butterfly garden has to feed two animals: the butterfly and the caterpillar it was.

Host plants

Most caterpillars eat the leaves of only a few kinds of plant, so a butterfly lays her eggs only where her young can eat. No host plant, no butterflies.

A certified Monarch Waystation

A Monarch Waystation is a garden with the milkweed monarch caterpillars eat and the nectar flowers the adults need on their long migrations. Ours is certified.
