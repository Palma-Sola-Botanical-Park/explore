Palma Sola Botanical Park

The Rare Palm Collection

Palms from islands and mountainsides around the world, a few of them scarcer in the wild than they are here.

More than ninety kinds of palm grow in this park, and we’re still finding and planting more. Some are Florida’s own. Others come from Madagascar, New Caledonia, Fiji, Vanuatu, Cuba, and tiny islands east of Madagascar. Together, they bring a remarkable slice of the palm world to these ten acres.

More about the collection

Some are now rare in their native habitat. In 2019, only nineteen Spindle Palms were known in the wild. The Vanuatu Palm was known only from old fruit specimens until living trees were found in 1987. Both grow beside our Big Pond, a short walk apart.

Thanks to generations of conservationists, growers, and palm enthusiasts, we have the chance to grow these palms here. Their stories reach far beyond the park: island forests, unlikely discoveries, and people determined to keep a species growing.

You’ll find palms throughout the grounds, each area with its own feel: a shady corner, a palm-lined walk, an open field you cross on the grass. Some are recent plantings, still years from looking like the mature palms pictured in books. Others may be so familiar that you hardly notice them—including a few of our most interesting palms outside the fence, along the road.

Our new signs and this guide offer a reason to take another look. There’s no need to see everything in one visit. Spend time in one area, notice what catches your eye, and come back for another. Use the map to get your bearings, then wander.

Three to meet first

If you see only three palms today, make it these. All three are on Palm Alley, a short walk apart.

Palm Alley

Palm Alley is the walk along the south end of the Big Pond, between the Rare Fruit bridge and the elms at the park's southern tip. Palms set five to ten feet apart frame it the whole way.

Some of the rarest palms in the park are on this one stretch: the Vanuatu Palm, the Pemba Palm from two patches of forest on one island off Tanzania, and the Teddy Bear Palm with its rust-red fuzz, Critically Endangered in Madagascar.

Shade Palms

Beyond the Royals, in the north-east corner near the front fence, is where we grow the palms that want shade. There is no set path. Wander in under the canopy and look around.

Three king palms stand side by side here: the Purple King, the Myola King and the Peach River King. With the Piccabeen nearby, that is four of the six species in their genus in one corner. Several are young, so come back in a few years.

Florida's Own

Our Florida natives are gathered on this page, though they grow in several parts of the park. Even the Royals by the Pavilion are Florida natives, from the hammocks of the far south.

At the southern tip, three elms shade a bench, a good place to rest. Along the south fence is a row of Florida's native palms. The Florida Thatch Palm puts on about six inches a year, so a trunk of any size on one is a long stretch of time.

Sun Palms

Turn right at the elms and you are in the open field of unusual palms, which runs north to the Plumeria beside the Cypress Pond. It is the biggest group in the collection and the most varied. There's no path through it: walk the grass.

Look for the Buccaneer Palm, with only about two hundred left wild in Florida, and the Lifou Palm, from one patch of forest on one small island.

Big Pond

The banks of the Big Pond carry palms of their own, all the way to the Carnaúba at the north end, where the walk meets the open field behind the Galleria.

Coming down the Galleria steps, the palm on the pond bank to your left, green above and silver below, is the Hispaniolan Silver Palm.

Outside the Fence

Some of our most interesting palms are planted outside the fence, on the strip by the road, and they get enjoyed far less than they deserve. You are welcome to walk out and see the whole line.

Butterfly Garden

Left of the Pavilion, a handful of palms grow among the flowers. Find the Bottle Palm: its trunk swells at the base. Its cousin the Spindle Palm, down by the Big Pond, swells in the middle instead.

Pavilion Field

Go through the trellis off the driveway and the Royal Palms are straight ahead: two rough rows, with strays around them. They are the oldest palms in the collection, and they got here by accident.

This was a county nursery, and these were grown for county streets. When the county stopped growing its own trees, they were left where they stood and rooted through their pots. Look at the spacing: it is a nursery's, not a garden designer's.

All the Rest

Palms turn up everywhere else too: on the islands in the drive, around the Front Pond, the office and the nursery, even in the Rare Fruit area. Each card says where.
